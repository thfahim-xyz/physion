# Foundations of Statistical Mechanics

## Postulates of Statistical Mechanics

In one formalism, the postulate is described as follows: For an isolated system with an exactly known energy and exactly known composition, the system can be found, with equal probability, in any microstate consistent with that knowledge.

In other formalisms, statistical mechanics follows the following postulates:

1. The probability density function is proportional to some function of the ensemble parameters and random variables.
2. Thermodynamic state functions are described by ensemble averages of random variables.
3. The entropy, as defined by Gibbs' entropy formula, matches with entropy defined in thermodynamics.

## Statistical Ensemble

An ensemble is an idealization consisting of a large number of virtual copies of a system, considered all at once, each of which represents a possible state that the real system might be in. In other words, an ensemble is the probability space of a system.

### Microcanonical Ensemble

Microcanonical ensemble (AKA $NVE$ Ensemble) is the ensemble that represents the possible states of a system whose total energy is specified.

**Ensemble Properties:**

| Characteristic      | Variable         |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Constant variables  | $N, V, E$        |
| Microscopic feature | Multiplicity $W$ |
| Macroscopic feature | Entropy $S$      |

- It is appropriate for describing isolated systems.

Probability of the system occupying the $i$-th microstate is

$$
    p_i = \frac{1}{W}
$$

**Ensemble Description:**

There are atleast 3 different definitions of thermodynamic quantities in this ensemble:

- Entropy (Boltzmann entropy, 'volume' entropy, 'surface' entropy)
- Temperature ('Volume' temperature, 'surface' temperature)

In this ensemble, temperature is a derived quantity rather than an external control parameter. The correspondence of the temperatures and entropies defined in this ensemble with the thermodynamic temperature and entropy is not perfect.

### Canonical Ensemble

Canonical ensemble (AKA $NVT$ Ensemble) is the ensemble that represents the possible states of a system in thermal equilibrium with a thermal reservoir at a constant temperature.

**Ensemble Properties:**

| Characteristic      | Variable                         |
|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| Constant variables  | $N, V, T$                        |
| Microscopic feature | Canonical partition function $Z$ |
| Macroscopic feature | Helmholtz free energy $F$        |

- It is appropriate for describing closed systems.
- The ensemble is uniquely determined for a given system at a given temperature.
- The ensemble is in statistical equilibrium.
- Systems described by this ensemble will be in thermodynamic equilibrium with other systems.
- The system has the maximum possible entropy of Stany ensemble with the same average energy.
- Helmholtz free energy is the minimum possible of any ensemble.

Probability of the system occupying the $i$-th microstate is

$$
    p_i = \exp \left( \frac{F - E_i}{k_B T} \right) = \frac{1}{Z} \exp \left( - \frac{E_i}{k_B T} \right)
$$

**Ensemble Description:**

Average pressure:

$$
    \langle P \rangle = - \frac{\partial F}{\partial V}
$$

Average energy:

$$
    \langle E \rangle = F + TS
$$

Gibbs entropy:

$$
    S_G = - k_B \langle \ln p \rangle = - \frac{\partial F}{\partial T}
$$

1st Law of Thermodynamics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \mathrm d F = - S \, \mathrm d T - \langle P \rangle \, \mathrm d V \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d \langle E \rangle = T \, \mathrm d S - \langle P \rangle \, \mathrm d V
\end{aligned}
$$

### Grand Canonical Ensemble

Grand canonical ensemble (AKA $\mu V T$ ensemble, macrocanonical ensemble) is the ensemble that represents the possible states of a system in thermal and chemical equilibrium with a thermal reservoir at a constant temperature.

**Ensemble Properties:**

| Characteristic      | Variable                                         |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Constant variables  | $N, V, T$                                        |
| Microscopic feature | Grand canonical partition function $\mathcal{Z}$ |
| Macroscopic feature | Helmholtz free energy $\Omega$                   |

- It is appropriate for describing open systems.
- The ensemble is uniquely determined for given system at a given temperature.
- The ensemble is in statistical equilibrium.
- Systems described by ensemble will be in thermodynamic and chemical equilibrium with other systems.
- The system has the maximum possible entropy for any ensemble with the same average energy, and average particle number.
- Grand potential is the minimum possible any ensemble.

Probability of the system occupying the $i$-th microstate is

$$
    p_i = \exp \left( \frac{\Omega + \mu N_i - E_i}{k_B T} \right) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}} \exp \left( \frac{\mu N_i - E_i}{k_B T} \right)
$$

**Ensemble Description:**

Average particle number:

$$
    \langle N \rangle = - \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial \mu}
$$

Average pressure:

$$
    \langle P \rangle = - \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial V}
$$

Average energy:

$$
    \langle E \rangle = \Omega + TS + \mu \langle N \rangle
$$

Gibbs entropy:

$$
    S_G = - k_B \langle \ln p \rangle = - \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial T}
$$

1st Law of Thermodynamics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \mathrm d \Omega = - S \, \mathrm d T - \langle P \rangle \, \mathrm d V - \langle N \rangle \, \mathrm d \mu \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d \langle E \rangle = T \, \mathrm d S - \langle P \rangle \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d \langle N \rangle
\end{aligned}
$$

## Statistical State

### Microstate

A microstate is a specific configuration of a system that describes the precise positions and momenta of all the individual particles that make up the system.

### Macrostate

A macrostate is a thermodynamic state of a system characterized by a probability distribution of possible states across a certain statistical ensemble of all microstates. The distribution describes the probability of finding the system in a certain microstate.

**Multiplicity:** Multiplicity $W$ or $\Omega$ is the number of real microstates corresponding to the macrostate of a system.

## Statistical Equilibrium

An ensemble that does not change with time is said to be in statistical equilibrium.

An ensemble that contains all the past and future phases of a system will always be in statistical equilibrium.
