## Fundamental Postulates of Statistical Mechanics

In one formalism, the postulate is described as follows: For an isolated system with an exactly known energy and exactly known composition, the system can be found, with equal probability, in any microstate consistent with that knowledge.

In other formalisms, statistical mechanics follows the following postulates:

1. The probability density function is proportional to some function of the ensemble parameters and random variables.
2. Thermodynamic state functions are described by ensemble averages of random variables.
3. The entropy, as defined by Gibbs' entropy formula, matches with entropy defined in thermodynamics.

## Statistical Ensemble

## Statistical State

### Microstate

A microstate is a specific configuration of a system that describes the precise positions and momenta of all the individual particles that make up the system.

### Macrostate

A macrostate is a thermodynamic state of a system characterized by a probability distribution of possible states across a certain statistical ensemble of all microstates. The distribution describes the probability of finding the system in a certain microstate.

**Multiplicity:** Multiplicity $W$ or $\Omega$ is the number of real microstates corresponding to the macrostate of a system.

## Statistical Equilibrium

An ensemble that does not change with time is said to be in statistical equilibrium.

An ensemble that contains all the past and future phases of a system will always be in statistical equilibrium.

## Entropy in Statistical Mechanics

In statistical mechanics, entropy is a measure of the number of ways a system can be arranged.

### Boltzmann's Entropy Formula

Boltzmann's entropy formula is the entropy equation for a system where each microstate is equally probable.

$$
    S_B = k_B \ln W
$$

where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $W$ is the multiplicity (number of microstates)

Multiplicity  $W$ is given by

$$
    W = \frac{N!}{\prod_i N_i !}
$$

where $N$ is the particle number and $N_i$ is the particle number in the $i$-th microstate of the system.

### Gibbs' Entropy Formula

Gibbs' entropy formula is the generalization of Boltzmann's entropy formula for systems where the microstates are not equally probable.

$$
    S_G = -k_B \sum_i p_i \ln p_i
$$

where $p_i$ is the probability of the $i$-th microstate having energy $E_i$.

## Degree of Freedom

A degree of freedom is a parameter of a system that may vary independently.

In statistical mechanics, the number of degrees of freedom describes the microstate of a system. A particle can have several degrees of freedom based on its molecular structure.

**Types:**

- **Translational Degrees of Freedom:** Translation along $x$, $y$, $z$ axes.
- **Rotational Degrees of Freedom:** Rotation about $x$, $y$, $z$ axes.
- **Vibrational Degrees of Freedom:** Only activated at high temperatures, so the number of vibrational degrees of freedom is temperature dependent.

### Degrees of Freedom of a Molecule

At normal temperatures:

| Molecule Type       | Translational DOF | Rotational DOF | Total DOF $(N_f)$ |
|---------------------|-------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Monatomic Molecule  | $3$               | $0$            | $3$               |
| Linear Molecule     | $3$               | $2$            | $5$               |
| Non-linear Molecule | $3$               | $3$            | $6$               |

At high temperatures:

| Molecule Type       | Translational DOF | Rotational DOF | Vibrational DOF  | Total DOF $(N_f)$ |
|---------------------|-------------------|----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| Monatomic Molecule  | $3$               | $0$            | $0$              | $3$               |
| Linear Molecule     | $3$               | $2$            | Atleast $3N - 5$ | Atleast $3N$      |
| Non-linear Molecule | $3$               | $3$            | Atleast $3N - 6$ | Atleast $3N$      |

## Equipartition Theorem

Energy is shared equally among all of its various forms for a system in thermodynamic equilibrium.

$$
    E = K = U \implies K = U = \frac{E}{2}
$$

## Characteristic Energy of a Degree of Freedom

This is the energy associated with each degree of freedom.

$$
    E_f = k_B T
$$

$$
    \implies K_f = U_f = \frac{1}{2} k_B T
$$
