# Statistical Thermodynamics

## Entropy in Statistical Mechanics

In statistical mechanics, entropy is a measure of the number of ways a system can be arranged.

### Boltzmann's Entropy Formula

Boltzmann's entropy formula is the entropy equation for a system where each microstate is equally probable.

$$
    S_B = k_B \ln W
$$

where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $W$ is the multiplicity (number of microstates).

Multiplicity  $W$ is given by

$$
    W = \frac{N!}{\prod_i N_i !}
$$

where $N$ is the particle number and $N_i$ is the particle number in the $i$-th microstate of the system.

### Gibbs' Entropy Formula

Gibbs' entropy formula is the generalization of Boltzmann's entropy formula for systems where the microstates are not equally probable.

$$
    S_G = -k_B \sum_i p_i \ln p_i
$$

where $p_i$ is the probability of the $i$-th microstate having energy $E_i$.

## Grand Potential

Grand potential (AKA Landau Potential) is the thermodynamic potential for the grand canonical ensemble. It is appropriate for describing irreversible processes in open systems.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \Omega \equiv \Omega (T, V, \mu) = F - \mu N = U - TS - \mu N \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d \Omega = \mathrm d F - N \, \mathrm d \mu = - S \, \mathrm d T - P \, \mathrm d V - N \, \mathrm d \mu
\end{aligned}
$$

## Partition Function

Partition functions are functions of thermodynamic state variables that describe the state properties of a system in thermodynamic equilibrium.

### Microcanonical Partition Function

Multiplicity $W$ can be considered as the partition function for the microcanonical ensemble.

### Canonical Partition Function

$$
    Z \equiv Z (N, V, T) = \sum_i \exp \left( - \frac{E_i}{k_B T} \right)
$$

where $E_i$ is the total energy of the system in the $i$-th microstate.

### Grand Canonical Partition Function

$$
    \mathcal{Z} \equiv \mathcal{Z} (\mu, V, T) = \sum_i \exp \left( \frac{\mu N_i - E_i}{k_B T} \right)
$$

where $E_i$ is the total energy of the system and $N_i$ is the particle number in the $i$-th microstate.

## Equipartition Theorem

Energy is shared equally among all of its various forms for a system in thermodynamic equilibrium.

$$
    E = K = U \implies K = U = \frac{E}{2}
$$

### Characteristic Energy of a Degree of Freedom

This is the energy associated with each degree of freedom.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & E_f = k_B T \\[0.4em]
    \implies & K_f = U_f = \frac{1}{2} k_B T
\end{aligned}
$$
