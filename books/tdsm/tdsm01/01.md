# Fundamental Concepts of Thermodynamics

## Thermodynamic System

A thermodynamic system is a body and/or radiation separate from its surroundings that can be studeied using the laws of thermodynamics. A thermodynamic system can be isolated, closed or open.

**Isolated System:** No exchange of matter or energy with its surroundings. A **mechanically isolated system** and **thermally isolated system** only allows the exchange of energy as heat and work, respectively.

**Closed System:** Exchanges energy with its surroundings, but not matter.

**Open System:** Exchanges both energy and matter with its surroundings.

## Thermodynamic State

A thermodynamic state is the condition of a thermodynamic system at a specific time.

### State Variables and Functions

State variables are the measurable properties that define the condition of a system. They are the independent coordinates that define a state. Common state variables in a thermodynamic system include pressure, volume, temperature, entropy, particle number, mass, mass density, etc.

State functions are properties whose values depend solely on the current state of the system. They are functions which depend on the state variables.

State variables can sometimes act like state functions and vice versa.

**Conjugate Variables:** Conjugate variables are variable pairs that multiply together to produce energy-like quantities.

- Thermal: Temperature and entropy.
- Mechanical: Pressure and volume.
- Chemical: Chemical potential and particle number.

## Thermodynamic Equilibrium

Thermodynamic equilibrium is the state of a system in which the macroscopic properties are uniform and do not spontaneously change with time.

A system is in thermodynamic equilibrium only if it simultaneously satisfies the following conditions:

- **Thermal Equilibrium:** The temperature should be uniform throughout the system and there should be no net heat flow within the system or with its surroundings (if they are in thermal contact).

- **Mechanical Equilibrium:** There should be no unbalanced forces, pressure gradients, bulk motion, expansion, compression, or acceleration.

- **Chemical Equilibrium:** There should be no net chemical reactions and no net diffusion of species due to concentration differences.

- **Phase Equilibrium:** If mutliple phases exist, then different phases should coexist without net phase change.

## Thermodynamic Process

A thermodynamic process is the transition of a system from one equilibrium state to another.

A thermodynamic process can occur in several ways:

- **Quasistatic (Quasi-equilibrium):** A quasistatic process proceeds infinitely slowly, so the system remains arbitrarily close to thermodynamic equilibrium at every instant. A quasistatic process is an idealization; no real process is perfectly quasistatic.

- **Reversible:** A reversible process is an ideal process that can be reversed by an infinitesimal change in conditions, restoring both the system and the surroundings to their initial states with no net changes. A reversible process **produces the maximum possible work** (or requires the minimum work for compression). A quasistaic process is reversible if there is no dissipative effects. In reality, however, almost all processes are irreversible and irreversible processes generate entropy.

- **Cyclic:** A cyclic process is one in which the system returns to its initial state after a sequence of processes. Cyclic processes may be reversible or irreversible.

### Constrained Processes

Constrained processes are thermodynamic processes where at least one state variable is held constant.

- **Isothermal Process:** Constant temperature $(T = \text{constant})$.

- **Isobaric Process:** Constant pressure $(P = \text{constant})$.

- **Isochoric Process:** Constant volume $(V = \text{constant})$.

- **Isentropic Process:** Constant entropy $(S = \text{constant})$.

- **Isoenergetic Process:** Constant internal energy $(U = \text{constant})$.

- **Isenthalpic Process:** Constant enthalpy $(H = \text{constant})$.

### Adiabatic Process

An adiabatic process is a process where there is no heat flow $(Q = 0)$.
