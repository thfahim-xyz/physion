# Heat Engines and Heat Pumps

## Heat Engine

A heat engine is a thermodynamic device that converts part of the heat supplied by a high-temperature reservoir into useful work, and the remaining heat is rejected to a low-temperature reservoir.

### Working Principle

A heat engine operates between two reservoirs at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$, with $T_H > T_C$.

It operates cyclically, so its working substance returns to its initial thermodynamic state after each cycle. Therefore, over one complete cycle, $\Delta U = 0$. Thus, from the first law of thermodynamics, the net work produced by the engine is

$$
    \boxed{W_{\text{net}} = Q_H - Q_C}
$$

where $Q_H$ is the heat absorbed from the hot reservoir, $Q_C$ is the heat rejected to the cold reservoir.

During each cycle:

1. The working substance absorbs heat $Q_H$ from the hot reservoir.
2. It performs net work $W_{\text{net}}$ on its surroundings.
3. It rejects heat $Q_C$ to the cold reservoir.
4. It returns to its initial state.

### Thermal Efficiency

The thermal efficiency of a heat engine is the fraction of the heat absorbed from the hot reservoir that is converted into useful work:

$$
    \eta = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_H} \implies \eta = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}
$$

Since a cyclic engine must reject some heat to a colder reservoir, $Q_C > 0$. Therefore, $\boxed{\eta < 1}$. Thus, no heat engine operating in a cycle can convert all the supplied heat into work.

### Reversible Heat Engine

For a reversible engine operating between reservoirs at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$, the entropy balance over a complete cycle gives

$$
    \Delta S = 0 \implies \frac{Q_H}{T_H} = \frac{Q_C}{T_C} \implies \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = \frac{T_C}{T_H}
$$

Therefore, the efficiency becomes

$$
    \boxed{\eta_{\mathrm{rev}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}}
$$

A **Carnot engine** is a reversible heat engine and therefore has the maximum possible efficiency for given reservoir temperatures.

### Limitation

The second law of thermodynamics imposes the restriction that a cyclic heat engine cannot have $Q_C=0$. Equivalently, no cyclic device can absorb heat from a single reservoir and convert that heat completely into work.

The efficiency of a real heat engine is therefore always less than the corresponding reversible efficiency:

$$
    \boxed{\eta_{\text{real}} < \eta_{\text{rev}}}
$$

Heat engines therefore illustrate an important consequence of the second law: **heat naturally flows from higher temperature to lower temperature, and only part of the transferred energy can be converted into work.**

## Heat Pump

A heat pump is a thermodynamic device that transfers heat from a low-temperature reservoir to a high-temperature reservoir by supplying external work.

### Working Principle

A heat pump operates cyclically between a cold reservoir at temperature $T_C$ and a hot reservoir at temperature $T_H$, where $T_H>T_C$. Unlike a heat engine, which produces work by allowing heat to flow from hot to cold, a heat pump uses work to force heat to flow from cold to hot.

During each cycle:

1. The working substance absorbs heat $Q_C$ from the cold reservoir.
2. External work $W$ is supplied to the system.
3. The working substance releases heat $Q_H$ to the hot reservoir.
4. The working substance returns to its initial state.

Since the working substance undergoes a complete cycle, $\Delta U = 0$. Thus, from the first law of thermodynamics, the input work for the pump is

$$
    \boxed{W = Q_H - Q_C}
$$

It is not a spontaneous process. The supplied work is what allows heat to be transferred from the colder reservoir to the hotter one.

### Coefficient of Performance

The performance of a heat pump is characterized by its coefficient of performance (COP) and not by thermal efficiency.

For a heat pump whose purpose is to deliver heat to the hot reservoir,

$$
    \mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \frac{Q_H}{W}
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \frac{Q_H}{Q_H - Q_C}}
$$

### Reversible Heat Engine

For a reversible heat pump,

$$
    \Delta S = 0 \implies \frac{Q_H}{T_H} = \frac{Q_C}{T_C} \implies \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = \frac{T_C}{T_H}
$$

Therefore,

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP,rev}} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_C}}
$$

Thus, heat pumps operate most efficiently when the temperature difference between the source and the destination is relatively small.

### Limitation

The second law of thermodynamics determines the minimum work required to transfer heat against its natural direction of flow. Thus, the reversible heat pump has the maximum possible coefficient of performance:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm{COP}_{\text{real}} < \mathrm{COP}_{\text{rev}}}
$$

### Refrigerator

A refrigerator and a heat pump operate according to the same thermodynamic principle. The difference is the desired heat transfer.

For a heat pump, the useful quantity is the heat delivered to the hot reservoir:

$$
    \mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \frac{Q_H}{W}
$$

For a refrigerator, the useful quantity is the heat removed from the cold reservoir:

$$
    \mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{R}} = \frac{Q_C}{W}
$$

Since, in both cases, $Q_H = Q_C + W$, we have

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{R}} + 1}
$$

The same physical device can therefore function as either a refrigerator or a heat pump, depending on which reservoir's heat transfer is considered useful.

## Carnot Theorem

Carnot's theorem establishes the maximum possible efficiency of a heat engine operating between two fixed temperatures. It consists of two fundamental statements:

1. **No heat engine operating between two reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible heat engine operating between the same reservoirs.**
2. **All reversible heat engines operating between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency, regardless of the working substance or the details of the cycle.**

Therefore, the efficiency of a reversible engine depends only on the temperatures of the two reservoirs.

For reservoirs at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$, with $T_H > T_C$, the maximum possible efficiency is

$$
    \boxed{\eta_{\mathrm{rev}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}}
$$

<div class="box__title">Proof</div>

**Proof of the First Statement:** Consider two heat engines operating between the same hot and cold reservoirs:

- Engine $A$ is an arbitrary engine with efficiency $\eta_A$.
- Engine $B$ is a reversible engine with efficiency $\eta_B$.

We will show that $\eta_A \leq \eta_B$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that the irreversible engine $A$ is more efficient than the reversible engine $B$, i.e., $\eta_A > \eta_B$. Because $B$ is reversible, it can be operated in reverse as a refrigerator or heat pump. Let engine $A$ absorb heat $Q_H$ from the hot reservoir and produce work $W_A = \eta_A Q_H$. It rejects heat $Q_{C, A} = Q_H - W_A$.

Now operate the reversible engine $B$ in reverse, using exactly the same amount of work $W_B = W_A$. Since $\eta_B = W_B / Q_{H, B}$, the heat that must be supplied to the reversed engine from the cold reservoir is related to its heat transfer at the hot reservoir.

Because we assumed $\eta_A > \eta_B$ for the same work output, it follows that $Q_{H, A} < Q_{H, B}$.

We can therefore scale the two devices so that their work transfers exactly cancel while the hot-reservoir heat transfers also cancel appropriately. The combined device produces a net transfer of heat from the cold reservoir to the hot reservoir **without any external work input**. This contradicts the **second law of thermodynamics**, which states that, heat cannot, by itself, be transferred from a colder body to a hotter body in a cyclic process.

Therefore our assumption must be false, and

$$
    \boxed{\eta_A \leq \eta_B}
$$

Hence, **no irreversible heat engine can be more efficient than a reversible engine operating between the same two reservoirs.**

---

**Proof of the Second Statement:** Now consider two reversible heat engines, $B$ and $C$, operating between the same reservoirs.

Suppose $\eta_B > \eta_C$. Because both engines are reversible, engine $C$ can be operated backward.

The same argument used above would then allow the combination of $B$ and reversed $C$ to transfer heat from the cold reservoir to the hot reservoir without external work, whcih violates the second law. Similarly, assuming $\eta_B < \eta_C$ would lead to the same contradiction by reversing $A$.

Therefore neither engine can be more efficient than the other, so they must be equally efficient:

$$
    \boxed{\eta_A=\eta_B}
$$

Thus, **all reversible heat engines operating between the same two temperatures have identical efficiencies.**

We can further show that their efficiency is independent of the nature of the working substance and depends only on the temperatures of the reservoirs:

For a reversible engine, the entropy balance gives

$$
\frac{Q_H}{T_H} = \frac{Q_C}{T_C} \implies \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}=\frac{T_C}{T_H}
$$

Since

$$
    \eta = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}
$$

we obtain

$$
    \boxed{\eta_{\mathrm{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}}
$$

This is known as the **Carnot efficiency**.
