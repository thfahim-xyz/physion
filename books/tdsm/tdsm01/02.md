# Laws of Thermodynamics

## Zeroth Law

If two systems are in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

The property that is the same for all systems in thermal equilibrium is called temperature.

## First Law

In a closed system, the change in internal energy of the system is equal to the difference between the heat supplied to the system and work done by the system on its surroundings.

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d U = \delta Q - \delta W}
$$

where

- $U$ is the internal energy of the system
- $Q$ is the heat supplied to the system
- $W$ is the work done by the system

$\delta$ denotes exact differentials (because $Q$ and $W$ are path dependent).

This law is essentially the **law of conservation of energy** for thermodynamic systems. Heat transfer into the system $(Q > 0)$ increases the internal energy and heat transfer out of the system $(Q < 0)$ decreases internal energy. Likewise, work done by the system $(W > 0)$ decreases internal energy and work done on the system $(Q < 0)$ increases internal energy.

When two initially isolated systems are combined into a new system, then the total internal energy of the combined system is equal to the sum of the internal energies of the two initial systems.

$$
    U = U_1 + U_2
$$

where $U$ is the total internal energy of the combined system; $U_1$ and $U_2$ are the internal energies of the individual systems.

**Note:** Our convention (known as the **physics convention**) defines $W$ as work done *by* the system $(W = W_{BY})$. There is an alternate convention (known as the **chemistry convention**) where $W$ is defined as the work done *on* the system $(W = W_{ON})$, in which case, the above equation becomes $\mathrm d U = \delta Q + \delta W$.

## Second Law

For every process occurring in an isolated system, the entropy cannot decrease. It remains constant for reversible processes and increases for irreversible processes. An isolated system in equilibrium has maximum entropy among all states satisfying the constraints.

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d S_{\mathrm{isolated}} \geq 0}
$$

This law establishes the preferred direction of spontaneous thermodynamic processes and identifies entropy as the quantity whose increase characterizes irreversibility.

Historically, several statements were used to describe the law, which were actually some consequences of the law, such as

- **Clausius Statement:** Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body.
- **Kelvin–Planck Statement:** No cyclic heat engine can convert all absorbed heat into work.
- The total entropy of the universe (considered as an isolated system) always increases.

### Clausius Theorem

For any cyclic process, the integral of heat exchanged divided by the absolute temperature over the cycle will never be positive. It is zero for a reversible cycle and negative for an irreversible cycle.

$$
    \boxed{\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \leq 0}
$$

For a reversible process, the Clausius theorem defines entropy.

$$
    \oint \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T} = 0 \implies \oint \mathrm d S = 0 \implies \boxed{\mathrm d S = \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}}
$$

## Third Law

The entropy of a system approaches a constant value as its temperature approaches **absolute zero**.
