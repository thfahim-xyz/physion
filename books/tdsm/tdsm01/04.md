# Thermodynamic Relations

## Fundamental Thermodynamic Relation

$$
    \mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V
$$

In general,

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
$$

For multiple different chemical species,

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \sum_i \mu_i \, \mathrm d N_i}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

The first law of thermodynamics for a simple compressible system is

$$
    \mathrm d U = \delta Q - \delta W
$$

For a reversible process,

$$
    \delta Q = T \, \mathrm d S
$$

$$
    \delta W = P \, \mathrm d V
$$

Therefore

$$
    \mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V
$$

To derive the general relation, suppose the system is open, so matter can be exchanged. Now the internal energy can also change due to changes in the number of particles. So we define

$$
    U \equiv U(S, V, N)
$$

$$
    \implies \mathrm d U = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N} \, \mathrm d S
                            + \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N} \, \mathrm d V
                            + \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V} \, \mathrm d N
$$

We already know that

$$
    \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N} = T, \quad \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N} = -P
$$

For the third term, we define the chemical potential as

$$
    \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V} = \mu
$$

Therefore

$$
    \mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N
$$

For particles of multiple chemical species, there are several different chemical potentials $\mu_i$. So the fully general form is

$$
    \mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \sum_i \mu_i \, \mathrm d N_i
$$

</div>

## Conjugate Relations of Thermodynamic Variables

$$
    \boxed{P = - \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{T = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\mu = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

We get the relations from the fundamental thermodynamic relation

$$
    \mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N
$$

Since, $U \equiv U(S, V, N)$, the total differential of $U$ is

$$
    \mathrm d U = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N} \, \mathrm d S 
                + \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N} \, \mathrm d V 
                + \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V} \, \mathrm d N
$$

By comparing the coefficients, we thus get

$$
    P = - \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N}, \quad
    T = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N}, \quad
    \mu = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V}
$$

</div>

## Maxwell's Relations

Maxwell's relations relate the rates of changes of the thermodynamic potentials to the changes of the state variables.

$$
    \boxed{\left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial V} \right)_S = - \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial S} \right)_V}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial P} \right)_S = - \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} \right)_P}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\left( \frac{\partial S}{\partial V} \right)_T = - \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \right)_V}
$$

$$
    \boxed{- \left( \frac{\partial S}{\partial P} \right)_T = - \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial T} \right)_P}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Maxwell's relations come from the symmetry of mixed partial derivatives because the thermodynamic state functions are smooth functions.

**From Internal Energy:**

$$
    T = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_V, \quad
    P = - \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_S
$$

By differentiating both sides of the equations, we get

$$
    \left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial V} \right)_S = \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial V \partial S}, \quad
    - \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial S} \right)_V = \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial S \partial V}
$$

Since, the mixed partial derivatives are symmetric,

$$
    \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial V \partial S} = \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial S \partial V}
$$

$$
    \implies \left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial V} \right)_S = - \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial S} \right)_V
$$

We can get other the relations in a similar way.

</div>

## General Heat-Capacity Relation

$$
    \boxed{C_P - C_V = T \left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \right)_V \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial T} \right)_P}
$$
