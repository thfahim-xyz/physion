# Thermodynamic Quantities

## State Quantities

### Pressure

Pressure $P$ is the normal force per unit area exerted by a system on its boundary. It measures a system's tendency to change its volume.

$$
    P = \frac{F_n}{A}
$$

The thermodynamic definition of pressure is

$$
    \boxed{P = - \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_{S, N}}
$$

### Volume

Volume $V$ is the measure of the region of space occupied by the system.

### Temperature

Temperature $T$ reflects the average kinetic energy of the particles in a system, providing a quantitative measure of how energy is distributed among microscopic degrees of freedom. It is the property that is equal for systems in thermal equilibrium (defined by the zeroth law of thermodynamics).

For a reversible process,

$$
    T = \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{\mathrm d S}
$$

The thermodynamic definition of temperature is

$$
    \boxed{T = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial S} \right)_{V, N}}
$$

### Entropy

Entropy $S$ is a quantity that characterizes the degree of disorder of a system. Entropy has no absolute value and we can only describe changes in entropy.

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d S = \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}} \implies \Delta S = \int \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}
$$

### Chemical Potential

Chemical potential $\mu$ is the energy of a chemical species that can be absorbed or released by a system due to a change in the number of particles of the given species. It measures the thermodynamic tendency of particles to be transferred between a system and its surroundings.

The thermodynamic definition of chemical potential is

$$
    \boxed{\mu = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N} \right)_{S, V}}
$$

If there are multiple chemical species of different types, then for each species,

$$
    \mu_i = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial N_i} \right)_{S, V, N_{j \neq i}}
$$

### Particle Number

Particle number $N$ is the total number of constituent particles in the system.

Sometimes, the number of particles is described by the **amount of substance** (number of moles) $n$ instead. It is related to particle number by

$$
    n = \frac{N}{N_A}
$$

where $N_A$ is the Avogadro constant.

The number of particles is also described by **number density** $\rho_N$, which is defined as

$$
    \rho_N = \frac{N}{V}
$$

where $V$ is the volume.

## Thermodynamic Potentials

### Internal Energy

Internal energy is the energy contained within a thermodynamic system. It is the capacity to do work plus the capacity to release heat. It cannot be measured absolutely, but its change is measurable.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & U \equiv U(S, V, N) = TS - PV + \mu N \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \boxed{\mathrm d U = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Enthalpy

Enthalpy is the capacity to do non-mechanical work plus the capacity to release heat. It cannot be measured absolutely, but its change is measurable.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & H \equiv H(S, P, N) = U + PV \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \boxed{\mathrm d H = T \, \mathrm d S + V \, \mathrm d P + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & H(S, P) = U + PV \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d H = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V + V \, \mathrm d P = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V + P \, \mathrm d V + V \, \mathrm d P \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d H = T \, \mathrm d S + V \, \mathrm d P
\end{aligned}
$$

We can generalize and add chemical potential and particle number term:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d H = T \, \mathrm d S + V \, \mathrm d P + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
$$

</div>

### Helmholtz Free Energy

Helmholtz free energy is the capacity to do mechanical work plus the capacity to do non-mechanical work.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & F \equiv F(T, V, N) = U - TS \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \boxed{\mathrm d F = - S \, \mathrm d T - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & F(T, V) = U - TS \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d F = \mathrm d U - T \, \mathrm d S - S \, \mathrm d T = T \, \mathrm d S - P \, \mathrm d V - T \, \mathrm d S - S \, \mathrm d T \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d F = - S \, \mathrm d T - P \, \mathrm d V
\end{aligned}
$$

We can generalize and add chemical potential and particle number term:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d F = - S \, \mathrm d T - P \, \mathrm d V + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
$$

</div>

### Gibbs Free Energy

Gibbs free energy is the capacity to do non-mechanical work.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & G \equiv G(P, T, N) = H - TS = U + PV - TS \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \boxed{\mathrm d G = V \, \mathrm d P - S \, \mathrm d T + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & G(P, T) = U + PV - TS = H - TS \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d G = \mathrm d H - T \, \mathrm d S - S \, \mathrm d T = T \, \mathrm d S + V \, \mathrm d P - T \, \mathrm d S - S \, \mathrm d T \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d G = V \, \mathrm d P - S \, \mathrm d T
\end{aligned}
$$

We can generalize and add chemical potential and particle number term:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d G = V \, \mathrm d P - S \, \mathrm d T + \mu \, \mathrm d N}
$$

</div>

### Relation between Thermodynamic Potentials

$$
    \mathrm d (PV) = \mathrm d H - \mathrm d U = \mathrm d G - \mathrm d F
$$

$$
    \mathrm d (TS) = \mathrm d U - \mathrm d F = \mathrm d H - \mathrm d G
$$

## Process Quantities

### Heat

Heat $Q$ is the transfer of energy across the boundary of a system due to a temperature difference between the system and its surroundings.

For a reversible process,

$$
    \delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}} = T \, \mathrm d S
$$

### Work

Work $W$ is the transfer of energy across the boundary of a system by the action of generalized forces acting through their corresponding generalized displacements.

For a simple compressible system,

$$
    \boxed{\delta W = P \, \mathrm d V}
$$

## Response Quantities

### Heat Capacity

Heat capacity is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a system by one unit under specified constraints.

$$
    C = \frac{\delta Q}{\mathrm d T}
$$

Heat capacity is path dependent so it must always be defined for a particular process.

**Molar Heat Capacity:** Molar heat capacity, which is the heat capacity per mole, is often used instead of the standard heat capacity.

$$
    C = \frac{1}{n} \frac{\delta Q}{\mathrm d T}
$$

#### Isochoric Heat Capacity

$$
    \boxed{C_V = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} \right)_V}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

At constant volume,

$$
    C_V = \left( \frac{\delta Q}{\mathrm d T} \right)_V
$$

For an isochoric process, $V = \text{constant} \implies \mathrm d V = 0$. So the first law of thermodynamics is

$$
    \mathrm d U = \delta Q - P \, \mathrm d V \quad \implies \mathrm d U = \delta Q
$$

Therefore

$$
    C_V = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} \right)_V
$$

</div>

#### Isobaric Heat Capacity

$$
    \boxed{C_P = \left( \frac{\partial H}{\partial T} \right)_P} = \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} \right)_P + P \, \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial T} \right)_P
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

At constant pressure,

$$
    C_P = \left( \frac{\delta Q}{\mathrm d T} \right)_P
$$

For an isochoric process, $P = \text{constant} \implies \mathrm d P = 0$. The first law of thermodynamics is

$$
    \mathrm d U = \delta Q - P \, \mathrm d V \quad \implies \delta Q = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V
$$

The definition of enthalpy is

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & H = U + PV \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d H = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V + V \, \mathrm d P \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d H = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathrm d H = \delta Q
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
    C_P = \left( \frac{\partial H}{\partial T} \right)_P
$$

</div>

### Compressibility

Compressibility is the measure of the instantaneous relative volume change of an object as a response to pressure change.

$$
    \boxed{\beta = - \frac{1}{V} \frac{\partial V}{\partial P}} = - \frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial P}
$$

It is the reciprocal of bulk modulus $(\beta = 1 / B)$.

#### Isothermal Compressibility

$$
    \boxed{\beta_T = - \frac{1}{V} \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial P} \right)_T}
$$

#### Isentropic Compressibility

$$
    \boxed{\beta_S = - \frac{1}{V} \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial P} \right)_S}
$$

### Thermal Expansion Coefficient

Thermal expansion coefficient measures the tendency of an object to expand due to heat.

$$
    \alpha = \frac{1}{V} \left( \frac{\partial V}{\partial T} \right)_P
$$

#### Thermal Expansion of Solid Objects

The amount of expansion of length, area and volume of any solid object is respectively given by

$$
    \mathrm d L = \alpha_L L \, \mathrm d T, \quad
    \mathrm d A = \alpha_A A \, \mathrm d T, \quad
    \mathrm d V = \alpha_V V \, \mathrm d T
$$

where $\alpha_L, \alpha_A, \alpha_V$ are the linear, area and volumetric thermal expansion coefficients, respectively. They are related by

$$
    \alpha_L = \frac{\alpha_L}{2} = \frac{\alpha_V}{3}
$$
