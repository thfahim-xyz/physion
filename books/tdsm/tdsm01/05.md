# Ideal Gases

## Ideal Gas Law

$$
    \boxed{PV = nRT}
$$

$$
    \implies \mathrm d T = \frac{P \, \mathrm d V + V \, \mathrm d P}{nR}
$$

$$
    \implies \frac{\mathrm d P}{P} + \frac{\mathrm d V}{V} = \frac{\mathrm d T}{T}
$$

Other forms of the ideal gas law are

$$
    PV = N k_B T, \quad PM = \rho RT
$$

where $N$ is the particle number, $\rho$ is the mass density of the gas, $M$ is the molar mass of the gas and $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant.

## Work Done by Ideal Gases

$$
    \boxed{W = \int P \, \mathrm d V}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

$$
    W = \int P \delta W
$$

Since

$$
    \delta W = P \, \mathrm d V
$$

we have

$$
    \boxed{W = \int P \, \mathrm d V}
$$

</div>

## Joule's Law for Ideal Gases

The internal energy of an ideal gas is independent of its volume and depends only on its temperature.

$$
    \left( \frac{\partial U}{\partial V} \right)_T = 0 \implies \boxed{U = U(T)} \qquad \text{(For ideal gases)}
$$

$$
    \implies \mathrm d U = n C_V \, \mathrm d T
$$

where $C_V$ is the isochoric molar heat capacity.

## Enthalpy of Ideal Gases

$$
    \mathrm d H = n C_P \, \mathrm d T
$$

where $C_P$ is the isobaric molar heat capacity.

## Mayer's Relation

$$
    \boxed{C_P - C_V = R}
$$

where $C_P$ and $C_V$ are the isobaric molar heat capacity and isochoric molar heat capacity, respectively.

### Heat Capacity Ratio

We can express $C_P$ and $C_V$ in terms of the adiabatic index $\gamma$, defined by

$$
    \boxed{\gamma = \frac{C_P}{C_V}} = \left( 1 + \frac{2}{f} \right)
$$

$$
    \implies C_V = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1}, \quad C_P = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}
$$

where $f$ is the number of degrees of freedom of a gas particle.

Values of the adiabatic index for different types of molecules:

<div class="table__container" markdown="1">

| Molecule Type       | Adiabatic Index $\gamma$ |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Monatomic Molecule  | $\dfrac{5}{3} = 1.67$    |
| Linear Molecule     | $\dfrac{7}{5} = 1.40$    |
| Non-linear Molecule | $\dfrac{4}{3} = 1.33$    |

</div>

## Isobaric Process

$$
    \frac{V}{T} = \text{constant} \qquad \text{(Charles' Law)}
$$

$$
    W = P \Delta V = nR \Delta T
$$

$$
    Q = \Delta H = n C_P \Delta T
$$

$$
    \Delta U = n C_V \Delta T
$$

Note that $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

For an isobaric process, $P = \text{constant} \implies \mathrm d P = 0$

---

**Charles' Law:** From the ideal gas law, we have

$$
    PV = nRT \implies \frac{V}{T} = \frac{nR}{P} \implies \boxed{\frac{V}{T} = \text{constant}}
$$

---

**Work:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \delta W = P \, \mathrm d V \implies W = \int \delta W
$$

$$
    \implies W = \int P \, \mathrm d V = P \int \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{W = P \Delta V}
$$

---

**Heat:** The fundamental thermodynamic relation for isobaric processes is

$$
    \mathrm d U = \mathrm d Q - P \, \mathrm d V \implies \mathrm d Q = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V
$$

Enthalpy is defined as

$$
    H(S, P) = U + PV
$$

$$
    \implies \mathrm d H = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V + V \, \mathrm d P
$$

For an isobaric process, $\mathrm d P = 0$. So

$$
    \mathrm d H = \mathrm d U + P \, \mathrm d V
$$

Therefore,

$$
    \mathrm d Q = \mathrm d H
$$

For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d H = n C_P \, \mathrm d T \implies \Delta H = n C_P \Delta T
$$

Thus,

$$
    \boxed{Q = \Delta H = n C_P \Delta T}
$$

---

**Internal Energy:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d U = n C_V \, \mathrm d T \implies \boxed{\Delta U = n C_V \Delta T}
$$

</div>

## Isochoric Process

$$
    \frac{P}{T} = \text{constant} \qquad \text{(Gay-Lussac's Law)}
$$

$$
    W = 0
$$

$$
    Q = \Delta U = n C_V \Delta T
$$

$$
    \Delta H = n C_P \Delta T
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

For an isochoric process, $V = \text{constant} \implies \mathrm d V = 0$

---

**Gay-Lussac's Law:** From the ideal gas law, we have

$$
    PV = nRT \implies \frac{P}{T} = \frac{nR}{V} \implies \boxed{\frac{P}{T} = \text{constant}}
$$

---

**Work:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \delta W = P \, \mathrm d V \implies \delta W = 0 \implies \boxed{W = 0}
$$

---

**Heat:** Since $\delta W = 0$, the fundamental thermodynamic relation becomes

$$
    \mathrm d Q = \mathrm d U \implies Q = \Delta U = n C_V \Delta T
$$

For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d U = n C_V \, \mathrm d T \implies \Delta U = n C_V \Delta T
$$

Therefore,

$$
    \boxed{Q = \Delta U = n C_V \Delta T}
$$

---

**Enthalpy:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d H = n C_P \, \mathrm d T \implies \boxed{\Delta H = n C_P \Delta T}
$$

</div>

## Isothermal Process

$$
    PV = \text{constant} \qquad \text{(Boyle's Law)}
$$

$$
    W = Q = nRT \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1} = - nRT \ln \frac{P_2}{P_1}
$$

$$
    \Delta U = 0
$$

$$
    \Delta H = 0
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

For an isothermal process, $T = \text{constant} \implies \mathrm d T = 0$

---

**Boyle's Law:** From the ideal gas law, we have

$$
    PV = nRT \implies \boxed{PV = \text{constant}}
$$

---

**Work:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \delta W = P \, \mathrm d V \implies W = \int \delta W
$$

$$
    \implies W = \int P \, \mathrm d V
$$

Using the ideal gas law, $PV = nRT$, we get

$$
    W = \int \frac{nRT}{V} \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies W = nRT \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{1}{V} \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies W = nRT \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

Since, from Boyle's law, $P_1 V_1 = P_2 P_2$, we also get

$$
    W = - nRT \ln \frac{P_2}{P_1}
$$

Therefore,

$$
    \boxed{W = Q = nRT \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1} = - nRT \ln \frac{P_2}{P_1}}
$$

---

**Internal Energy:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d U = n C_V \, \mathrm d T
$$

For an isothermal process, $\mathrm d T = 0$, so

$$
    \mathrm d U = 0 \implies \boxed{\Delta U = 0}
$$

---

**Enthalpy:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d H = n C_P \, \mathrm d T
$$

For an isothermal process, $\mathrm d T = 0$, so

$$
    \mathrm d H = 0 \implies \boxed{\Delta H = 0}
$$


</div>

## Reversible Adiabatic Process

$$
    PV^{\gamma} = \text{constant}, \quad 
    TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}, \quad 
    T^{\gamma} P^{1 - \gamma} = \text{constant}
$$

$$
    W = - \Delta U = - n C_V \Delta T = \frac{nR}{\gamma - 1} (T_1 - T_2) = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1}
$$

$$
    \Delta H = n C_P \Delta T
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

For an adiabatic process, $\delta Q = 0 \implies Q = 0$

---

**Adiabatic Gas Laws:** For adiabatic processes, the first law of thermodynamics gives

$$
    \mathrm d U = - \delta W
$$

$$
    \implies n C_V \, \mathrm d T = - P \, \mathrm d V
$$

From the ideal gas law, $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V}$, so

$$
    n C_V \, \mathrm d T = - \frac{nRT}{V} \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies \frac{C_V}{T} \, \mathrm d T = - \frac{R}{V} \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies \int_{T_1}^{T_2} \frac{C_V}{T} \, \mathrm d T = - \int_{V_1}^{V_2} \frac{R}{V} \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies C_V \ln \frac{T_2}{T_1} = - R \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

Since $R = C_P - C_V$, we get

$$
    C_V \ln \frac{T_2}{T_1} = - (C_P - C_V) \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

$$
    \implies C_V \left( \ln \frac{T_2}{T_1} - \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1} \right) = - C_P \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

$$
    \implies \ln \frac{T_2 V_1}{T_1 V_2} = - \frac{C_P}{C_V} \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

Now, $\gamma = C_P / C_V$, so

$$
    \ln \frac{V_1 T_2}{T_1 V_2} = - \gamma \ln \frac{V_2}{V_1}
$$

$$
    \implies \ln \frac{V_1 T_2}{T_1 V_2} = \ln \left( \frac{V_1}{V_2} \right)^{\gamma}
$$

$$
    \implies \ln \frac{nR P_2}{P_1 nR} = \ln \left( \frac{V_1}{V_2} \right)^{\gamma}
$$

$$
    \implies \ln \frac{P_2}{P_1} - \ln \left( \frac{V_1}{V_2} \right)^{\gamma} = 0
$$

$$
    \implies \frac{P_2 V_2^{\gamma}}{P_1 v_1^{\gamma}} = 1
$$

$$
    \implies P_1 v_1^{\gamma} = P_2 v_2^{\gamma}
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{PV^{\gamma} = \text{constant}}
$$

The other laws follow from the above equation by using the ideal gas law, $PV = nRT$

$$
    TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}, \quad 
    T^{\gamma} P^{1 - \gamma} = \text{constant}
$$

---

**Work:** For adiabatic processes, the first law of thermodynamics gives

$$
    \mathrm d U = - \delta W
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{W = - \Delta U = - n C_V \Delta T} = \frac{nR}{\gamma - 1} (T_1 - T_2) = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1}
$$

---

**Enthalpy:** For an ideal gas,

$$
    \mathrm d H = n C_P \, \mathrm d T \implies \boxed{\Delta H = n C_P \Delta T}
$$
