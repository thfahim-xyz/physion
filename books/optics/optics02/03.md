# Polarization

## Polarization

**Definition:** polarization is the behavior of the transverse electric-field vector $\mathbf E$ at fixed $\mathbf r$ as a function of time. For a monochromatic plane wave along $\mathbf{\hat z}$:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf E(z,t)=\mathrm{Re}\left[(E_x\,\mathbf{\hat x}+E_y\,\mathbf{\hat y})e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\right]}
$$

The tip of $\mathbf E$ traces an ellipse in the $xy$-plane (polarization ellipse). The three pure states are:

$$
    \boxed{E_y=0\implies \text{linear}},\quad \boxed{E_y=\pm iE_x\implies \text{circular}},\quad \boxed{\text{otherwise}\implies \text{elliptical}}
$$

Unpolarized light is an incoherent mixture with no stable ellipse; partially polarized light mixes pure and unpolarized components.

**Interpretation:** transversality $(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf E_0=0$ from Light Waves) leaves exactly two complex amplitudes; their relative magnitude and phase fix the ellipse. Interference and diffraction treated intensities; polarization treats the vector character.

**Remark:** dielectric $\mathbf P,\mathbf D$ (Electrodynamics) and gravitational polarizations (Relativity) are separate topics and are not redefined here.

## Polarization Angle

**Definition:** for the polarization ellipse, the polarization angle $\psi$ is the orientation of its major axis measured from $x$ toward $y$:

$$
    \boxed{\tan 2\psi=\frac{2\mathrm{Re}(E_x^*E_y)}{|E_x|^2-|E_y|^2}}
$$

For purely linear light this reduces to the field direction, $E_y/E_x=\tan\psi$.

**Remark:** this angle describes the ellipse itself and must not be confused with Brewster's angle below, which is an incidence angle producing polarization by reflection.

## Malus' Law

**Law:** linearly polarized light of irradiance $I_0$ incident on an ideal polarizer with transmission axis at angle $\theta$ to the field transmits:

$$
    \boxed{I=I_0\cos^2\theta}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Only the projection $E_0\cos\theta$ passes, and irradiance is proportional to $\lvert E \rvert^2$. For unpolarized input, averaging over $\theta$ gives $I=I_0/2$ after one polarizer.

The canonical consequence is extinction by crossed polarizers: $\theta=\pi/2\implies I=0$.

</div>

**Limitation:** real polarizers have finite extinction; the law assumes a single frequency and ideal dichroism.

## Brewster's Angle

**Result:** for incidence from $n_1$ to $n_2$, the $p$-polarized (in-plane) amplitude vanishes when the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular. The incidence angle satisfying this is:

$$
    \boxed{\tan\theta_B=\frac{n_2}{n_1}},\quad \boxed{\theta_B+\theta_t=\frac{\pi}{2}}
$$

so reflected light at $\theta_B$ is purely $s$-polarized.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Snell gives $n_1\sin\theta_B=n_2\sin\theta_t$; perpendicularity gives $\theta_t=\pi/2-\theta_B$, so $\sin\theta_t=\cos\theta_B$. Division yields $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$. Equivalently, the Fresnel coefficient $r_p$ vanishes there.

For air to glass $(n\approx 1.5)$, $\theta_B\approx 56^{\circ}$.

</div>

**Limitation:** exact zero occurs only for non-absorbing media; for metals the minimum is nonzero (pseudo-Brewster angle).
