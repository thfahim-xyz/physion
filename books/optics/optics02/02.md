# Interference and Diffraction

## Interference

**Result:** for two monochromatic fields $E_1,E_2$ with phase difference $\delta$, the time-averaged irradiance is:

$$
    \boxed{I=|E_1+E_2|^2=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\delta}
$$

with $\delta=2\pi\Delta/\lambda$ for path difference $\Delta$. Constructive and destructive interference are:

$$
    \boxed{\delta=2\pi m\implies \text{bright}},\quad \boxed{\delta=2\pi\left(m+\tfrac12\right)\implies \text{dark}}
$$

for integer $m$.

**Interpretation:** interference redistributes energy into fringes; total power is conserved. Equal intensities give $I=2I_0(1+\cos\delta)=4I_0\cos^2(\delta/2)$.

## Coherence

**Definition:** coherence measures stable phase relations. Temporal coherence is limited by spectral width $\Delta\lambda$; spatial coherence by source extent.

**Result:** the coherence length for quasi-monochromatic light is:

$$
    \boxed{L_c\sim\frac{\lambda^2}{\Delta\lambda}}
$$

Fringes of order $m$ with $\Delta=m\lambda$ remain visible only for $\Delta\lesssim L_c$.

**Interpretation:** a narrow line (small $\Delta\lambda$) interferes over many wavelengths; white light interferes only near zero path difference. Spatial coherence requires the source to subtend a small angle at the slits; extended sources wash out fringes unless apertured.

**Remark:** laser physics is not needed here; $L_c$ is the only quantitative criterion carried forward.

## Young's Double-slit Experiment

**Setup:** two narrow slits separated by $d$, screen at distance $L\gg d$, monochromatic $\lambda$.

**Result:** with $\theta$ from the central axis:

$$
    \boxed{d\sin\theta=m\lambda\implies \text{bright}},\quad \boxed{d\sin\theta=\left(m+\tfrac12\right)\lambda\implies \text{dark}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

In the far field the paths differ by $\Delta=d\sin\theta$, so $\delta=2\pi\Delta/\lambda$; the interference conditions above give the fringe positions. On the screen $y=L\tan\theta\approx L\theta$, so the fringe spacing is:

$$
    \boxed{\Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d}}
$$

</div>

**Limit:** the envelope of finite slit width modulates these fringes; that factor belongs to Single-slit Diffraction below.

## Multiple-slit Interference

**Result:** for $N$ identical slits with spacing $d$, the principal maxima remain at:

$$
    \boxed{d\sin\theta=m\lambda}
$$

but sharpen as $N$ grows. Between adjacent principal maxima there are $N-1$ minima and $N-2$ secondary maxima.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Phasor summation gives $E\propto \sin(N\delta/2)/\sin(\delta/2)$ with $\delta=2\pi d\sin\theta/\lambda$. Maxima occur when the denominator vanishes ($\delta=2\pi m$); the numerator's extra zeros supply the $N-1$ minima.

</div>

**Interpretation:** increasing $N$ concentrates energy into narrow principal fringes, the basis of the grating below.

## Thin-film Interference

**Setup:** film of index $n$ and thickness $t$ with internal angle $\theta_t$. Reflection off a higher-index medium suffers a half-wave $(\pi)$ phase flip; off a lower-index medium it does not.

**Result:** the round-trip phase gives, for one flip (e.g. air–film–substrate with $n_{\mathrm{air}} \lt n \gt n_{\mathrm{sub}}$ or the reverse single-flip case):

$$
    \boxed{2nt\cos\theta_t=\left(m+\tfrac12\right)\lambda\implies \text{bright}},\quad \boxed{2nt\cos\theta_t=m\lambda\implies \text{dark}}
$$

with the bright/dark conditions interchanged when zero or two flips occur.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

The extra optical path is $2nt\cos\theta_t$; adding $\lambda/2$ for one half-wave flip shifts the constructive condition by half an order.

The canonical air wedge of local thickness $t(x)$ at normal incidence gives $2t=m\lambda$ (dark with one flip) per fringe, so each fringe steps $\lambda/2$ in thickness.

</div>

## Single-slit Diffraction

**Setup:** slit of width $a$, Fraunhofer screen, monochromatic $\lambda$.

**Result:** minima occur at:

$$
    \boxed{a\sin\theta=m\lambda,\quad m=\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots}
$$

with no minimum at $m=0$ (central maximum). The full irradiance with $\beta=\pi a\sin\theta/\lambda$ is:

$$
    \boxed{I=I_0\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

The Huygens integral across the slit gives $E\propto\int_{-a/2}^{a/2}e^{ikx\sin\theta}\,\mathrm d x\propto \sin\beta/\beta$; zeros of the numerator with nonzero denominator give $a\sin\theta=m\lambda$.

</div>

**Interpretation:** narrowing $a$ broadens the central lobe; this envelope multiplies the Young fringes when slits have finite width.

## Diffraction Grating

**Result:** for grating spacing $d$ (grating constant $N=1/d$):

$$
    \boxed{d\sin\theta=m\lambda}
$$

in transmission at normal incidence (with $\sin\theta_i$ added for oblique incidence). Missing orders occur where this coincides with single-slit minima.

The canonical spectroscopic figure of merit in order $m$ with $N_{\mathrm{tot}}$ illuminated grooves is the resolving power:

$$
    \boxed{R=\frac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda}=mN_{\mathrm{tot}}}
$$

**Interpretation:** the grating is the large-$N$ limit of multiple-slit interference modulated by the single-slit envelope; higher orders and denser rulings resolve finer wavelength differences.

**Limitation:** the scalar Fraunhofer form suffices here; efficiency and polarization dependence require the vector grating theory.
