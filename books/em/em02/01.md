# Section 1

## Electric Charge in Matter

In dielectric materials, there are 2 types of charges:

- **Bound Charge:** Bound charge is the net accumulation of charge from the orientation of the dipoles created by stationary charges in the material. Bound charges cannot be removed from the material and they do not conduct electricity.

    $$
        Q_b = \int_V \rho_b \, \mathrm d V
    $$

- **Free Charge:** The net excess charge in the material that can move into electrostatic equilibrium. Free charges can move throughout the material and can conduct electricity.

    $$
        Q_f = \int_V \rho_f \, \mathrm d V
    $$

## Electric Charge Density in Matter

### Volume Charge Density

In matter,

$$
    \rho = \rho_f + \rho_b
$$

### Surface Charge Density

In matter,

$$
    \sigma = \sigma_f + \sigma_b
$$

## Permittivity

$$
    \varepsilon_r = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

## Electric Susceptibility

A constant indicating the degree of polarization of a dielectric material in response to an electric field.

$$
    \chi_e = \varepsilon_r - 1 = \dfrac{\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

In vacuum, $\chi_e = 0$

## Polarizability

Polarizability $\alpha$ is defined by

$$
    \mathbf p = \alpha \mathbf E
$$

where $\mathbf p$ is the induced electric dipole moment of an atom and $\mathbf E$ is the electric field that produces it.

## Polarization Density

Polarization density expresses the volumetric density of electric dipole moments in a dielectric material.

$$
    \mathbf P = \dfrac{\mathrm d \mathbf p}{\mathrm d V} = \rho_b \mathbf d
$$

where $\rho_b$ is the bound charge density and $\mathbf d$ is the separation vector (displacement of the charge due to an applied electric field).

In linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf P = \chi_e \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E \implies \mathbf P = \dfrac{\chi_e}{\varepsilon} \mathbf D
$$

## Polarization Current Density

$$
    \mathbf J_P = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf P}{\partial t}
$$

In electrostatics,

$$
    \mathbf J_P = \mathbf 0
$$

## Electric Displacement Field

Electric displacement field is an auxiliary field that accounts for the electromagnetic effects of polarization and that of an electric field.

$$
    \mathbf D = \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E + \mathbf P
$$

In vacuum,

$$
    \mathbf D = \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E
$$

In linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf D = ( 1 + \chi_e ) \mathbf E = \varepsilon \mathbf E
$$

## Electric Displacement Flux

$$
    \Phi_D = \int_S \mathbf D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

In linear materials,

$$
    \Phi_D = \varepsilon \Phi_E
$$

## Displacement Current Density

$$
    \mathbf J_D = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf D}{\partial t} = \mathbf J_P  + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t}
$$

In linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf J_D = \varepsilon \dfrac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t}
$$

## Displacement Current

$$
    I_D = \int_S \mathbf J_D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{\partial \Phi_D}{\partial t}
$$

In linear materials,

$$
    I_D = \varepsilon \dfrac{\partial \Phi_E}{\partial t}
$$

## Electric Field Energy in Matter

In matter,

$$
    u_e = \dfrac{1}{2} \mathbf E \cdot \mathbf D
$$
