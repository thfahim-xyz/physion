# Magnetic Field

## Electric Current

Electric current is a flow of charged particles moving through an electrical conductor or space.

$$
    I = \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

### Current Density

Amount of charge per unit time that flows through a unit area of a chosen cross section.

$$
    \mathbf J = \dfrac{\partial I}{\partial A} 
$$

$$
    \mathbf J = \rho \mathbf v
$$

### Magnetic Force

In magnetostatics, magnetic force is the force exerted by a magnetic field on a moving charged particle.

On a point charge,

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F_m = q \mathbf v \times \mathbf B}
$$

On a current-carrying wire,

$$
    \mathbf F_m = I \int_C \mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \mathbf B
$$

## Magnetic Field

Magnetic field $\mathbf B$ is the physical field responsible for magnetic influence on moving electric charges, electric currents, and magnetic materials. It is a pseudovector field and is produced by electric fields varying in time.

### Biot-Savart Law

Biot-Savart law describes the magnetic field generated by a steady current.

For a current element $I \, \mathrm d \mathbf \ell$, the infinitesimal magnetic field at a point is

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \boxed{\mathrm d \mathbf B = \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \frac{I \, \mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{r^2}} \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi} \int \dfrac{\mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{r^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux is the measure of magnetic field through a surface.

$$
    \Phi_B = \int_B \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Magnetic Fields of Common Current Distributions

### Moving Point Charge

$$
    \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \dfrac{q \mathbf v \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{|\mathbf r|^2}
$$

### Infinite Straight Wire

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf B = \frac{\mu_0}{2 \pi} \frac{I}{R} \hat{\mathbf r}}
$$

### Infinite Cylindrical Straight Wire

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf B =
        \begin{cases}
            \dfrac{\mu_0}{2 \pi} \dfrac{I r}{R^2} \hat{\mathbf r} & , & \text{Inside the wire} \; (r < R) \\[2ex]
            \dfrac{\mu_0}{2 \pi} \dfrac{I}{r} \hat{\mathbf r} & , & \text{Outside the wire} \; (r \geq R)
        \end{cases}}
$$

### Infinite Solenoid

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf B =
        \begin{cases}
            \dfrac{\mu_0 I N}{L} \hat{\mathbf r} & , & \text{Inside the solenoid} \; (r < R) \\[2ex]
            \, \mathbf 0 & , & \text{Outside the solenoid} \; (r \geq R)
        \end{cases}}
$$

where $N$ is the number of turns and $L$ is the length of the solenoid. The solenoid has identical and uniform turns.

### Toroid

$$
    B = \frac{\mu_0}{2 \pi} \frac{IN}{R}
$$

where $N$ is the number of poloidal loops and $R$ is the radius of each poloidal loop. The toroid has uniformly distributed poloidal loops.
