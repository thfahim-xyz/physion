# Magnetostatics in Matter

## Electric Current in Matter

In magnetic materials, The total current $I$ is composed of 2 types of currents $(I = I_f + I_b)$:

**Bound Current:** Current produced by bound charges.

$$
    I_b = \int_S \mathbf J_b \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \frac{\mathrm d Q_b}{\mathrm d t}
$$

**Free Current:** Current produced by free charges.

$$
    I_f = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \frac{\mathrm d Q_f}{\mathrm d t}
$$

In a conductor,

$$
    I = n A Q v_d
$$

where $v_d$ is the drift speed of the charges, $n$ is the number density of the charges and $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the conductor.

### Current Density in Matter

$$
    \mathbf J = \mathbf J_f + \mathbf J_b
$$

where

- $\mathbf J_f = \rho_f \mathbf v_d$ is the free charge density

- $\mathbf J_b = \mathbf J_M = \nabla \times \mathbf M$ is the bound current density (for magnetostatics)

## Magnetic Dipoles

### Magnetic Moment

There are 2 models that describe magnetic moment.

#### Magnetic Pole Model

This model assumes fictitious monopoles that create a magnetic dipole which has magnetic moment. This makes the model analogous to electric dipole. In this model, the magnetic field is represented by the irrotational $\mathbf H$-field, analogous to the electric field.

The magnetic moment is given by

$$
    \mathbf m = p \mathbf \ell
$$

where $p$ is the monopole charge and $\mathbf \ell$ is the separation vector.

However, **this model is older and is not usually used** because magnetic dipoles do not exist.

#### Ampèrian Loop Model

This model is based on the fact that a loop of electric current produces a magnetic field that is similar to that of a dipole. In this model, the magnetic field is represented by the solenoidal $\mathbf B$-field, analogous to the electric displacement field.

The magnetic moment is given by

$$
    \mathbf m = I \mathbf S
$$

where $I$ is the electric current and $\mathbf S$ is the directed surface area of the loop.

### Magnetic Field of a Magnetic Dipole

$$
    \mathbf B = \frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \dfrac{3 (\mathbf m \cdot \hat{\mathbf r}) \hat{\mathbf r} - \mathbf m}{r^3}
$$

### Torque on a Magnet

Torque on a magnetic dipole due to an external magnetic field.

$$
    \boldsymbol \tau = \int_V \mathrm d \mathbf m \times \mathbf B
$$

## Magnetization

### Magnetization Density

Magnetization density expresses the density of magnetic moments in a magnetic material. It describes bound electric current and is a pseudovector.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf M = \dfrac{\mathrm d \mathbf m}{\mathrm d V}}
$$

### Gauss's Law for Magnetization Density

## Magnetizing Field

An auxiliary field that accounts for the electromagnetic effects of magnetization and that of a magnetic field.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \mathbf B - \mathbf M}
$$

## Magnetizing Field Flux

$$
    \Phi_H = \int_S \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Magnetic Field Energy in Matter

$$
    u_m = \dfrac{1}{2} \mathbf B \cdot \mathbf H
$$

## Linear Magnetic Materials

### Magnetic Response Quantities

#### Permeability

$$
    \mu_r = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0}
$$

#### Magnetic Susceptibility

A constant indicating the degree of magnetization of a magnetic material in response to an electric field.

$$
    \chi_V = \mu_r - 1 = \dfrac{\mu - \mu_0}{\mu_0}
$$

### Magnetization Density for Linear Magnetic Materials

For linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf M = \chi_V \mathbf H = \dfrac{\chi_V}{\mu} \mathbf B
$$

### Magnetizing Field for Linear Magnetic Materials

For linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{(1 + \chi_V) \mu_0} \mathbf B = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \mathbf B
$$
