# Circuit Elements

## Voltage Source

A voltage source is an electrical circuit or element that maintains a constant potential difference across its terminals.

An ideal voltage source has zero internal resistance.

## Current Source

A current source is an electrical circuit or element that delivers or absorbs constant electric current, independent of the voltage across it.

An ideal current source has an infinite internal resistance.

## Resistor

A resistor is a passive electronic component that limits or regulates the flow of electric current in a circuit.

### Resistance of a Resistor

$$
    V = IR
$$

## Capacitor

A capacitor is a passive electronic component that stores electrical energy in an electric field.

### Capacitance of a Capacitor

$$
    Q = CV \implies I = C \frac{\mathrm d V}{\mathrm d t}
$$

### Energy of a Capacitor

The potential energy stored in a capacitor is

$$
    U = \frac{1}{2} C V^2
$$

## Inductor

An inductor is a passive electronic component that stores energy in a magnetic field when electric current flows through it.

### Inductance of an Inductor

$$
    V = L \frac{\mathrm d I}{\mathrm d t}
$$

### Energy of an Inductor

The potential energy stored in an inductor is

$$
    U = \frac{1}{2} L I^2
$$

## Measuring Instruments

### Ammeter

An ammeter is an instrument used to measure current in an electric circuit.

It has a low internal resistance so it causes a negligible amount of potential drop. It is connected in series and not in parallel (to avoid creating an short cicuit).

A galvanometer can be used as an ammeter by connecting a very small resistor, called a shunt resistance $R_A$, in parallel with the galvanometer.

$$
    R_A = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}
$$

where $I$ is the maximum total current to be measured, $G$ is the galvanometer's resistance and $I_g$ is the current required for a full-scale deflection on the galvanometer.

### Voltmeter

A voltmeter is an instrument used to measure voltage between two points in an electrical circuit.

It has a high internal resistance so it draws a negligible amount of current. This is also why it is connected in parallel and not in series (to avoid creating an open cicuit).

A galvanometer can be used as a voltmeter by connecting a very high resistor $R_V$ in series with the galvanometer.

$$
    R_V = \frac{V}{I_g} - G
$$

where $V$ is the maximum total voltage to be measured, $G$ is the galvanometer's resistance and $I_g$ is the current required for a full-scale deflection on the galvanometer.

### Ohmmeter

An ohmmeter is an instrument that measures resistance in an electrical circuit. It can be connected in either series or parallel with a circuit.
