# Circuit Analysis

## Kirchhoff's Circuit Laws

### Kirchhoff's Current Law

Kirchhoff's current law states that, for any node in an electrical circuit, the sum of currents flowing into that node is equal to the sum of currents flowing out of that node.

$$
    \boxed{\sum I_i = 0}
$$

### Kirchhoff's Voltage Law

Kirchhoff's voltage law states that, the directed sum of the voltages around any closed loop is zero.

$$
    \boxed{\sum V_i = 0}
$$

## Voltage Divider

For $n$ resistances connected in series, the voltage of each resistance is

$$
    V_i = \frac{R_i}{\sum R_i} V
$$

where $V$ is the total input voltage.

For 2 resistances,

$$
    V_1 = \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2} V, \quad V_2 = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} V
$$

## Current Divider

For $n$ resistances connected in parallel, the current through each resistance is

$$
    I_i = \frac{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}{R_i} I = \frac{1}{R_i} \left( \sum \frac{1}{R_i} \right)^{-1} I
$$

For 2 resistances,

$$
    I_1 = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} I, \quad I_2 = \frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2} I
$$

## RC Circuit

A RC circuit is an electric circuit composed of a resistor and a capacitor.

$$
    V_C + V_R = E
$$

The equation that describes an RC circuit is

$$
    \boxed{\tau_C \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t} + V_C = E}
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{V_C (t) = E + (V_0 - E) e^{- t / \tau_C}}
$$

where

- $\tau_C = RC$ is the capacitive time constant
- $E$ is the input voltage
- $V_0 = V_C (0)$ is the initial capacitor voltage
- $R$ is the resistance
- $C$ is the capacitance

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Let us consider a series RC circuit consisting of a voltage source $E$, capacitor of capacitance $C$, resistor of resistance $R$. Let $V_C \equiv V_C(t)$ be the capacitor voltage and $V_R \equiv V_R(t)$ the resistor voltage. Assume the capacitor initially has voltage $V_C(0) = V_0$.

Choosing the current $I(t)$ clockwise and using KVL, we get

$$
    E = V_R + V_C
$$

For the resistor,

$$
    V_R = IR
$$

For the capacitor,

$$
    I = C \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t}
$$

Substituting this into the resistor voltage equation gives

$$
    V_R = RC \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t}
$$

Let $\tau_C = RC$. Therefore,

$$
    \boxed{\tau_C \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t} + V_C = E}
$$

That's the fundamental RC charging equation. It's a first order linear ODE whose solution is the sum of a homogeneous solution (natural response) and a particular solution (forced response).

---

**Homogeneous solution:** The homogeneous solution is found by solving the ODE after setting the input $E$ to zero:

$$
    \tau_C \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t} + V_C = 0
$$

$$
    \implies \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{\mathrm d t} = - \frac{V_C}{\tau_C}
$$

$$
    \implies \frac{\mathrm d V_C}{V_C} = - \frac{\mathrm d t}{\tau_C}
$$

Integrating the above equation gives

$$
    \ln V_C = -\frac{t}{\tau_C} + \ln A
$$

$$
    \implies V_C(t) = A e^{-t/\tau_C}
$$

This is the natural response voltage (AKA zero-input voltage).

---

**Particular solution:** Since $E$ is constant, verifying a constant solution $V_C = k$ for the ODE gives $k = E$. Therefore,

$$
    V_C = E
$$

This is the forced response voltage (AKA steady-state voltage).

---

**Total solution:** Adding the two solutions gives

$$
    V_C(t) = E + A e^{-t/\tau_C}
$$

Applying the initial condition $V_C(0)=V_0$ leads to $V_0 = E + A$. So

$$
    \boxed{V_C(t) = E + (V_0 - E) e^{-t/\tau_C}} = \underbrace{V_0 e^{-t/\tau_C}}_{\text{zero-input}} + \underbrace{E(1-e^{-t/\tau_C})}_{\text{zero-state}}
$$

where $\tau_C = RC$

---

**Charging Equation:** If the capacitor is initially uncharged, then $V_0=0$, which gives

$$
    \boxed{V_C(t) = E \left( 1 - e^{-t/\tau_C} \right)}
$$

This is the **charging equation**.

---

For the resistor, we have

$$
    E = V_R + V_C \implies V_R = E - V_C \implies V_R(t) = (E - V_0) e^{-t/\tau_C}
$$
