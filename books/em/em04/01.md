# Fundamentals of Circuit Analysis

## Electrical Circuit

An **electrical circuit** is an electrical network consisting of a closed loop. An **electrical network** is the model of an interconnection of electrical elements. It can also refer to the actual physical interconnection of electrical component.

An **electric element** is a conceptual abstraction representing an electronic component. An **electronic component** is any basic electrical device used to affect electrons or their associated fields (e.g. battery, resistor, capacitor, inductor, transformer, swicth, transistor, etc.)

- An active element generates electrical energy (e.g. voltage source and current source).
- A passive element uses electrical energy but does not generate it (e.g. resistance, capacitance, inductance).

## Electric Current in Electrical Circuits

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a conductor in electric circuits.

$$
    \boxed{I = \frac{\mathrm d Q}{\mathrm d t}}
$$

The conventional current direction is arbitrarily defined as the direction in which positive charges flow.

## Voltage in Electrical Circuits

Voltage ($\Delta V$ or $V$) is the difference in electric potential between two points in a electrical circuit.

$$
    \boxed{\Delta V = V(\mathbf r_B) - V(\mathbf r_A) = - \int_{\mathbf r_A}^{\mathbf r_B} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell}
$$

## Electromotive Force

Electromotive force ($E$ or $\mathcal E$) is an energy transfer to an electrical circuit per unit of electric charge. It is the total voltage of the electrical circuit.

$$
    \boxed{\mathcal E = - \oint_C \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell}
$$

In an electrical circuit,

$$
    \mathcal E = V_{\mathrm{ter}} + V_{\mathrm{load}}
$$

where $V_{\mathrm{ter}}$ is the terminal voltage (voltage of the power source) and $V_{\mathrm{load}}$ is the load voltage (voltage of the external circuit).

## Electrical Resistance

$$
    \boxed{R = \frac{\mathrm d V}{\mathrm d I}}
$$

**Electrical Resistivity:** Electrical resistivity is the resistance of a conductor of unit length and uniform unit cross-sectional area at a certain temperature.

$$
    \boxed{\rho = \frac{RA}{l}} \implies R = \rho \frac{l}{A}
$$

### Electrical Conductance

Electrical conductance is the reciprocal quantity of electrical resistance. It describes the ease with which an electric current passes.

$$
    G = \frac{1}{R}
$$

**Electrical Conductivity:** Electrical rconductivity is the conductance of a conductor of unit length and uniform unit cross-sectional area at a certain temperature. It is the reciprocal of electrical resistivity.

$$
    \sigma = \frac{1}{\rho}
$$

## Capacitance

Capacitance is the capability of a material object or device to store electric charge.

**Self-capacitance** is the amount of electric charge that must be added to an isolated conductor to raise its electric potential by one unit.

$$
    \boxed{C = \frac{\mathrm d Q}{\mathrm d V}}
$$

where $Q$ is the electric charge and $V$ is the voltage.

**Mutual capacitance** is the mutual effect of multiple electrical components with self-capacitances.

### Capacitances of Common Objects

#### Parallel-Plate Capacitor

$$
    C = \varepsilon \frac{A}{d}
$$

where

- $\varepsilon$ is the permittivity of the dielectric material between the plates
- $A$ is the cross-sectional area of each plate
- $d$ is the separation between the plates

If a dielectric of thickness $t$ partially fills the space between the plates, then

$$
    C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon_r}}
$$

where $\varepsilon_r$ is the relative permittivity (AKA dielectric constant) of the dielectric material.

#### Sphere

$$
    C = 4 \pi \varepsilon R
$$

#### Concentric Spheres

$$
    C = \frac{4 \pi \varepsilon}{\frac{1}{R_I} - \frac{1}{R_O}}
$$

where $R_I$ and $R_O$ is the radii of the inner and outer spheres, respectively.

#### Concentric Cylinders

$$
    C = \frac{2 \pi \varepsilon l}{\ln \frac{R_O}{R_I}}
$$

where $R_I$ and $R_O$ is the radii of the inner and outer cross-sections of the cylinder, respectively, and $l$ is the length of the cylinder.

#### Disc

$$
    C = 8 \varepsilon R
$$

## Inductance

Inductance is the tendency of a conductor to oppose a change in the electric current flowing through it.

$$
    L = \frac{\Phi_B}{I} \implies \boxed{L = \frac{V}{\mathrm d I / \mathrm d t}}
$$

### Inductances of Common Objects

#### Solenoid

$$
    L = \frac{N \Phi_B}{I} = \mu \frac{N^2 A}{l}
$$

where

- $N$ is the number of turns
- $A$ is the cross-sectional area of each turn
- $l$ is the length of the solenoid

## Ohm's Law

The electric current between two points in a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points.

$$
    \boxed{I = \frac{V}{R}}
$$

Where $I$ is the electric current between two points, $V$ is the voltage across the two points, and $R$ is the resistance between the two points due to the conductor.

In general,

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf J_f = \sigma \mathbf E}
$$

where $\mathbf J_f$ is the free current density, $\sigma$ is the electrical conductivity and $\mathbf E$ is the electric field.
