# Section 1

## Electric Current in Matter

In a conductor,

$$
    I = n A Q v_d
$$

where $v_d$ is the drift speed of the charges, $n$ is the number density of the charges and $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the conductor.

In magnetic materials, there are 2 types of currents:

**Bound Current:** Current produced by bound charges.

$$
    I_b = \int_S \mathbf J_b \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_b}{\mathrm d t}
$$

**Free Current:** Current produced by free charges.

$$
    I_f = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_f}{\mathrm d t}
$$

So, total current is

$$
    I = I_f + I_b
$$

## Electric Current Density in Matter

$$
    \mathbf J = \mathbf J_f + \mathbf J_b
$$

$$
    \mathbf = \rho_f \mathbf v_d
$$

$$
    \mathbf J_b = \mathbf J_M + \mathbf J_P = \nabla \times \mathbf M + \dfrac{\partial \mathbf P}{\partial t}
$$

For electrostatics,

$$
    \mathbf J_b = \mathbf J_M = \nabla \times \mathbf M
$$

## Permeability

$$
    \mu_r = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0}
$$

## Magnetic Susceptibility

A constant indicating the degree of magnetization of a magnetic material in response to an electric field.

$$
    \chi_V = \mu_r - 1 = \dfrac{\mu - \mu_0}{\mu_0}
$$

## Magnetization

Expresses the density of magnetic moments in a magnetic material.

It describes bound electric current and is a pseudovector.

$$
    \mathbf = \dfrac{\mathrm d \mathbf m}{\mathrm d V}
$$

For linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf M = \chi_V \mathbf H = \dfrac{\chi_V}{\mu} \mathbf B
$$

## Magnetization Flux

$$
    \Phi_M = \int_S \mathbf \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Magnetization Current Density

$$
    \mathbf J_M = \nabla \times \mathbf M
$$

## Magnetizing Field

An auxiliary field that accounts for the electromagnetic effects of magnetization and that of a magnetic field.

$$
    \mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{\mu_0} \mathbf B - \mathbf M
$$

For linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{(1 + \chi_V) \mu_0} \mathbf B = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \mathbf B
$$

## Magnetizing Field Flux

$$
    \Phi_H = \int_S \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

For linear materials,

$$
    \Phi_H = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \Phi_B
$$

## Magnetizing Field Current Density

$$
    \mathbf J_H = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf H}{\partial t}
$$

For linear materials,

$$
    \mathbf J_H = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf B}{\partial t}
$$

## Magnetizing Field Current

$$
    I_H = \int_S \mathbf J_H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{\partial \Phi_H}{\partial t}
$$

For linear materials,

$$
    I_H = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \dfrac{\partial \Phi_B}{\partial t}
$$

## Magnetic Polarization

$$
    \mathbf I = \mu_0 \mathbf M \implies \mathbf B = \mu_0 \mathbf H + \mathbf I
$$

## Magnetic Field Energy in Matter

$$
    u_m = \dfrac{1}{2} \mathbf B \cdot \mathbf H
$$
