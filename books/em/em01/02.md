# Electric Potential

## Work and Energy in an Electric Field

Work done by moving a point charge in an electric field is

$$
    \boxed{W = q \int_C \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r}
$$

Work done by a point charge in the electric field of another point charge is

$$
    W = \dfrac{qQ}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \left( \dfrac{1}{r_0} - \dfrac{1}{r} \right)
$$

$$
    W_{\infty r} = - \dfrac{qQ}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 r}
$$

### Electric Potential Energy

Electric potential energy is the potential energy of a charge moving in an electric field.

$$
    \boxed{U_e = - W} = - q \int_C \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r
$$

### Electric Field Energy Density

The electric field energy density is

$$
    \boxed{u_e = \dfrac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 \Vert \mathbf E \Vert^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \rho \phi
$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density and $\phi$ is the electric potential.

Potential energy stored in an electric field is

$$
    U = \int_V u_e \, \mathrm d V
$$

## Electric Potential

In electrostatics, electric potential is the electric potential energy per unit charge.

$$
    \phi = \dfrac{U_e}{q} = - \int_C \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{\mathbf E = - \nabla \phi}
$$

The electric potential due to a point charge is called the Coulomb potential, and is given by

$$
    \phi = \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{r}
$$

### Voltage

Voltage is the difference in electric potential between two points.

In electrostatics, it is the work needed per unit of charge to move a positive test charge from the first point to the second point.

$$
    \Delta V \equiv V = \phi(\mathbf r_1) - \phi(\mathbf r_0) = - \int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r_1} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell
$$

## Irrotationality of Electrostatic Fields

$$
    \boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf E = \mathbf 0}
$$

## Poisson's Equation for Electrostatics

$$
    \boxed{\nabla^2 \phi = - \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}}
$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density.

## Laplace's Equation

$$
    \nabla^2 \phi = 0
$$

## Electric Potentials of Common Charge Distributions

### Infinite Wire:

$$
    \phi = - \dfrac{\lambda}{2 \pi \varepsilon_0} \ln x
$$

### Finite Wire

### Infinitely Large Surface:
    
$$
    \phi = - \dfrac{\sigma x}{2 \varepsilon_0}
$$

### Infinitely Long Cylindrical Volume:

$$
    \phi = - \dfrac{\lambda}{2 \pi \varepsilon_0} \ln x
$$

### Spherical Volume:

Assuming total charge is uniformly distributed in the volume.

Outside the sphere:
    
$$
    \phi = \dfrac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 x}
$$

Inside the sphere:

$$
    \phi = \dfrac{Q (3R^2 - r^2)}{8 \pi \varepsilon_0 R^3}
$$

### Spherical Surface:

Assuming total charge is uniformly distributed on the surface.

Outside the sphere:
    
$$
    \phi = \dfrac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 x}
$$
    
Inside the sphere:
    
$$
    \phi = \dfrac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R}
$$

### Charged Ring:

Total charge is uniformly distributed on the ring.

On the axis:

$$
    \phi = \dfrac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 \sqrt{R^2 + x^2}}
$$

### Charged Disc:

On the axis:

$$
    \phi = \dfrac{\sigma}{2 \varepsilon_0} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + R^2} - x^2 \right)
$$

### Electric Dipole:

Potential:
    
$$
    \phi = \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{p \cos \theta}{r^2}
$$
