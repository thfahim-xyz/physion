# Electric Potential

## Electric Potential

In electrostatics, electric potential is defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \boxed{\mathbf E = - \nabla V} \\[0.4em]
    \implies & V = - \int \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell
\end{aligned}
$$

Electric potential is defined relative to a reference point $\mathbf r_0$.

$$
    \Delta V = V(\mathbf r) - V(\mathbf r_0) = - \int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell
$$

We usually take the reference point to be at infinity, where the potential is zero $(V(\infty) = 0)$.

$$
    \boxed{V(\mathbf r) = - \int_{\infty}^{\mathbf r} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell}
$$

**Voltage:** Voltage is the difference in electric potential between two points. In electrostatics, it is the work needed per unit of charge to move a positive test charge from the first point to the second point.

$$
    \Delta V \equiv V = V(\mathbf r_1) - V(\mathbf r_0) = - \int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r_1} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell
$$

### Electric Potential of a Point Charge

The electric potential due to a point charge is called the Coulomb potential, and is given by

$$
    V = \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{Q}{r}
$$

### Electric Potential of Charge Distributions

#### Discrete Charge Distribution

$$
    \boxed{V (\mathbf r) = \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \sum_i \frac{q_i}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r_i |}}
$$

#### Continuous Charge Distribution

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \boxed{\mathrm d V = \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{\mathrm d q}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r' |}} \\[0.4em]
    \implies & V (\mathbf r) = \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \int \frac{\rho (\mathbf r')}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r' |} \mathrm d^3 r'
\end{aligned}
$$

## Irrotationality of Electrostatic Fields

$$
    \boxed{\nabla \times \mathbf E = \mathbf 0}
$$

## Poisson's Equation for Electrostatics

$$
    \boxed{\nabla^2 V = - \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}}
$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density.

### Laplace's Equation

$$
    \nabla^2 V = 0
$$

## Electric Potentials of Common Charge Distributions

### Point Charge

$$
    \boxed{V = \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{r}}
$$

### Solid Sphere

$$
    \boxed{V =
        \begin{cases}
            \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q (3R^2 - r^2)}{2 R^3} & , & r < R \\[2ex]
            \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{r} & , & r \geq R
        \end{cases}}
$$

where $Q$ is the total charge uniformly distributed in the volume.

### Spherical Shell

$$
    \boxed{V =
        \begin{cases}
            \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{R} & , & r < R \\[2ex]
            \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{r} & , & r \geq R
        \end{cases}}
$$

where $Q$ is the total charge uniformly distributed on the surface.

### Infinite Line

$$
    \boxed{V = - \dfrac{\lambda}{2 \pi \varepsilon_0} \ln r}
$$

### Infinite Plane

$$
    \boxed{V = - \dfrac{\sigma}{2 \varepsilon_0} r}
$$

### Charged Ring

$$
    V = \dfrac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}} \qquad \text{(On the axis)}
$$

where $Q$ is the total charge uniformly distributed on the ring.

### Charged Disc

$$
    V = \dfrac{\sigma}{2 \varepsilon_0} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + R^2} - x^2 \right) \qquad \text{(On the axis)}
$$

where $\sigma$ is the uniform surface charge density.

## Electrostatic Energy

### Electric Potential Energy

The potential energy of a charge moving between two points in an electric field is

$$
    U = q \Delta V = - q \int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r
$$

If a charge is brought into the electric field from infinity, then

$$
    \boxed{U_e = q V(\mathbf r)}
$$

### Electric Field Energy Density

The electric field energy density is

$$
    \boxed{u_e = \dfrac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 \Vert \mathbf E \Vert^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \rho V
$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density and $V$ is the electric potential.

Potential energy stored in an electric field is

$$
    U = \int_V u_e \, \mathrm d V
$$
