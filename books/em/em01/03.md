# Electrostatics in Matter

## Electric Charge in Matter

In dielectric materials, the total charge $Q$ is composed of 2 types of charges $(Q = Q_f + Q_b)$:

**Bound Charge:** Bound charge $Q_b$ is the net accumulation of charge from the orientation of the dipoles created by stationary charges in the material. Bound charges cannot be removed from the material and they do not conduct electricity. Both conductors and dielectrics have bound charges.

**Free Charge:** Free charge $Q_f$ is the net excess charge in the material that can move into electrostatic equilibrium. Free charges can move throughout the material and can conduct electricity. Conductors have an abundance of free charges, whereas dielectrics have almost no free charges.

### Charge Density in Matter

**Volume Charge Density:**

$$
    \rho = \rho_f + \rho_b
$$

where

- $\rho_f = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_f}{\mathrm d V} = \nabla \cdot \mathbf D$ is the volume free-charge density

- $\rho_b = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_b}{\mathrm d V} = - \nabla \cdot \mathbf P$ is the volume bound-charge density

**Surface Charge Density:**

$$
    \sigma = \sigma_f + \sigma_b
$$

where

- $\sigma_f = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_f}{\mathrm d S} = \mathbf D \cdot \hat{\mathbf n}$ is the surface free-charge density

- $\sigma_b = \dfrac{\mathrm d Q_b}{\mathrm d S} = \mathbf P \cdot \hat{\mathbf n}$ is the surface bound-charge density

## Conductors

A **conductor** is a material containing mobile charge carriers that can move freely through the material. In electrostatic equilibrium, these charges redistribute until the electric field inside the conducting material vanishes:

$$
    \mathbf E = 0 \implies V = \text{constant}
$$

Thus, the potential is constant throughout the conductor. Any excess charge resides on the conductor's surface. At the surface, the tangential component of the electric field must vanish $(\mathbf E_{\parallel}=0)$, so the electric field just outside the conductor is entirely normal to the surface.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf E = \frac{\sigma_f}{\varepsilon_0} \hat{\mathbf n}}
$$

where $\sigma_f$ is the surface free-charge density.

Conductors can shield their interiors from external electrostatic fields. Cavities within conductors may also contain induced surface charges while maintaining zero electric field in the conducting material.

## Dielectrics

A dielectric (AKA insulator) is an electrically insulating material whose charges are bound rather than freely mobile. An applied electric field slightly displaces positive and negative charges within atoms or molecules, producing electric dipole moments.

## Polarization

Polarization density expresses the volumetric density of electric dipole moments in a dielectric material.

$$
    \mathbf P = \dfrac{\mathrm d \mathbf p}{\mathrm d V} = \rho_b \mathbf d
$$

where $\rho_b$ is the bound charge density and $\mathbf d$ is the separation vector (displacement of the charge due to an applied electric field).

## Electric Displacement Field

Electric displacement field is an auxiliary field that accounts for the electromagnetic effects of polarization and that of an electric field.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf D = \varepsilon \mathbf E + \mathbf P}
$$

In vacuum, there is no polarization, so

$$
    \mathbf D = \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E \qquad \text{(In vacuum)}
$$

## Electric Field Energy in Matter

In matter,

$$
    u_e = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf E \cdot \mathbf D
$$

## Linear Dielectrics

A linear dielectric is a dielectric in which the polarization $\mathbf P$ is linearly proportional to the applied electric field $\mathbf E$.

### Electric Response Quantities

#### Permittivity

The relative permittivity (AKA dielectric constant) of a material is defined as

$$
    \varepsilon_r = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

#### Electric Susceptibility

Electric susceptibility is a constant indicating the degree of polarization of a dielectric material in response to an electric field.

$$
    \chi_e = \varepsilon_r - 1 = \frac{\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

In vacuum, $\chi_e = 0$.

### Polarization in Linear Dielectrics

In linear materials, the polarization density is

$$
    \mathbf P = \chi_e \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E
$$

### Electric Displacement Field in Linear Dielectrics

In linear materials, the electric displacement field is

$$
    \mathbf D = ( 1 + \chi_e ) \mathbf E = \varepsilon \mathbf E
$$

## Capacitors

A capacitor is a device that stores electric energy by establishing an electric field between two conductors separated by an insulating region.

## Capacitance

Capacitance is the capability of a material object or device to store electric charge. It is defined by

$$
    \boxed{C = \frac{\mathrm d Q}{\mathrm d V}}
$$

where $Q$ is the electric charge on either conductor of a capacitor and $V$ is the voltage between them.

Capacitance depends on the geometry of the conductors and the dielectric medium between them, but not on $Q$ or $V$ individually for a linear dielectric.

### Capacitances of Common Objects

#### Parallel-Plate Capacitor

$$
    C = \varepsilon \frac{A}{d}
$$

where

- $\varepsilon$ is the permittivity of the dielectric material between the plates
- $A$ is the cross-sectional area of each plate
- $d$ is the separation between the plates

If a dielectric of thickness $t$ partially fills the space between the plates, then

$$
    C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon_r}}
$$

where $\varepsilon_r$ is the relative permittivity (AKA dielectric constant) of the dielectric material.

#### Sphere

$$
    C = 4 \pi \varepsilon R
$$

#### Concentric Spheres

$$
    C = \frac{4 \pi \varepsilon}{\frac{1}{R_I} - \frac{1}{R_O}}
$$

where $R_I$ and $R_O$ is the radii of the inner and outer spheres, respectively.

#### Concentric Cylinders

$$
    C = \frac{2 \pi \varepsilon l}{\ln \frac{R_O}{R_I}}
$$

where $R_I$ and $R_O$ is the radii of the inner and outer cross-sections of the cylinder, respectively, and $l$ is the length of the cylinder.

#### Disc

$$
    C = 8 \varepsilon R
$$

## Capacitor Energy

The potential energy stored in a charged capacitor is

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & U = \int V \, \mathrm d Q' \\[0.4em]
    \implies & U = \frac{1}{2} C V^2 = \frac{1}{2} QV = \frac{1}{2} \frac{Q^2}{C}
\end{aligned}
$$
