# Light Waves

## Wave Nature of Light

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave: coupled oscillating $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ fields propagate through vacuum without a material medium, with $\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B\perp\mathbf k$.

For a monochromatic plane wave of frequency $f$ and wavelength $\lambda$:

$$
    \boxed{c=f\lambda},\quad \boxed{k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}},\quad \boxed{\omega=2\pi f}
$$

so that $\omega=ck$. General wave quantities $(\Psi,\phi,\mathbf k,\omega,v_p,v_g)$ are already defined in Waves and are not re-derived here.

**Interpretation:** geometrical optics is the $\lambda\to 0$ limit of this wave field; interference, diffraction, and polarization are its finite-$\lambda$ and transverse signatures, treated in the following sections.

**Remark:** wave-particle duality is a quantum statement belonging to Quantum Mechanics; here light is treated classically.

## Electromagnetic Radiation

Electromagnetic radiation is ordered by wavelength (equivalently frequency or photon energy) into a single spectrum with $c=f\lambda$ fixed in vacuum:

| Band | Approximate wavelength range | Canonical source or use |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | $\gt 10^{-1}\ \mathrm m$ | Broadcast, communications |
| Microwave | $10^{-3}$–$10^{-1}\ \mathrm m$ | Radar, heating, CMB |
| Infrared | $7.5\times 10^{-7}$–$10^{-3}\ \mathrm m$ | Thermal emission, remote sensing |
| Visible | $3.8$–$7.5\times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm m$ | Human vision |
| Ultraviolet | $10^{-8}$–$3.8\times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm m$ | Atomic excitation, sterilization |
| X-ray | $10^{-11}$–$10^{-8}\ \mathrm m$ | Inner-shell transitions, imaging |
| Gamma ray | $\lt 10^{-11}\ \mathrm m$ | Nuclear decay, high-energy astrophysics |

The visible window is narrowly:

$$
    \boxed{380\ \mathrm{nm}\lesssim\lambda_{\mathrm{vis}}\lesssim 750\ \mathrm{nm}}
$$

**Interpretation:** optics conventionally covers visible plus neighboring infrared and ultraviolet, where lenses, mirrors, and fibers operate; the wave equation below applies to all bands.

## Electromagnetic Wave Equation

**Result:** in vacuum $(\rho=0,\mathbf J=\mathbf 0)$, Maxwell's equations (already stated in Electrodynamics) imply:

$$
    \boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf E=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}},\quad \boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf B=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial^2\mathbf B}{\partial t^2}}
$$

so the propagation speed is:

$$
    \boxed{c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Take the curl of $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial\mathbf B/\partial t$ and use $\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\nabla^2\mathbf E$ with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0$ in vacuum. Then $\nabla\times\partial\mathbf B/\partial t=\partial(\nabla\times\mathbf B)/\partial t=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial^2\mathbf E/\partial t^2$ by the vacuum Ampère-Maxwell law, giving the $\mathbf E$ equation; the $\mathbf B$ equation follows symmetrically.

</div>

A monochromatic plane-wave solution propagating along $\mathbf{\hat k}$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf E=\mathbf E_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}},\quad \boxed{\mathbf B=\mathbf B_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}}
$$

with $\omega=ck$ and transversality $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf E_0=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B_0=0$. Faraday's law gives $\mathbf k\times\mathbf E_0=\omega\mathbf B_0$, so:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf{\hat k}\times\mathbf E=c\mathbf B},\quad \boxed{E=cB}
$$

and $(\mathbf E,\mathbf B,\mathbf k)$ form a right-handed orthogonal triad with $E/B=c$ in SI.

**Limitation:** in matter $c\to v=c/n$ with dispersion; the vacuum derivation is the reference case.

## Huygens-Fresnel Principle

**Principle:** every point of a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets; the field beyond is the superposition of these wavelets, and the new wavefront is their envelope.

Fresnel's refinement adds amplitude, phase, and obliquity: with incident field $U_0$, aperture $\Sigma$, and inclination factor $K(\chi)$ suppressing backward propagation:

$$
    \boxed{U(P)\propto\iint_{\Sigma}U_0\frac{e^{ikr}}{r}K(\chi)\,\mathrm d S}
$$

where $r$ is the secondary path to $P$ and $\chi$ the angle from the forward normal.

**Consequences:** in free space the envelope reproduces rectilinear propagation and, at interfaces, recovers reflection and Snell's law; at apertures comparable to $\lambda$ the same integral gives diffraction, treated canonically in Interference and Diffraction.

**Limitation:** the scalar form suffices for the canonical results here; full polarization-resolved diffraction requires the vector theory.
