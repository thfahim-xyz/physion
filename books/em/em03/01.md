# Magnetic Field

## Electric Current

Electric current is a flow of charged particles moving through an electrical conductor or space.

$$
    I = \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Current Density

Amount of charge per unit time that flows through a unit area of a chosen cross section.

$$
    \mathbf J = \dfrac{\partial I}{\partial A} 
$$

$$
    \mathbf J = \rho \mathbf v
$$

## Magnetic Moment

There are 2 models that describe magnetic moment.

### Magnetic Pole Model

This model assumes fictitious monopoles that create a magnetic dipole which has magnetic moment. This makes the model analogous to electric dipole.

In this model, the magnetic field is represented by the irrotational $\mathbf H$-field, analogous to the electric field.

The magnetic moment is given by

$$
    \mathbf m = p \mathbf \ell
$$

where $p$ is the monopole charge and $\mathbf \ell$ is the separation vector.

### Ampèrian Loop Model

This model is based on the fact that a loop of electric current produces a magnetic field that is similar to that of a dipole.

In this model, the magnetic field is represented by the solenoidal $\mathbf B$-field, analogous to the electric displacement field.

The magnetic moment is given by

$$
    \mathbf m = I \mathbf S
$$

where $I$ is the electric current and $\mathbf S$ is the directed surface area of the loop.

**Magnetic Moment of a Solenoid:**

A generalization of the current loop model is a solenoid (also known as a coil), whose magnetic moment is the vector sum of the moments of the individual turns (A turn is a single-layer winding).

If the solenoid has $N$ identical turns, then the magnetic moment is

$$
    \mathbf m = NI \mathbf S
$$

## Magnetic Field

Magnetic field is the physical field responsible for magnetic influence on moving electric charges, electric currents, and magnetic materials.

It is a pseudovector field and is produced by electric fields varying in time.

**Examples:**

- Magnetic Field of a Magnet:

    $$
        \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \dfrac{3 (\mathbf m \cdot \hat{\mathbf r}) \hat{\mathbf r} - \mathbf m}{|\mathbf r|^3}
    $$

- Magnetic Field of a Current Loop:

    The loop is taken to be centered at the origin.

    $$
        \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}} \hat{\mathbf x}
    $$

- Magnetic Field of a Moving Point Charge:

    $$
        \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \dfrac{q \mathbf v \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{|\mathbf r|^2}
    $$

## Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux is the measure of magnetic field through a surface.

$$
    \Phi_B = \int_B \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Biot-Savart Law

Describes the magnetic field generated by a steady current in a vacuum.

Along a closed infinitely narrow wire,

$$
    \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \int_C \dfrac{I \, \mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{|\mathbf r|^2}
$$

Throughout conductor volume,

$$
    \mathbf B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \int_V \dfrac{\mathbf J \times \hat{\mathbf r}}{|\mathbf r|^2} \, \mathrm d V
$$

## Magnetic Force

On a point charge,

$$
    \mathbf F_m = q \mathbf v \times \mathbf B
$$

On a current-carrying wire,

$$
    \mathbf F_m = I \int_C \mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \mathbf B
$$

On a magnet (In the current loop model),

$$
    \mathbf F_m = \nabla (\mathbf m \cdot \mathbf B)
$$

where $\mathbf m$ is the magnetic moment.

On a solenoid,

$$
    \mathbf F_m = NI \mathbf \ell \times \mathbf B
$$

## Magnet (+ Torque on a Magnet)

Torque on a magnet (magnetic moment) due to an external magnetic field.

$$
    \boldsymbol \tau = \int_V \mathrm d \mathbf m \times \mathbf B
$$
