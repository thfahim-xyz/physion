# Maxwell's Equations

## Lorentz Force

For a point charge,

$$
    \mathbf F_{em} = \mathbf F_e + \mathbf F_m \implies \mathbf F_{em} = q(\mathbf E + \mathbf v \times \mathbf B)
$$

where $q$ is the charge, $\mathbf E$ is the electric field, $\mathbf v$ is the velocity of the particle and $\mathbf B$ is the magnetic field.

For a continuous charge distribution,

$$
    \mathbf F_{em} = \int_V \rho(\mathbf E + \mathbf v \times \mathbf B) \, \mathrm d V = \int_V (\rho E + \mathbf J \times \mathbf B) \, \mathrm d V
$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density and $\mathbf J$ is the current density.

For a current-carrying wire,

$$
    \mathbf F_{em} = \mathbf F_m = I \int_C \mathrm d \mathbf \ell \times \mathbf B
$$

For a solenoid with $N$ identical turns,

$$
    \mathbf F_{em} = \mathbf F_m = NI \mathbf \ell \times \mathbf B
$$

## Gauss's Law

The electric flux out of an arbitrary closed surface is proportional to the electric charge enclosed by the surface.

$$
    \Phi_E = \dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_0} \int_V \rho \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf E = \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

where $Q$ is the total charge enclosed by the closed surface and $\rho$ is the charge density inside the surface.

## Gauss's Law for Magnetism

The net magnetic flux through a closed surface is zero.

$$
    \Phi_B = 0
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = 0
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf B = 0
$$

## Gauss's Laws in Matter

### Gauss's Law for Electric Displacement Field

$$
    \Phi_D = Q_f
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \int_V \rho_f \, \mathrm d V
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf D = \rho_f
$$

where $Q_f$ is the total free charge enclosed by the surface and $\rho_f$ is the free charge density inside the surface.

## Faraday's Law of Induction

The circulation of electric field around a closed path is equal to the negative of the time rate of change of magnetic flux through a surface enclosed by the path.

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_B}{\mathrm d t}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf E = - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf B}{\partial t}
$$

Alternatively, the law states that the electromotive force around a closed path is equal to the negative of the time rate of change of the magnetic flux enclosed by the path.

$$
    \mathcal E = - \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_B}{\mathrm d t}
$$

For an inductor with identical turns,

$$
    \mathcal E = - N \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_B}{\mathrm d t}
$$

## Maxwell-Faraday Equation

A time-varying magnetic field always accompanies a spatially-varying (and also possibly time-varying) electric field and vice versa.

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_B}{\mathrm d t}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf E = - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf B}{\partial t}
$$

## Lenz's Law

The direction of the electric current induced in a conductor by a changing magnetic field is such that the magnetic field created by the induced current opposes changes in the initial magnetic field.

## Ampère's Circuital Law

The circulation of the magnetic field around a closed curve is proportional to the electric current passing through a surface enclosed by the curve.

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \mu_0 I \quad \text{(For linear materials)}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \mu_0 \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \times \mathbf B = \mu_0 \mathbf J
$$

If we use the magnetizing field, we get an equation for Ampère's circuital law in a conductor.

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = I_f
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \times \mathbf H = \mathbf J_f
$$

Ampère's circuital is valid only in steady state (Electrostatics and Magnetostatics) situations. Specifically when $\frac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t} = \mathbf 0$ 

## Ampère-Maxwell Equation

Relates the circulation of a magnetic field around a closed loop to the electric current passing through the loop and the time rate of change of electric flux. It is a generalization of Ampère's circuital law.

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \mu_0 \left( I + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_E}{\mathrm d t} \right)
$$

$$
    \implies  \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \mu_0 \left( \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S \right)
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \times \mathbf B = \mu_0 \left( \mathbf J + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t} \right)
$$

For magnetizing field,

$$
    \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = I_f + \dfrac{\mathrm d \Phi_D}{\mathrm d t} \quad \text{(For linear materials)}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S + \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \times \mathbf H = \mathbf J_f + \dfrac{\partial \mathbf D}{\partial t}
$$

## Maxwell's Equations

Maxwell's equations describe all classic electromagnetic phenomena.

In the following discussion, $V$ is any volume with a closed boundary $\partial V$ and differential element $\mathrm d V$. $S$ is any surface with a closed boundary curve $\partial S$ and differential element $\mathrm d \mathbf S$. The integral form of the equations relates fields within a region of space to fields on the boundary. The differential forms of the equations are purely local relations between the fields. Both forms are equivalent descriptions of electromagnetic phenomena.

Maxwell's equations have 2 variants:

- Microscopic Equations (Maxwell's Equations in Vacuum)
- Macroscopic Equations (Maxwell's Equations in Matter)

### Microscopic Equations

This variant of equations has universal applicability and describes electromagnetic phenomena at the microscopic (atomic) scale.

| Equation Name | Integral Equations | Differential Equations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gauss's Law | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_0} \int_V \rho \, \mathrm d V$$ | $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf E = \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$ |
| Gauss's Law for Magnetism | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = 0$$ | $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf B = 0$$ |
| Maxwell-Faraday Equation | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$$ | $$\nabla \times \mathbf E = - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf B}{\partial t}$$ |
| Ampère-Maxwell Equation | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \mu_0 \left( \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S \right)$$ | $$\nabla \times \mathbf B = \mu_0 \left( \mathbf J + \varepsilon_0 \dfrac{\partial \mathbf E}{\partial t} \right)$$ |

where:

| Description | Symbol |
| --- | --- |
| Electric field | $\mathbf E$ |
| Magnetic field | $\mathbf B$ |
| Vacuum permittivity | $\varepsilon_0$ |
| Vacuum permeability | $\mu_0$ |
| Total electric charge density inside $V$ | $\rho$ |
| Total current density through $S$ | $\mathbf J$ |
| Total electric charge enclosed in $V$ | $Q = \int_V \rho \, \mathrm d V$ |
| Net electric current through $S$ | $I = \int_S \mathbf J \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$ |
| Net electric flux through $S$ | $\Phi_E = \int_S \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$ |
| Net magnetic flux through $S$ | $\Phi_B = \int_S \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$ |

### Macroscopic Equations

This variant is applicable in practical situations and describes electromagnetic phenomena at the macroscopic (dielectric and magnetic materials) scale.

| Equation Name | Integral Equations | Differential Equations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gauss's Law | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \int_V \rho_f \, \mathrm d V$$ | $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf D = \rho_f$$ |
| Ampère-Maxwell Equation | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf H \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S + \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf D \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$$ | $$\nabla \times \mathbf H = \mathbf J_f + \dfrac{\partial \mathbf D}{\partial t}$$ |
| Gauss's Law for Magnetism | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = 0$$ | $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf B = 0$$ |
| Maxwell-Faraday Equation | $$\displaystyle \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf E \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf \ell = - \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \int_S \mathbf B \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$$ | $$\nabla \times \mathbf E = - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf B}{\partial t}$$ |

where:

| Description | Symbol |
| --- | --- |
| Electric field | $\mathbf E$ |
| Magnetic field | $\mathbf B$ |
| Electric displacement field | $\mathbf D = \varepsilon_0 \mathbf E + \mathbf P$ |
| Magnetizing field | $\mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{\mu_0} \mathbf B - \mathbf M$ |
| Free charge density | $\rho_f$ |
| Free current density | $\mathbf J_f$ |
| Free charge | $Q_f = \int_V \rho_f \, \mathrm d V$ |
| Free current | $I_f = \int_S \mathbf J_f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$ |
| Bound charge density | $\rho_b = - \nabla \cdot \mathbf P$ |
| Bound current density | $\mathbf J_b = \mathbf J_M + \mathbf J_P = \nabla \times \mathbf M + \dfrac{\partial \mathbf P}{\partial t}$ |
| Bound charge | $Q_b = \int_V \rho_b \, \mathrm d V$ |
| Bound current | $I_b = \int_S \mathbf J_b \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S$ |
| Total charge density | $\rho = \rho_f + \rho_b = \rho_f - \nabla \cdot \mathbf P$ |
| Total current density | $\mathbf J = \mathbf J_f + \mathbf J_b = \mathbf J_f + \nabla \times \mathbf M + \dfrac{\partial \mathbf P}{\partial t}$ |
| Total charge | $Q = Q_f + Q_b$ |
| Total current | $I = I_f + I_b$ |

**Material Properties:**

- In homogeneous materials, $\varepsilon$ and $\mu$ are constant.
- In inhomogeneous materials, $\varepsilon$ and $\mu$ can vary.
- In isotropic materials, $\varepsilon$ and $\mu$ are scalers.
- In anisotropic materials, $\varepsilon$ and $\mu$ are tensors.
- In linear materials, the constitutive relations are linear. That is,

    $$
        \mathbf D = \varepsilon \mathbf E, \quad \mathbf H = \dfrac{1}{\mu} \mathbf B
    $$

## Maxwell's Equations in Potential Formulation

In potential formulation, Maxwell's equations are grouped into 2 categories:

- Homogeneous equations
- Inhomogeneous equations

### Homogeneous Equations

These are identically true for any potential.

1. Maxwell-Faraday Equation
2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

### Inhomogeneous Equations

These equations describe the dynamics.

$$
    \nabla^2 \phi + \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t} (\nabla \cdot \mathbf A) = - \dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}
$$

$$
    \left( \nabla^2 \mathbf A - \dfrac{1}{c^2} \dfrac{\partial^2 \mathbf A}{\partial t^2} \right) - \nabla \left( \nabla \cdot \mathbf A + \dfrac{1}{c^2} \dfrac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} \right) = -\mu_0 \mathbf J
$$

The electric and magnetic fields are given by

$$
    \mathbf E = - \nabla \phi - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf A}{\partial t}
$$

$$
    \mathbf B = \nabla \times \mathbf A
$$

where:

| Description | Symbol |
| --- | --- |
| Electric field | $\mathbf E = - \nabla \phi - \dfrac{\partial \mathbf A}{\partial t}$ |
| Magnetic field | $\mathbf B = \nabla \times \mathbf A$ |
| Electric potential | $\phi$ |
| Magnetic vector potential | $\mathbf A$ |
| Speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum | $c = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_0 \mu_0}}$ |
| Total charge density | $\rho$ |
| Total electric current density | $\mathbf J$ |
