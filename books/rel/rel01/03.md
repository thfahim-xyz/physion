# Relativistic Electrodynamics

## Four-Current

**Definition:** the charge-current four-vector is:

$$
    \boxed{J^{\mu}=(c\rho,\mathbf J)}
$$

with $\rho$ the charge density and $\mathbf J$ the current density. Charge conservation is the invariant continuity equation:

$$
    \boxed{\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0}\implies \boxed{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0}
$$

**Interpretation:** a static charge density in one frame mixes into current in another via $J'^{\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\nu}J^{\nu}$; only the combination is covariant. The 3-vector Maxwell sources $(\rho,\mathbf J)$ are defined in Electrodynamics and not re-derived here.

## Electromagnetic Field Tensor

**Result:** the antisymmetric tensor unifying $\mathbf E,\mathbf B$ is, with $F^{0i}=-E^i/c$:

$$
    \boxed{F^{\mu\nu}=
    \begin{bmatrix}
    0 & -E_x/c & -E_y/c & -E_z/c \\
    E_x/c & 0 & -B_z & B_y \\
    E_y/c & B_z & 0 & -B_x \\
    E_z/c & -B_y & B_x & 0
    \end{bmatrix}},\quad \boxed{F^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\nu\mu}}
$$

It transforms as $F'^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\nu}{}_{\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}$, mixing $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ between frames.

The covariant Lorentz force on charge $q$ (completing the pointer in Relativistic Mechanics) is:

$$
    \boxed{K^{\mu}=qF^{\mu\nu}U_{\nu}}
$$

whose spatial part gives $\mathbf F=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ and time part $\gamma\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v/c$.

Invariants include $\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=B^2-E^2/c^2$ and $\tfrac14\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\alpha\beta}=-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B/c$.

## Covariant Maxwell Equations

**Result:** with Lorenz-gauge-independent sources, Maxwell's equations (3-vector forms in Electrodynamics, cited not repeated) become two tensor equations:

$$
    \boxed{\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^{\nu}}\implies \text{Gauss + Ampere-Maxwell}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\partial_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu]}=0}\implies \text{no monopoles + Faraday}
$$

where brackets antisymmetrize. The first contains Gauss ($\nu=0$) and Ampère-Maxwell ($\nu=i$); the second contains $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ and Faraday.

**Interpretation:** covariance is manifest: both sides transform as tensors, so validity in one inertial frame implies validity in all. The 3-vector derivations remain in Electrodynamics.

## Four-Wavevector

**Definition:** the wave four-vector for phase $\phi=k_{\mu}x^{\mu}=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t$ is:

$$
    \boxed{k^{\mu}=\left(\frac{\omega}{c},\mathbf k\right)}
$$

Phase invariance makes $k^{\mu}$ a four-vector, $k'^{\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\nu}k^{\nu}$. For light in vacuum:

$$
    \boxed{k\cdot k=0}\implies \boxed{\omega=ck}
$$

**Interpretation:** Lorentz-transforming $k^{\mu}$ gives the relativistic Doppler shift and aberration already stated in Fundamentals; only the covariant origin is added here.

## Four-Frequency

**Definition:** the time part of the wave four-vector is the (angular) four-frequency:

$$
    \boxed{\frac{\omega}{c}=k^0}
$$

so frequency mixes with wavevector between frames exactly as time mixes with space. For photons $p^{\mu}=\hbar k^{\mu}$, giving $E=\hbar\omega$ and $\mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k$; the quantum dynamics belongs to Quantum Mechanics and is only named here.

## Relativistic Wave Equation

**Result:** with four-potential $A^{\mu}=(\phi/c,\mathbf A)$ and Lorenz gauge $\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0$, Maxwell's equations become decoupled wave equations:

$$
    \boxed{\Box A^{\mu}=\mu_0J^{\mu}},\quad \boxed{\Box=\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}-\nabla^2}
$$

with $F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu}$ identically satisfying the homogeneous equation.

**Derivation:** insert $F^{\mu\nu}$ in terms of $A^{\mu}$ into $\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^{\nu}$; the Lorenz condition cancels mixed derivatives, leaving $\Box$ on each component. In vacuum $J^{\mu}=0$ each component obeys $\Box A^{\mu}=0$ with speed $c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$.

**Limitation:** the Lorenz gauge choice is convenient but not physical; observables in $F^{\mu\nu}$ are gauge-independent.
