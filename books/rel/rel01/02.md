# Relativistic Mechanics

## Four-Position

**Definition:** the position four-vector with $x^0=ct$ is:

$$
    \boxed{x^{\mu}=(ct,\mathbf r)}
$$

Its differential norm reproduces the interval from Fundamentals, $\mathrm ds^2=\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathrm dx^{\mu}\mathrm dx^{\nu}$, invariant under $x'^{\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\nu}x^{\nu}$.

## Four-Velocity

**Result:** differentiating by proper time $\mathrm d\tau=\mathrm dt/\gamma$ (defined in Fundamentals) gives:

$$
    \boxed{U^{\mu}=\frac{\mathrm dx^{\mu}}{\mathrm d\tau}=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)}
$$

with invariant norm:

$$
    \boxed{U\cdot U=\eta_{\mu\nu}U^{\mu}U^{\nu}=c^2}
$$

**Derivation:** $\mathrm dx^{\mu}/\mathrm d\tau=(\mathrm dt/\mathrm d\tau)\,\mathrm dx^{\mu}/\mathrm dt=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$; contraction gives $\gamma^2(c^2-v^2)=c^2$.

## Four-Acceleration

**Definition:** the proper-time derivative of four-velocity is:

$$
    \boxed{A^{\mu}=\frac{\mathrm dU^{\mu}}{\mathrm d\tau}}
$$

Differentiating $U\cdot U=c^2$ gives orthogonality:

$$
    \boxed{U\cdot A=0}
$$

so in the instantaneous rest frame $A^{\mu}=(0,\mathbf a_0)$ with proper acceleration $\mathbf a_0$; in the lab $A^{\mu}$ mixes $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a$ terms.

**Remark:** constant coordinate acceleration is not Lorentz-invariant; constant proper acceleration (hyperbolic motion) is the covariant canonical case.

## Four-Momentum

**Result:** with invariant mass $m$:

$$
    \boxed{p^{\mu}=mU^{\mu}=\left(\frac{E}{c},\mathbf p\right)=(\gamma mc,\gamma m\mathbf v)}
$$

so that:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf p=\gamma m\mathbf v},\quad \boxed{E=\gamma mc^2}
$$

with invariant norm $p\cdot p=m^2c^2$, i.e. $E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4$ (developed below).

**Interpretation:** $m$ is fixed; the velocity dependence sits in $\gamma$, not in a varying mass. The low-velocity limit $\gamma\to 1$ recovers $\mathbf p\to m\mathbf v$.

## Four-Force

**Definition:** the proper-time rate of four-momentum is:

$$
    \boxed{K^{\mu}=\frac{\mathrm dp^{\mu}}{\mathrm d\tau}=\gamma\left(\frac{\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v}{c},\mathbf F\right)}
$$

with lab force $\mathbf F=\mathrm d\mathbf p/\mathrm d t$. Since $p\cdot p$ is constant for fixed $m$:

$$
    \boxed{K\cdot U=0}
$$

so power $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v$ and force mix covariantly; $\mathbf F$ itself is not the spatial part of a four-vector without the $\gamma$ factor.

**Remark:** the electromagnetic form $K^{\mu}=qF^{\mu\nu}U_{\nu}$ belongs to Relativistic Electrodynamics, not here.

## Relativistic Energy

**Result:** the total energy of a free particle is the time component of $p^{\mu}$:

$$
    \boxed{E=\gamma mc^2}
$$

with rest energy $E_0=mc^2$ below. At low velocity $\gamma\approx 1+\tfrac12v^2/c^2$, so $E\approx mc^2+\tfrac12mv^2$.

**Interpretation:** $E/c$ generates time translations as $\mathbf p$ generates space translations; conservation of $p^{\mu}$ unifies energy-momentum conservation in collisions.

## Relativistic Kinetic Energy

**Result:** kinetic energy is total minus rest energy:

$$
    \boxed{K=E-mc^2=(\gamma-1)mc^2}
$$

**Derivation:** work $\int\mathbf F\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf r$ with $\mathbf F=\mathrm d(\gamma m\mathbf v)/\mathrm d t$ integrates to $(\gamma-1)mc^2$. Expansion gives:

$$
    \boxed{K=\tfrac12mv^2+\tfrac38m\frac{v^4}{c^2}+\cdots\implies K\to\tfrac12mv^2\ (v\ll c)}
$$

so Newtonian kinetic energy is the leading term.

## Energy-momentum Relation

**Result:** eliminating $\mathbf v$ between $E=\gamma mc^2$ and $\mathbf p=\gamma m\mathbf v$ gives:

$$
    \boxed{E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4}
$$

For massless particles $(m=0)$:

$$
    \boxed{E=pc}
$$

**Derivation:** $E^2-p^2c^2=\gamma^2m^2c^4(1-v^2/c^2)=m^2c^4$ using $\gamma^{-2}=1-\beta^2$.

**Interpretation:** $(E/c,\mathbf p)$ lies on the mass hyperboloid; photons sit on the light cone $E=pc$ with $\mathbf v=c\mathbf{\hat p}$.

## Mass-energy Equivalence

**Result:** a mass $m$ at rest carries:

$$
    \boxed{E_0=mc^2}
$$

**Interpretation:** rest energy is convertible: nuclear binding, annihilation, and pair production exchange $m$ for kinetic and radiant energy with total $p^{\mu}$ conserved, not $m$ or $E$ separately.

The canonical $1\ \mathrm u\approx 931.5\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2$ sets the nuclear scale; detailed reactions belong to nuclear courses, not here.

**Limitation:** $m$ here is invariant mass; the obsolete relativistic mass $m_{\mathrm{rel}}=\gamma m$ is not used in this text.
