# Differentiation

## Derivative

The derivative $f^{\prime}(x)$ of a function $f(x)$ is defined as

$$
    f^{\prime}(x) = \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d x} f(x) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x + \Delta x) - f(x)}{\Delta x}
$$

provided that the limit exists.

At a point $x = a$, the derivative is

$$
    f^{\prime}(a) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(a + \Delta x) - f(a)}{\Delta x}
$$

Geometrically, the derivative is the slope of the tangent line to the graph at $x = a$. Physically, it is the instantaneous rate of change of $f$ with respect to $x$.

**Differentiability:** If the derivative of a function exists and is finite at a point $x = a$, then the function is differentiable at that point. A function that is differentiable at a point is also continuous at that point.

## Derivatives of Elementary Functions

## Implicit Differentiation

Implicit differentiation is the process of finding the derivative of a variable defined implicitly by an equation relating two or more variables, by differentiating both sides of the equation with respect to the independent variable and applying the chain rule as needed.

For an implicit equation $f(x, y) = 0$, the derivative of $y$ with respect to $x$ is

$$
    \frac{\mathrm d y}{\mathrm d x} = - \frac{\partial_x f}{\partial_y f}
$$

## Logarithmic Differentiation

Logarithmic differentiation is the process of finding the derivative of a function by first taking the natural logarithm of both sides of the equation, then differentiating implicitly, and finally solving for the derivative. It is particularly useful for functions involving variable exponents or complicated products, quotients, and powers.

## Higher-Order Derivatives

Higher-order derivatives are derivatives of derivatives. They describe how the rate of change of a function changes.

**Zeroth Derivative:** Zeroth derivative is usually considered to be the function itself.

**First Derivative:**

$$
    f^{\prime}(x) = \frac{\mathrm d f}{\mathrm d x}
$$

**Second Derivative:**

$$
    f^{\prime \prime}(x) = \frac{\mathrm d^2 f}{\mathrm d x^2} = \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d x} \left( \frac{\mathrm d f}{\mathrm d x} \right)
$$

**$n$th Derivative:**

$$
    f^{(n)}(x) = \frac{\mathrm d^n f}{\mathrm d x^n}
$$


## Differential

A differential is an infinitesimal (or linear) change in a variable or function.

$$
    \mathrm d f(x) = \frac{\mathrm d f(x)}{\mathrm d x} \, \mathrm d x
$$
