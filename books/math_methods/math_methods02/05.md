# Fundamental Theorems of Multivariable Calculus

## Gradient Theorem

The gradient theorem states that the line integral of a gradient field along a curve is equal to the original scalar field at the endpoints of the curve.
	
For a scaler field $f$, the line integral along a curve $C$ with endpoints $a$ and $b$ is

$$
	\int_C \nabla f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r = f(\mathbf b) - f(\mathbf a)
$$

The gradient theorem implies that line integrals through gradient fields are path-independent.
	
The converse of the theorem is also true: If $\mathbf F$ is a path-independent vector field, then $\mathbf F$ is the gradient of some scalar-valued function.

Some corollaries of this theorem are:

- $$ \oint_C \nabla f \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r = 0 $$

## Divergence Theorem

The divergence theorem, also known as Gauss's theorem, states that the flux of a vector field through a closed surface is equal to the volume integral of the divergence over the region enclosed by the surface.
	
Suppose $V$ is a volume in 3D Euclidean space, with boundary $\partial V$. For a vector field $\mathbf F$ in the neighborhood of $V$, the theorem states that

$$
	\iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf F) \, \mathrm dV = \iint_{\partial V} \mathbf F \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S
$$

## Stokes' Theorem

The Stokes' theorem states that the line integral of a vector field over a loop is equal to the surface integral of its curl over the enclosed surface.
	
Suppose $S$ is an open surface in 3D space, with boundary $\partial S$. For a vector field $\mathbf F$ in the neighborhood of $S$, the theorem states that

$$
	\iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf F) \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf S = \oint_{\partial S} \mathbf F \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf l
$$

## Green's Theorem

$$
    \oint_C P \mathrm d x + Q \mathrm d y = \iint_S \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) \, \mathrm d x \, \mathrm d y
$$
