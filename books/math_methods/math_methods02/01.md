# Vectors

## Vector

A vector is an object that has both magnitude and direction. It is a generalization of a single number. Generally, vectors are elements of a vector space.

In introductory physics, vectors can be represented geometrically using Euclidean vectors, which are arrows in space whose length and direction represent the magnitude and direction of a vector. Euclidean vectors and be added together using the **triangle law of vector addition**. They are also translation invariant (i.e. they can be moved freely without changing their direction)

### Components and Basis Vectors

A vector can be represented using a linear combination of components and basis vectors. The components and basis vectors used to represent a vector can change under coordinate transformations, but vectors themselves are abstract geometric objects which do not change under such transformations.

For a vector $\mathbf v$,

$$
    \mathbf v = \sum_i v^i \mathbf e_i
$$

where $v^i$ are the components and $\mathbf e_i$ are the basis vectors of $\mathbf v$

## Vector Operations

### Vector Addition

**Properties:**

- Commutativity: $ \mathbf u + \mathbf v = \mathbf v + \mathbf u $
- Associativity: $ (\mathbf u + \mathbf v) + \mathbf w = \mathbf u + (\mathbf v + \mathbf w) $
- Identity element (zero vector): $ \mathbf v + \mathbf 0 = \mathbf v $
- Inverse elements (additive inverse): $ \mathbf v + (-\mathbf v) = \mathbf 0$

### Scaler Multiplication

**Properties:**

- Commutativity: $ a \mathbf v = \mathbf v a $
- Associativity: $ a(b \mathbf v) = (ab) \mathbf v $
- Identity element (unit scaler): $ 1 \mathbf v = \mathbf v $
- Distributivity over vector addition: $ a(\mathbf u + \mathbf v) = a \mathbf u + a \mathbf v $
- Distributivity over scaler addition: $ (a + b) \mathbf v = a \mathbf v + b \mathbf v $
- $ 0 \mathbf v = \mathbf 0 $
- $ -1 \mathbf v = - \mathbf v $

### Dot Product

$$
    \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = \Vert \mathbf u \Vert \Vert \mathbf v \Vert \cos \theta
$$

**Properties:**

- Bilinearity: $ \mathbf u \cdot (a \mathbf v + b \mathbf w) = a \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v + b \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf w $
- Symmetry: $ \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = \mathbf v \cdot \mathbf u $

Dot product can be used to define the orthogonality, magnitude and projection of vectors.

**Magnitude:**

$$
    \Vert \mathbf v \Vert = \sqrt{\mathbf v \cdot \mathbf v} = v
$$

**Angle:**

$$
    \angle (\mathbf u, \mathbf v) = \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v}{|\mathbf u||\mathbf v|} \right)
$$

**Normalization:**

$$
    \hat{\mathbf v} = \frac{\mathbf v}{\Vert \mathbf v \Vert} = \frac{\mathbf v}{\sqrt{\mathbf v \cdot \mathbf v}} \implies \mathbf v = \Vert \mathbf v \Vert \hat{\mathbf v}
$$

**Projection:**

The scaler projection of $\mathbf v$ on $\mathbf u$ is

$$
    \left| \mathrm{proj}_{\mathbf u} \mathbf v \right| = \mathbf v \cdot \hat{\mathbf u} = \frac{\mathbf v \cdot \mathbf u}{|\mathbf u|^2}
$$

### Cross Product

$$
    \mathbf u \times \mathbf v = \Vert \mathbf u \Vert \Vert \mathbf v \Vert \sin (\theta) \hat{\mathbf n}
$$

where $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is the unit normal vector, determined by the **right-hand rule**.

**Properties:**

- Bilinearity: $ \mathbf u \times (a \mathbf v + b \mathbf w) = a \mathbf u \times \mathbf v + b \mathbf u \times \mathbf w $
- Antisymmetry: $ \mathbf u \times \mathbf v = - \mathbf v \times \mathbf u $
- $ \mathbf v \times \mathbf v = \mathbf 0 $
- $ \mathbf v \times \mathbf 0 = \mathbf 0 \times \mathbf v = \mathbf 0 $
- Jacobi Identity: $ \mathbf u \times (\mathbf v \times \mathbf w) + \mathbf v \times (\mathbf w \times \mathbf u) + \mathbf w \times (\mathbf u \times \mathbf v) = \mathbf 0 $

The area of the paralellogram formed by $\mathbf u$ and $\mathbf v$ is
$$
    A( \mathbf u, \mathbf v ) = |\mathbf u \times \mathbf v|
$$

The unit normal vector to both $\mathbf u$ and $\mathbf v$ is given by

$$
    \hat{\mathbf n} = \frac{\mathbf u \times \mathbf v}{\Vert \mathbf u \times \mathbf v \Vert}
$$

The dot and cross products are related by

$$
    \Vert \mathbf u \times \mathbf v \Vert^2 = \Vert \mathbf u \Vert^2 \Vert \mathbf v \Vert^2 - (\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v)^2
$$

$$
    \Vert \mathbf u \times \mathbf v \Vert = (\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v) \tan \theta
$$

## Scaler Triple Product

$$
    \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v \times \mathbf w) = 
    \left|
    \begin{matrix}
        u_x & u_y & u_z \\
        v_x & v_y & v_z \\
        w_x & w_y & w_z
    \end{matrix}
    \right|
$$

Properties:

- Invariance under cyclic permutation: $ \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v \times \mathbf w) = \mathbf w \cdot (\mathbf u \times \mathbf v) = \mathbf v \cdot (\mathbf w \times \mathbf u)$

- Operation swap: $ \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v \times \mathbf w) = (\mathbf u \times \mathbf v) \cdot \mathbf w$

Volume of the parallelopiped formed by $\mathbf u$, $\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf w$ is

$$
    V(\mathbf u, \mathbf v, \mathbf w) = \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v \times \mathbf w)
$$

$\mathbf u$, $\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf w$ are coplanar if their scaler triple product is zero.

## Vector Triple Product

$$
    \mathbf a \times (\mathbf b \times \mathbf c) = \mathbf b (\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf c) - \mathbf c (\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b)
$$

## Coordinate Systems

### Cartesian Coordinate System

Coordinates: $ (x, y, z) $

| Elements | Equations |
|-------------|----------------|
| Position vector    | $$\mathbf r = \mathbf r (x, y, z)$$ |
| Unit basis vectors | $$\hat{\mathbf x} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial x}, \quad \hat{\mathbf y} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial y}, \quad \hat{\mathbf z} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial z}$$ |
| Differential line element | $$\mathrm d \mathbf r = \mathrm d x \, \hat{\mathbf x} + \mathrm d y \, \hat{\mathbf y} + \mathrm d z \, \hat{\mathbf z}$$ |
| Differential surface area elements | $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_x = \mathrm d y \, \mathrm d z \, \hat{\mathbf x},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_y = \mathrm d z \, \mathrm d x \, \hat{\mathbf y},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_z = \mathrm d x \, \mathrm d y \, \hat{\mathbf z}$$ |
| Differential volume element | $$\mathrm d V = \mathrm d x \, \mathrm d y \, \mathrm d z$$ |

### Cylindrical Coordinate System

Coordinates: $ (\rho, \theta, z) $

- $ \rho $ is the radial distance or radius $ (r \geq 0) $
- $ \theta $ is the azimuth $ (0 \leq \theta \leq 2 \pi) $
- $ z $ is the axial coordinate $ (-\infty \leq z \leq \infty) $

The convertion from Cartesian to cylindrical coordinates is done using the following equations:

$$
    \rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}
$$

$$
    \varphi = \arctan \left( \frac{y}{x} \right)
$$

$$
    z = z
$$

| Elements | Equations |
|-------------|----------------|
| Position vector    | $$\mathbf r = \mathbf r (\rho, \theta, z)$$ |
| Unit basis vectors | $$\hat{\boldsymbol \rho} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial \rho}, \quad \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \dfrac{1}{r} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial \theta}, \quad \hat{\mathbf z} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial z}$$ |
| Differential line element | $$\mathrm d \mathbf r = \mathrm d \rho \, \hat{\boldsymbol \rho} + r \, \mathrm d \theta \, \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + \mathrm d z \, \hat{\mathbf z}$$ |
| Differential surface area elements | $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_{\rho} = \rho \, \mathrm d \varphi \, \mathrm d z \, \hat{\boldsymbol \rho},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_{\varphi} = \mathrm d \rho \, \mathrm d z \, \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_z = \rho \, \mathrm d \rho \, \mathrm d \varphi \, \hat{\mathbf z}$$ |
| Differential volume element | $$\mathrm d V = \rho \, \mathrm d \rho \, \mathrm d \theta \, \mathrm d z$$ |


### Spherical Coordinate System

Coordinates: $ (r, \theta, \varphi) $

- $ r $ is the radial distance or radius $ (r \geq 0) $
- $ \theta $ is the polar angle $ (0 \leq \theta \leq \pi) $
- $ \varphi $ is the azimuthal angle $ (0 \leq z < 2 \pi) $

The convertion from Cartesian to spherical coordinates is done using the following equations:

$$
    r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}
$$

$$
    \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{z}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}} \right)
$$

$$
    \varphi = \arctan \left( \frac{y}{x} \right)
$$

| Elements | Equations |
|-------------|----------------|
| Position vector    | $$\mathbf r = \mathbf r (r, \theta, \varphi)$$ |
| Unit basis vectors | $$\hat{\mathbf r} = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial r}, \quad \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \dfrac{1}{r} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial \theta}, \quad \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi} = \dfrac{1}{r \sin \theta} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial \varphi}$$ |
| Differential line element | $$\mathrm d \mathbf r = \mathrm d r \, \hat{\mathbf r} + r \, \mathrm d \theta \, \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + r \sin \theta \, \mathrm d \varphi \, \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi}$$ |
| Differential surface area elements | $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_r = r^2 \sin \theta \, \mathrm d \theta \, \mathrm d \varphi \, \hat{\mathbf r},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_{\theta} = r \sin \theta \, \mathrm d r \, \mathrm d \varphi \, \hat{\boldsymbol \theta},$$ <br> $$\mathrm d \mathbf S_{\varphi} = r \, \mathrm d r \, \mathrm d \theta \, \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi}$$ |
| Differential volume element | $$\mathrm d V = r^2 \sin \theta \, \mathrm d r \, \mathrm d \theta \, \mathrm d \varphi$$ |

### General Orthogonal Coordinate System

Coordinates: $ (q_1, q_2, q_3) $

| Elements                           | Equations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Position vector                    | $$\mathbf r = \mathbf r (q_1, q_2, q_3)$$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Coordinate basis vectors           | $$\boxed{\mathbf e_i = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_i}} \implies \mathbf e_1 = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_1}, \quad \mathbf e_2 = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_2}, \quad \mathbf e_3 = \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_3}$$                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Scale factors                      | $$\boxed{h_i = \Vert \mathbf e_i \Vert = \left\Vert \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_i} \right\Vert} \implies h_1 = \left\Vert \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_1} \right\Vert , \quad h_2 = \left\Vert \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_2} \right\Vert , \quad h_3 = \left\Vert \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_3} \right\Vert$$                                                                                                                          |
| Unit basis vectors                 | $$\boxed{\hat{\mathbf e}_i = \dfrac{\mathbf e_i}{h_i} = \dfrac{1}{h_i} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_i}} \implies \hat{\mathbf e}_1 = \dfrac{1}{h_1} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_1}, \quad \hat{\mathbf e}_2 = \dfrac{1}{h_2} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_2}, \quad \hat{\mathbf e}_3 = \dfrac{1}{h_3} \dfrac{\partial \mathbf r}{\partial q_3}$$                                                                                                   |
| Differential line element          | $$\boxed{\mathrm d \mathbf r = \sum_i \mathbf e_i \, \mathrm d q_i} \implies \mathrm d \mathbf r = h_1 \, \mathrm d q_1 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_1 + h_2 \, \mathrm d q_2 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_2 + h_3 \, \mathrm d q_3 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_3$$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Differential arc length            | $$\boxed{\mathrm d s^2 = \Vert \mathrm d \mathbf r \Vert^2} \implies \mathrm d s^2 = h_1^2 \, \mathrm d q_1^2 + h_2^2 \, \mathrm d q_2^2 + h_3^2 \, \mathrm d q_3^2$$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Differential surface area elements | $$\boxed{\mathrm d \mathbf S_i = (\mathbf e_j \times \mathbf e_k) \, \mathrm d q_j \, \mathrm d q_k = h_j \, h_k \, \mathrm d q_j \, \mathrm d q_k \, \hat{\mathbf e}_i} \implies \mathrm d \mathbf S_1 = h_2 \, h_3 \, \mathrm d q_2 \, \mathrm d q_3 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_1, \quad \mathrm d \mathbf S_2 = h_3 \, h_1 \, \mathrm d q_3 \, \mathrm d q_1 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_2, \quad \mathrm d \mathbf S_3 = h_1 \, h_2 \, \mathrm d q_1 \, \mathrm d q_2 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_3$$ |
| Differential volume element        | $$\boxed{\mathrm d V = \lvert (\mathbf e_1 \times \mathbf e_2) \cdot \mathbf e_3 \rvert \, \mathrm d q_1 \, \mathrm d q_2 \, \mathrm d q_3} \implies \mathrm d V = h_1 \, h_2 \, h_3 \, \mathrm d q_1 \, \mathrm d q_2 \, \mathrm d q_3$$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## Vectors in Orthogonal Coordinate Systems

For a general vector $\mathbf v$, the following equations hold for any orthogonal coordinate system (e.g. Cartesian, cylindrical, spherical, etc.) if the unit basis vectors are used.

Vector representation:

$$
    \mathbf v = v_1 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_1 + v_2 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_2 + v_3 \, \hat{\mathbf e}_3
$$

Magnitude:

$$
    \Vert \mathbf v \Vert = \sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2}
$$

Dot product:

$$
    \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + u_3 v_3
$$

Cross product:

$$
    \mathbf u \times \mathbf v = 
    \left|
    \begin{matrix}
        \hat{\mathbf e}_1 & \hat{\mathbf e}_2 & \hat{\mathbf e}_3 \\
        u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\
        v_1 & v_2 & v_3
    \end{matrix}
    \right|
    = (u_2 v_3 - u_3 v_2) \, \hat{\mathbf e}_1 + (u_3 v_1 - u_1 v_3) \, \hat{\mathbf e}_2 + (u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1) \, \hat{\mathbf e}_3
$$
