# Tensors

## Tensor

A **tensor** is a mathematical object that generalizes scalars and vectors to represent multilinear relationships between geometric quantities. Unlike ordinary arrays of numbers, a tensor is defined independently of any coordinate system. Its components may change under a change of coordinates, but the tensor itself remains the same geometric object.

The **rank** (or **order**) of a tensor is the number of indices required to specify its components. A rank-$0$ tensor is a scalar, a rank-$1$ tensor is a vector, and higher-rank tensors describe increasingly complex relationships between vectors, covectors, and other tensors.

### Tensor Components

In a coordinate basis, a tensor is represented by a collection of components with upper (contravariant) and lower (covariant) indices. A tensor of type $(r, s)$ possesses $r$ contravariant indices and $s$ covariant indices, and is written as

$$
    T^{i_1\cdots i_r}_{j_1\cdots j_s}
$$

The integers $r$ and $s$ specify the tensor's type, while its rank is $r + s$.

### Coordinate Transformations

A tensor is characterized by its transformation law. Under a change of coordinates $x^i \mapsto x^{i'}$, the components of a tensor transform according to

$$
    \boxed{T^{i'_1 \cdots i'_r}_{j'_1 \cdots j'_s} = \left( \frac{\partial x^{i'_1}}{\partial x^{i_1}} \cdots \frac{\partial x^{i'_r}}{\partial x^{i_r}} \right)
                                            \left( \frac{\partial x^{j_1}}{\partial x^{j'_1}} \cdots \frac{\partial x^{j_s}}{\partial x^{j'_s}} \right)
                                            T^{i_1\cdots i_r}_{j_1\cdots j_s}}
$$

Contravariant indices transform with the Jacobian of the coordinate transformation, while covariant indices transform with it the inverse Jacobian.

**Scalers:** Scalers remain unchanged under a coordinate transformation.

$$
    \phi^{\prime} = \phi
$$

**Contravariant Vectors:** A contravariant vector has one upper index.

$$
    A^{i'} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^j} A^j
$$

**Covariant Vectors:** A covariant vector has one lower index.

$$
    A_{i'} = \frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{i'}} A_j
$$

**Contravariant Second Rank Tensors:**

$$
    T^{i'j'} = \frac{\partial x^{i'}}{\partial x^i} \frac{\partial x^{j'}}{\partial x^j} T^{ij}
$$

**Covariant Second Rank Tensors:**

$$
    T_{i'j'} = \frac{\partial x^i}{\partial x^{i'}} \frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{j'}} T_{ij}
$$

### Tensor Symmetry

A tensor is **symmetric** with respect to a pair of indices if exchanging those indices leaves the tensor unchanged.

$$
    T_{ij} = T_{ji},
$$

A tensor is **antisymmetric** with respect to a pair of indices if exchanging those indices changes the sign of the tensor.

$$
    T_{ij} = - T_{ji}.
$$

### Tensor Operations

Several algebraic operations can be performed on tensors:

- **Tensor addition** is defined only for tensors of the same type.
- **Scalar multiplication** multiplies every component by a scalar.
- **Tensor product** combines two tensors into a tensor whose rank is the sum of their ranks.
- **Tensor contraction** pairs one contravariant and one covariant index, reducing the rank by two.

These operations preserve the tensor nature of the resulting objects.

## Index Notation

### Einstein Summation Convention

According to this convention, when an index variable appears twice in a single term and is not otherwise defined, it implies summation of that term over all the values of the index.

$$
    x = \sum_i a^i b_i \quad \mapsto \quad x = a^i b_i
$$

### Tensor Index Manipulation

On a space equipped with a metric tensor $g_{ij}$, tensor indices may be raised and lowered using

$$
    A_i = g_{ij}A^j, \quad A^i = g^{ij}A_j
$$

where $g^{ij}$ is the inverse metric satisfying

$$
    g^{ik} g_{kj} = \delta^i_j.
$$

This establishes a correspondence between contravariant and covariant components while preserving the underlying tensor.

## Special Tensors

### Kronecker Delta

$$
    \boxed{
        \delta_{ij} = 
        \begin{cases} 
            0 & \text{if } i \neq j \\
            1 & \text{if } i = j 
        \end{cases}
    }
$$

Kronecker delta represents the identity matrix. It also represents the metric in Cartesian coordinates. In an orthonormal basis, Kronecker delta is defined by

$$
    \hat{\mathbf e}_i \cdot \hat{\mathbf e}_j = \delta_{ij}
$$

- $$ \delta_{ij} A_i = A_j $$

- $$ \delta_{ij} = \delta_{ji} $$

- $$ \delta_{ii} = n \qquad \text{(For n-dimensional space)} $$

- $$ \delta_{ij} \delta_{jk} = \delta_{ik} $$

### Levi-Civita Tensor

$$
    \boxed{
        \varepsilon_{ijk} = 
        \begin{cases} 
            +1 & \text{for even permutation of } (1, 2, 3) \\
            -1 & \text{for odd permutation of } (1, 2, 3) \\
            0 & \text{for repeated indices} 
        \end{cases}
    }
$$

In an orthonormal basis, Levi-Civita symbol is defined by

$$
    (\hat{\mathbf e}_i \times \hat{\mathbf e}_j) = \varepsilon_{ijk} \hat{\mathbf e}_k 
    \implies \varepsilon_{ijk} = (\hat{\mathbf e}_i \times \hat{\mathbf e}_j) \cdot \hat{\mathbf e}_k
$$

- $$ \boxed{\varepsilon_{ijk} = \varepsilon_{jik}} \\ \implies \varepsilon_{ijk} = \varepsilon_{jik} \\ \implies \varepsilon_{iik} = 0 $$

- The product of two Levi-Civita symbols is

    $$
        \varepsilon_{ijk} \varepsilon_{lmn} = 
        \left|
            \begin{matrix}
                \delta_{il} & \delta_{im} & \delta_{in} \\
                \delta_{jl} & \delta_{jm} & \delta_{jn} \\
                \delta_{kl} & \delta_{km} & \delta_{kn}
            \end{matrix}
        \right|
    $$
    
    $$
        \implies \boxed{\varepsilon_{ijk} \varepsilon_{imn} = \delta_{jm} \delta_{kn} - \delta_{jn} \delta_{km}}
    $$

    $$
        \implies \varepsilon_{ijk} \varepsilon_{mjk} = 2 \delta_{im}
    $$

- $$ \boxed{\varepsilon_{ijk} \delta_{jk} = 0} $$

## Tensor Field

A **tensor field** assigns a tensor to every point of a manifold.

Scalar fields, vector fields, covector fields, metric tensors, and curvature tensors are all examples of tensor fields. Tensor fields provide a coordinate-independent language for describing physical laws and geometric structures.
