# Manifolds

## Manifold

A manifold is a space that locally resembles Euclidean space near each point (but does not necessarily resemble Euclidean space globally). Although its global structure may be curved or topologically complex, every point possesses a neighborhood that behaves like an open subset of Euclidean space.

An **$n$-dimensional manifold** is a space in which every point has a neighborhood that can be put into one-to-one correspondence with an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$. The integer $n$ is called the **dimension** of the manifold. While the manifold itself need not be embedded in any higher-dimensional space, it can always be described locally by $n$ independent coordinates.

### Local Coordinates

A **coordinate chart** is a one-to-one mapping $\phi : U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$, where $U$ is an open subset of the manifold.

The coordinates assigned by a chart provide a local description of points on the manifold. The collection of charts that covers the manifold is called an **atlas**. Whenever two charts overlap, their coordinate systems must be related by smooth, invertible **transition maps**.

**Intrinsic Geometry:** The defining properties of a manifold depend only on the manifold itself and not on how it may be represented or embedded in another space. Quantities defined intrinsically remain independent of the choice of coordinates, ensuring that geometric and physical laws have the same meaning in every coordinate system.

### Smooth Manifolds

A manifold is called **smooth** (or differentiable) if all transition maps between overlapping coordinate charts are infinitely differentiable. This smooth structure allows the tools of calculus to be applied on curved spaces in exactly the same way they are applied in Euclidean space.

### Maps Between Manifolds

A **smooth map** $F : M \rightarrow N$ is a function between manifolds whose coordinate representations are smooth in every pair of compatible charts. Smooth maps preserve the differentiable structure of manifolds.

### Importance of Manifolds

Manifolds provide the mathematical framework for describing spaces that are locally Euclidean but globally more general. They form the foundation of modern differential geometry and are indispensable in classical mechanics, electromagnetism, fluid dynamics, continuum mechanics, gauge theory, and general relativity. Subsequent concepts such as tangent spaces, metrics, covariant derivatives, geodesics, and curvature are all naturally defined on manifolds.
