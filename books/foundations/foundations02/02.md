# Units and Dimensional Analysis

## Units

A unit is a particular physical quantity, defined and adopted by convention, with which other quantities of the same kind are compared in order to express their values.

The result $q=\{q\}\cdot[q]$ from Physical Quantities separates a measured quantity into a pure number $\{q\}$ and a reference $[q]$. Change of unit changes $\{q\}$ but not $q$ itself.

$$
    \boxed{q=\{q\}_1[q]_1=\{q\}_2[q]_2}\implies \{q\}_2=\{q\}_1\frac{[q]_1}{[q]_2}
$$

A system of units selects a small set of independent base quantities and defines all other units from them by physical laws and fixed numerical conventions.

### SI Base Units

The International System of Units (SI) uses seven base quantities. Their present definitions fix the numerical values of selected fundamental constants.

| Base quantity | Symbol | SI base unit | Defining constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | $l$ | metre, $\mathrm m$ | speed of light $c$ |
| Mass | $m$ | kilogram, $\mathrm{kg}$ | Planck constant $h$ |
| Time | $t$ | second, $\mathrm s$ | caesium frequency $\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}$ |
| Electric current | $I$ | ampere, $\mathrm A$ | elementary charge $e$ |
| Thermodynamic temperature | $T$ | kelvin, $\mathrm K$ | Boltzmann constant $k_B$ |
| Amount of substance | $n$ | mole, $\mathrm{mol}$ | Avogadro constant $N_A$ |
| Luminous intensity | $I_v$ | candela, $\mathrm{cd}$ | luminous efficacy $K_{cd}$ |

Thus all SI units are ultimately traceable to fixed constants, not to material artefacts.

### Derived Units

A derived unit is formed algebraically from base units through a defining relation. For example, Newton's second law defines force dimensionally through mass and acceleration:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F=m\mathbf a}\implies [\mathbf F]=[m][\mathbf a]
$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{1\ \mathrm N=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}}
$$

Important coherent SI derived units include:

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm J=1\ \mathrm{N\,m}=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm W=1\ \mathrm{J\,s^{-1}}=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm{Pa}=1\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm C=1\ \mathrm{A\,s}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm V=1\ \mathrm{J\,C^{-1}}=1\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,A^{-1}}}
$$

Coherence means no extra numerical factor enters when base SI units are used.

### Unit Conversion and Prefixes

Conversion between units of the same kind is multiplication by an exact ratio. For example:

$$
    \boxed{1\ \mathrm{eV}=1.602176634\times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm J}
$$

SI decimal multiples use standard prefixes:

$$\mathrm k=10^3,\quad \mathrm M=10^6,\quad \mathrm G=10^9,\quad \mathrm m=10^{-3},\quad \mu=10^{-6},\quad \mathrm n=10^{-9}$$

Hence:

$$1\ \mathrm{km}=10^3\ \mathrm m,\quad 1\ \mathrm{mm}=10^{-3}\ \mathrm m,\quad 1\ \mathrm{ns}=10^{-9}\ \mathrm s$$

Only quantities of the same kind can be added or equated; conversion never changes the dimension.

## Dimensions

The dimension of a quantity is its dependence on the chosen base quantities, ignoring the particular choice of units.

Dimensions are denoted by square brackets. In mechanics the principal dimensions are mass $\mathsf M$, length $\mathsf L$, and time $\mathsf T$:

$$
    \boxed{[q]=\mathsf M^a\mathsf L^b\mathsf T^c}
$$

where $a,b,c$ are rational exponents. Thus:

$$[v]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-1},\quad [a]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2},\quad [F]=\mathsf M\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}$$

$$[E]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-2},\quad [P]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-2}$$

A dimensionless quantity has $a=b=c=0$, so $[q]=1$. Angles, ratios, counts, strain, refractive index, and relative errors are dimensionless.

$$
    \boxed{[\theta]=1,\quad [\varepsilon]=1,\quad [n_{\mathrm{refr}}]=1}
$$

Dimensionless does not mean unitless in practice: radian and steradian mark the kind of dimensionless quantity.

### Principle of Dimensional Homogeneity

Every physically meaningful equation must be dimensionally homogeneous: all additive terms must have the same dimension.

$$
    \boxed{A=B+C\implies [A]=[B]=[C]}
$$

In particular, arguments of exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, and other transcendental functions must be dimensionless:

$$
    \boxed{[x]=1\ \text{for}\ e^x,\ \ln x,\ \sin x,\ \cos x}
$$

This provides a rapid consistency check. For the constant-acceleration relation:

$$x=x_0+v_0t+\frac12 at^2$$

$$[x_0]=\mathsf L,\quad [v_0t]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-1}\cdot\mathsf T=\mathsf L,\quad [at^2]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}\cdot\mathsf T^2=\mathsf L$$

$$
    \implies [x]=[x_0]=[v_0t]=[at^2]=\mathsf L
$$

Hence the equation is dimensionally consistent. Dimensional consistency is necessary but not sufficient: a wrong dimensionless prefactor cannot be detected dimensionally.

Dimensional analysis also constrains unit conversion of derived quantities. If length is rescaled by $\lambda_L$, mass by $\lambda_M$, and time by $\lambda_T$, then:

$$F\propto \mathsf M\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}\implies F\to \lambda_M\lambda_L\lambda_T^{-2}F$$

## Dimensional Derivation of Relations

State the assumed dependence first, then determine exponents by homogeneity.

Suppose the period $\tau$ of a simple pendulum depends only on its length $l$, gravitational acceleration $g$, and mass $m$:

$$\tau\propto l^\alpha g^\beta m^\gamma$$

Dimensions give:

$$[\tau]=\mathsf T,\quad [l]=\mathsf L,\quad [g]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2},\quad [m]=\mathsf M$$

$$
    \implies \mathsf T=(\mathsf L)^\alpha(\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2})^\beta(\mathsf M)^\gamma
$$

$$
    \implies \mathsf T=\mathsf M^\gamma\mathsf L^{\alpha+\beta}\mathsf T^{-2\beta}
$$

Equating exponents:

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathsf M&:\quad \gamma=0\\
\mathsf T&:\quad -2\beta=1\\
\mathsf L&:\quad \alpha+\beta=0
\end{aligned}$$

$$
    \implies \beta=-\frac12,\quad \alpha=\frac12,\quad \gamma=0
$$

$$
    \boxed{\tau\propto\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}}
$$

Thus the mass drops out, while the dimensionless prefactor remains undetermined. Full dynamics gives $\tau=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ for small amplitude. The limitation is explicit: dimensional analysis fixes the power law, not $2\pi$.

Similarly, for wave speed $v$ on a string of tension $F$ and linear mass density $\mu$:

$$v\propto F^\alpha\mu^\beta$$

$$[v]=\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-1},\quad [F]=\mathsf M\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2},\quad [\mu]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}$$

$$
    \implies \mathsf L\mathsf T^{-1}=(\mathsf M\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2})^\alpha(\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1})^\beta
$$

$$
    \implies \mathsf L\mathsf T^{-1}=\mathsf M^{\alpha+\beta}\mathsf L^{\alpha-\beta}\mathsf T^{-2\alpha}
$$

$$\implies \alpha+\beta=0,\quad -2\alpha=-1,\quad \alpha-\beta=1$$

$$
    \implies \boxed{v\propto\sqrt{\frac{F}{\mu}}}
$$

Again the exact dimensionless constant requires the equation of motion; here it is unity, $v=\sqrt{F/\mu}$.

## Buckingham Pi Theorem

When many variables enter a problem, dimensional homogeneity reduces them to fewer dimensionless groups.

If a relation involves $n$ dimensional variables built from $k$ independent base dimensions, it can be rewritten as a relation among $n-k$ independent dimensionless products $\Pi_i$:

$$
    \boxed{f(q_1,\ldots,q_n)=0\implies F(\Pi_1,\ldots,\Pi_{n-k})=0}
$$

Each group satisfies:

$$
    \boxed{\Pi_i=\prod_{j=1}^n q_j^{a_{ij}},\quad [\Pi_i]=1}
$$

For pendulum period with variables $(\tau,l,g,m)$, $n=4$ and $k=3$ ($\mathsf M,\mathsf L,\mathsf T$), so only one independent dimensionless group exists:

$$
    \boxed{\Pi=\tau\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}}}
$$

$$
    \implies \tau\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}}=\text{constant}
$$

which reproduces the square-root law. The theorem organizes experiments and scaling laws but does not supply the unknown function $F$.

## Limitations and Remarks

Dimensional analysis cannot determine dimensionless constants, distinguish quantities with identical dimensions, or supply missing variables.

Energy, torque, and heat share $\mathsf M\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-2}$, yet torque is not energy. Frequency and angular velocity share $\mathsf T^{-1}$ in ordinary treatment, although radian bookkeeping distinguishes them when needed.

Dimensionless arguments are required for series-defined functions:

$$e^x=1+x+\frac{x^2}{2!}+\cdots$$

can be homogeneous only if $[x]=1$, since $1,x,x^2,\ldots$ are added together.

Natural units set selected constants to unity, for example $c=\hbar=1$. This simplifies equations but hides dimensions; restoring them requires tracking powers of $c$ and $\hbar$ through $[E]=[m]c^2$ and $[E][t]=[\hbar]$.
