## Conservation of Momentum

### Conservation of Linear Momentum

Total linear momentum of an isolated system is conserved.

$$
    \boxed{\sum \mathbf p = \text{constant}} \qquad \text{(For an isolated system)}
$$

This law emerges from the translational symmetry of the system.

### Conservation of Angular Momentum

Total angular momentum of an isolated system is conserved.

$$
    \boxed{\sum \mathbf L = \text{constant}} \qquad \text{(For an isolated system)}
$$

This law emerges from the rotational symmetry of the system.

## Collisions

A collision is any event in which two or more bodies exert forces on each other in a relatively short time.

Total momentum is conserved in a collision where external forces are not applied.

$$
    \boxed{\sum \Delta \mathbf p = \mathbf 0} \implies \sum \mathbf p_i = \sum \mathbf p_f \implies \sum \mathbf J = \mathbf 0
$$

Collisions are of two types:

- **Elastic collision:** If all of the total kinetic energy is conserved, the collision is said to be perfectly elastic. Such a system is an idealization and cannot occur in reality, due to the second law of thermodynamics. Total kinetic energy is conserved in an elastic collision.

    $$
        \boxed{\sum \Delta K = 0} \implies \sum K_i = \sum K_f
    $$

- **Inelastic collision:** If most or all of the total kinetic energy is lost, the collision is said to be inelastic; such collisions involve objects coming to a full stop. A **perfectly inelastic collision** is a limiting case of inelastic collision in which the two bodies coalesce after impact.

    $$
        \boxed{\sum \Delta K \neq 0} \implies \sum K_i \neq \sum K_f
    $$

### Collisions in One Dimension

If two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ are moving along a straight line with initial velocities $u_1$ and $u_2$, respectively, then after a **perfectly elastic collision** with each other, their respective final velocities are

$$
    \boxed{v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - m_2) u_1 + 2 m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{v_2 = \frac{2 m_1 u_1 + (m_2 - m_1) u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Consider two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ moving along a straight line with initial velocities $u_1$ and $u_2$, respectively. After a **perfectly elastic collision** with each other, their final velocities are $v_1$ and $v_2$, respectively.

Total momentum of the system before collision:

$$
    p_i = m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2
$$

Total momentum of the system after collision:

$$
    p_f = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2
$$

Therefore, by conservation of momentum

$$
\begin{aligned}
    p_i = p_f & \implies m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 \\[0.4em]
                & \implies m_1 (u_1 - v_1) = m_2 (v_2 - u_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

For an elastic collision, the total kinetic energy is conserved.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    K_i = K_f & \implies \frac{1}{2} m_1 u_1^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_2 u_2^2 = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_2 v_2^2 \\[0.4em]
                & \implies m_1 u_1^2 + m_2 u_2^2 = m_1 v_1^2 + m_2 v_2^2 \\[0.4em]
                & \implies m_1 (u_1^2 - v_1^2) = m_2 (v_2^2 - u_2^2) \\[0.4em]
                & \implies m_1 (u_1 - v_1)(u_1 + v_1) = m_2 (v_2 - u_2)(v_2 + u_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since, from momentum conservation, we got 

$$
    m_1 (u_1 - v_1) = m_2(v_2 - u_2)
$$

we can cancel the common factors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & u_1 + v_1 = v_2 + u_2 \\[0.4em]
    \implies & u_1 - u_2 = v_2 - v_1
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the **relative velocity relation** for a 1D elastic collision, which essentially states that

$$
    \boxed{\text{relative approach speed} = \text{relative separation speed}}
$$

Now, from the relative velocity relation,

$$
    v_2 = u_1 - u_2 + v_1
$$

Substitute this into momentum conservation equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 (u_1 - u_2 + v_1) \\[0.4em]
    \implies & m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 u_1 - m_2 u_2 + m_2 v_1 \\[0.4em]
    \implies & (m_1 + m_2) v_1 = (m_1 - m_2) u_1 + 2 m_2 u_2 \\[0.4em]
    \implies & \boxed{v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - m_2) u_1 + 2 m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly,

$$
    \boxed{v_2 = \frac{2 m_1 u_1 + (m_2 - m_1) u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

</div>

#### Special Cases

If the second particle is initially at rest, $u_2=0$:

$$
    \boxed{v_1=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1}
$$

$$
    \boxed{v_2=\frac{2m_1}{m_1+m_2}u_1}
$$

### Coefficient of Restitution

The coefficient of restitution $e$ lets us generalize the elastic collision equations to any 1D collision. It is defined as

$$
    \boxed{e = \frac{\text{relative velocity of separation}}{\text{relative velocity of approach}}}
$$

For two particles,

$$
    \boxed{e = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}}
$$

Using coefficient of restitution, the final velocities of two particles after any 1D collision are

$$
    \boxed{v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - e \, m_2) u_1 + (1 + e) m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{v_2 = \frac{(1 + e) m_1 u_1 + (m_2 - e \, m_1) u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

$e$ essentially tells us how much of the relative speed survives the collision.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

By momentum conservation, we have

$$
    m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2
$$

$$
    \implies m_1 (u_1 - v_1) = m_2 (v_2 - u_2)
$$

The coefficient of restitution for our two particles as

$$
    e = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}
$$

$$
    \implies v_2 - v_1 = e (u_1 - u_2)
$$

$$
    \implies v_2 = v_1 + e (u_1 - u_2)
$$

Substituting this into the momentum equation, we get

$$
    m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 [v_1 + e (u_1 - u_2)]
$$

$$
    \implies m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_1 + m_2 e (u_1 - u_2)
$$

$$
    \implies (m_1 + m_2) v_1 = m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 - m_2 e (u_1 - u_2)
$$

$$
    \implies (m_1 + m_2) v_1 = m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 - m_2 e u_1 + m_2 e u_2
$$

Thus,

$$
    \boxed{v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - e \, m_2) u_1 + (1 + e) m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

Similarly,

$$
    \boxed{v_2 = \frac{(1 + e) m_1 u_1 + (m_2 - e \, m_1) u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}
$$

</div>

#### Special cases

**Perfectly elastic collision $(e = 1)$:**

$$
    v_1 = \frac{(m_1 - m_2) u_1 + 2 m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

$$
    v_2 = \frac{2 m_1 u_1 + (m_2 - m_1) u_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

**Perfectly inelastic collision $(e = 0)$:**

$$
    v = v_1 = v_2 = \frac{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
