# Pendulum
For small amplitudes $(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad})$, the motion of a pendulum is approximately similar to the motion of a simple harmonic oscillator.

## Simple Pendulum

$$
F = mg \sin \theta, \quad \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}}}
$$

$$
\boxed{\ddot \theta + \omega^2 \theta = 0} \qquad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

Solution:

$$
\boxed{\theta (t) = \theta_0 \sin (\omega t + \varphi)} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

$$
T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

## Compound Pendulum

$$
    \boxed{\ddot \theta + \omega^2 \theta = 0} \qquad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

Solution:

$$
    \boxed{\theta (t) = \theta_0 \sin (\omega t + \varphi)} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

$$
\begin{gather*}
    \boxed{l_{eq} = \dfrac{I_0}{mr_{cm}}} \\
    \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l_{eq}}}} \\
    T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I_0}{mgr_{cm}}}
\end{gather*}
$$

# Equations of Motion of a Harmonic Wave

$$
\begin{gather*}
    \boxed{f = \dfrac{N}{t}} \\
    \boxed{T = \dfrac{t}{N} = \dfrac{1}{f}} \\
    \boxed{\omega = \dfrac{2 \pi N}{t} = 2 \pi f}
\end{gather*}
$$
