# Waves

## Wave

**Definition:** a wave is a propagating disturbance carrying energy without net transport of matter. Linear waves obey superposition; the waveform $\Psi(\mathbf r,t)$ below specifies the shape.

### Waveform

**Definition:** the waveform is the functional shape of $\Psi$ at fixed $t$ (profile) or fixed $\mathbf r$ (time trace). Harmonic, pulse, and packet forms are canonical cases of the plane-wave superposition below.

### Wave Envelop

**Definition:** the envelope is the smooth curve bounding rapid oscillations, generalizing constant amplitude $A$ to instantaneous amplitude $A(\mathbf r,t)$ for modulated waves and beats.

### Wavefront

Set of all points in a wave field having the same phase:

$$
    \boxed{\phi(\mathbf r,t)=\text{constant}\implies \text{wavefront}}
$$

Plane, spherical, and cylindrical fronts correspond to $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r$, $k \lvert \mathbf r \rvert$, and $k\rho$ phase dependence. Rays are normals to wavefronts in the geometrical limit.

Transverse waves displace perpendicular to $\mathbf k$; longitudinal waves displace parallel to $\mathbf k$.

## Wave Transmission Media

**Definition:** a transmission medium fixes the dispersion $\omega(\mathbf k)$ and attenuation. Nondispersive media have $v_p=\omega/k$ constant; dispersive media have $v_p(k)$ with $v_g\ne v_p$; dissipative media add $\mathrm{Im}\,k$ (spatial decay).

The canonical string has $v=\sqrt{F/\mu}$ (tension over linear density); sound has $v=\sqrt{B/\rho}$ (bulk modulus over mass density).

## Standing Wave

**Result:** superposition of counter-propagating harmonic waves of equal $A,\omega,k$ gives:

$$
    \boxed{y(x,t)=2A\sin kx\cos\omega t}
$$

with nodes at $kx=m\pi$ and antinodes at $kx=(m+\tfrac12)\pi$. Allowed $k$ are quantized by boundary conditions (e.g. $k=m\pi/L$ for fixed ends).

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

$A\sin(kx-\omega t)+A\sin(kx+\omega t)=2A\sin kx\cos\omega t$ by sum identities. No net power flows; energy oscillates between kinetic and potential lobes.

</div>

## Wave Function

**Definition:** the scalar (or component) field describing the disturbance:

$$
    \boxed{\Psi\equiv\Psi(\mathbf r,t)}
$$

Linearity means sums of solutions are solutions; the harmonic plane-wave basis below spans them.

## Phase

Phase is the argument fixing the local state of oscillation. For a plane wave:

$$
    \boxed{\phi(\mathbf r,t)=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t+\phi_0}
$$

with initial phase $\phi_0$. Surfaces of constant $\phi$ propagate along $\mathbf{\hat k}$ with $v_p=\omega/k$.

## Amplitude

**Definition:** peak magnitude of the field:

$$
    \boxed{A=\max|\Psi(\mathbf r,t)|}
$$

For modulated waves $A\to A(\mathbf r,t)$ is the envelope; intensity scales as $|A|^2$.

## Wave Vector

**Definition:** the local spatial rate of phase:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf k=\nabla\phi},\quad \boxed{k=|\mathbf k|=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}}
$$

so $[\mathbf k]=\mathsf L^{-1}$. Direction $\mathbf{\hat k}$ is the propagation normal.

## Frequency

Frequency is the temporal rate of phase. Angular and linear forms:

$$
    \boxed{\omega=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}},\quad \boxed{f=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}}
$$

## Period

Period is the smallest $T$ with $2\pi$ phase advance in time:

$$
    \boxed{\phi(\mathbf r,t+T)-\phi(\mathbf r,t)=2\pi}\implies \boxed{T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=\frac1f}
$$

## Wavelength

Wavelength $\lambda$ is the shortest displacement $\boldsymbol\lambda$ with $2\pi$ phase advance in space:

$$
    \boxed{\phi(\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\lambda,t)-\phi(\mathbf r,t)=2\pi}\implies \boxed{\lambda=\frac{2\pi}{k}=\frac{v_p}{f}}
$$

## Phase Velocity

Phase velocity is the speed of constant-phase surfaces:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf v_p=\frac{\omega}{k}\,\mathbf{\hat k}}
$$

It may exceed $c$ in dispersive media without carrying signals; energy travels at $v_g$.

## Group Velocity

Group velocity is the velocity of the envelope (wave packet):

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf v_g=\nabla_{\mathbf k}\,\omega}
$$

For nondispersive $\omega=v_pk$, $v_g=v_p$; otherwise $v_g=\mathrm d\omega/\mathrm d k$ in 1D.

## Path Length

Physical distance along the ray from source to field point:

$$
    \boxed{s=\int_C |\mathrm d\mathbf r|},\quad \boxed{\mathrm d\phi=\mathbf k\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf r}
$$

In uniform $n$, optical path $L=ns$ links this to Fermat's principle (Optics); here $s$ is the geometric length.

## Wave Equation

$$
    \boxed{\dfrac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial t^2} = v_p^2 \nabla^2 \Psi}
$$

General Solution (superposition of plane waves):

$$
    \boxed{\Psi(\mathbf r,t)=\int A(\mathbf k)e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}\,\mathrm d^3k}
$$

Monochromatic Plane Wave Solution:

$$
    \boxed{\Psi(\mathbf r,t)=Ae^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}}
$$

## Dispersion Relation

**Result:** the medium fixes $\omega(\mathbf k)$; nondispersive propagation is the linear case:

$$
    \boxed{\omega=\omega(\mathbf k)=v_pk}
$$

Dispersive corrections (e.g. $\omega^2=\omega_0^2+c^2k^2$) make $v_p\ne v_g$ and spread packets.

## Principle of Superposition

**Principle:** for a linear wave equation, sums of solutions are solutions:

$$
    \boxed{\Psi=\sum_i\Psi_i\implies \text{each }\Psi_i\text{ evolves independently}}
$$

Standing waves, interference, beats, and Fourier synthesis above are all instances; nonlinearity invalidates the sum.

## Doppler Effect for Mechanical Waves

**Result:** for source speed $v_s$ and observer speed $v_o$ along the line with wave speed $v$ (receding positive for $v_s$, approaching positive for $v_o$):

$$
    \boxed{f'=f\,\frac{v\pm v_o}{v\mp v_s}},\quad \boxed{\lambda'=\frac{v\mp v_s}{f}}
$$

Approach raises pitch; recession lowers it. The electromagnetic (relativistic) form belongs to Relativity, not here.

## Acoustic Wave Function

**Result:** small-amplitude sound is described equivalently by excess pressure $p$ or displacement $\xi$:

$$
    \boxed{p=-B\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\xi},\quad \boxed{\frac{\partial^2p}{\partial t^2}=v^2\nabla^2p},\quad \boxed{v=\sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}}}
$$

with bulk modulus $B$ ($B=\gamma p_0$ for ideal gases). Plane harmonic form $p=p_0e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ with $\omega=vk$ follows.

## Wave Interference

**Result:** two coherent harmonic sources of equal $A$ with phase difference $\delta=2\pi\Delta/\lambda$ give:

$$
    \boxed{I=2I_0(1+\cos\delta)=4I_0\cos^2(\delta/2)}
$$

Constructive for $\Delta=m\lambda$, destructive for $\Delta=(m+\tfrac12)\lambda$. Optical applications (Young, thin-film, grating) belong to Physical Optics; this is the mechanical prototype.

## Energy and Power of a Wave

**Result:** for a harmonic string wave $(y=A\sin(kx-\omega t))$ with linear density $\mu$:

$$
    \boxed{u=\tfrac12\mu\omega^2A^2},\quad \boxed{P=\tfrac12\mu v\omega^2A^2}
$$

with energy density $u$ and transmitted power $P=uv$. For bulk sound of mass density $\rho$:

$$
    \boxed{I=\tfrac12\rho v\omega^2A_{\xi}^2=\frac{p_0^2}{2\rho v}}
$$

with displacement amplitude $A_{\xi}$ and pressure amplitude $p_0=\rho v\omega A_{\xi}$.

## Acoustic Beat

**Result:** superposition of nearby frequencies $f_1,f_2$ gives carrier $\bar f=(f_1+f_2)/2$ modulated at:

$$
    \boxed{f_{\mathrm{beat}}=|f_1-f_2|}
$$

since $\sin\omega_1t+\sin\omega_2t=2\sin\bar\omega t\cos(\Delta\omega t/2)$; intensity beats at $f_{\mathrm{beat}}$ (envelope-squared), amplitude at $f_{\mathrm{beat}}/2$.

## Wave Reflection and Transmission

**Result:** at a junction of impedances $Z_1,Z_2$ $(Z=\rho v$ bulk, $Z=\mu v$ string$)$, amplitude coefficients for incidence from 1 are:

$$
    \boxed{r=\frac{Z_1-Z_2}{Z_1+Z_2}},\quad \boxed{t=\frac{2Z_1}{Z_1+Z_2}}
$$

Fixed end $(Z_2\to\infty)$ gives $r=-1$ (phase flip); free end $(Z_2\to 0)$ gives $r=+1$.

Power coefficients $R=r^2$, $T=4Z_1Z_2/(Z_1+Z_2)^2$ satisfy $R+T=1$.

## Equations of Motion of a Harmonic Wave

Consolidated counting relations for $N$ cycles in time $t$:

$$
    \boxed{f=\frac{N}{t}},\quad \boxed{T=\frac{t}{N}=\frac1f},\quad \boxed{\omega=\frac{2\pi N}{t}=2\pi f}
$$

with $k=2\pi/\lambda$, $v_p=f\lambda=\omega/k$ already boxed above.
