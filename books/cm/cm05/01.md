### Simple Harmonic Oscillator

Equation of motion:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & F_{net} = F_s = -kx \\
    \implies & \boxed{\ddot x + \omega^2 x = 0}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
    \omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}
$$

Solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & x(t) = A \sin (\omega t + \varphi) \qquad (-A \leq x \leq A) \\[0.4em]
    \implies & v = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = A \omega \cos (\omega t + \varphi) \qquad (0 \leq v \leq A \omega) \\[0.4em]
    \implies & a = - \omega^2 x = -A \omega^2 \sin (\omega t + \varphi) \qquad (-A \omega^2 \leq x \leq A \omega^2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Energy of the oscillator:

$$
    K = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 (A^2 - x^2) \qquad (0 \leq K \leq E)
$$

$$
    U = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 \qquad (0 \leq U \leq E)
$$

$$
    E = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2 \qquad (E = \text{constant})
$$

### Damped Harmonic Oscillator

Equation of motion:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & F_{net} = F_s + f = - kx - bv \\
    \implies & \boxed{\ddot x + \gamma \dot x + \omega_0^2 x = 0}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
    \boxed{\gamma = \dfrac{b}{m}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}}
$$

Solutions:

$$
    \boxed{x(t) = Ae^{- \gamma t / 2} \sin (\omega t + \varphi)}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \dfrac{\gamma^2}{4}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\lambda = \dfrac{\gamma}{2}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\tau = \dfrac{1}{\gamma}}
$$

Energy of the oscillator:

$$
    \boxed{E(t) = E_0 e^{- \gamma t / 2} = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2 e^{- \gamma t / 2}} \implies \frac{\mathrm d E}{\mathrm d t} = -\gamma E
$$

$$
    \boxed{E_0 = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2}
$$

Damping conditions:

$$
    \zeta = \dfrac{\gamma}{2 \omega_0} = \dfrac{b}{2 \sqrt{mk}}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & \zeta < 1 \implies \text{Underdamped} \\
    & \zeta = 1 \implies \text{Critically damped} \\
    & \zeta > 1 \implies \text{Overdamped}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Spring-Mass System

$$
    F_s = -kx
$$

$$
    \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}}
$$

Solution:

$$
    x = A \sin (\omega t + \varphi)
$$

$$
    v = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}
$$

$$
    a = - \omega^2 x
$$

$$
    T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}
$$

$$
    K = \dfrac{1}{2} \omega \left( A^2 - x^2 \right)
$$

$$
    \boxed{U =  \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2}
$$

$$
    E = \dfrac{1}{2} k A^2
$$

$$
    \boxed{W = - \dfrac{1}{2} k \left(x^2 - x_0^2 \right)}
$$

For springs in a series combination,

$$
    \boxed{\dfrac{1}{k_s} = \sum \dfrac{1}{k}}
$$

For springs in a parallel combination,

$$
    \boxed{k_p = \sum k}
$$

## Pendulum

For small amplitudes $(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad})$, the motion of a pendulum is approximately similar to the motion of a simple harmonic oscillator.

### Simple Pendulum

$$
    F = mg \sin \theta, \quad \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\ddot \theta + \omega^2 \theta = 0} \qquad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

Solution:

$$
    \boxed{\theta (t) = \theta_0 \sin (\omega t + \varphi)} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

$$
    T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

### Compound Pendulum

$$
    \boxed{\ddot \theta + \omega^2 \theta = 0} \qquad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

Solution:

$$
    \boxed{\theta (t) = \theta_0 \sin (\omega t + \varphi)} \quad \text{$\left(\theta_0 \ll 1 \; \mathrm{rad}\right)$}
$$

$$
    \boxed{l_{eq} = \dfrac{I_0}{mr_{cm}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l_{eq}}}}
$$

$$
    T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I_0}{mgr_{cm}}}
$$
