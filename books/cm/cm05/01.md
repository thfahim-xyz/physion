# Oscillations

## Oscillation

**Definition:** an oscillation is bounded motion of a system about a stable equilibrium state. Periodic oscillation repeats after a fixed period $T$; damped oscillation decays toward equilibrium while driven oscillation is sustained by external work.

Simple harmonic motion is the small-amplitude, undamped, linear limit treated next.

## Simple Harmonic Motion

**Definition:** simple harmonic motion (SHM) is periodic motion with linear restoring acceleration toward equilibrium:

$$
    \boxed{\ddot x+\omega^2 x=0}
$$

with general solution:

$$
    \boxed{x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)}
$$

so $v=\dot x$, $a=\ddot x=-\omega^2 x$. Frequency, period, and $\omega$ relations ($f=\omega/2\pi$, $T=2\pi/\omega$) are defined in Waves and not re-derived here.

## Hooke's Law

**Law:** for displacement $x$ within the elastic limit, the restoring force is:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F=-k\mathbf x}
$$

with stiffness $k \gt 0$ and $[k]=\mathsf M\mathsf T^{-2}$.

**Limitation:** linearity fails beyond the elastic limit (see Elasticity); damping and driving forces are added in the oscillator below.

## Harmonic Oscillator

**Definition:** a harmonic oscillator is a mass acted on by a linear restoring force, optionally with linear damping. Undamped and damped cases follow.

### Simple Harmonic Oscillator

**Result:** with $F_{\mathrm{net}}=-kx$, Newton's second law gives:

$$
    \boxed{\ddot x+\omega^2 x=0},\quad \boxed{\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}}
$$

Solution and kinematics:

$$
    \boxed{x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)},\quad \boxed{v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}},\quad \boxed{a=-\omega^2 x}
$$

with $-A\le x\le A$. Energy partition (derived in Energy of a Harmonic Oscillator below):

$$
    \boxed{K=\tfrac12m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)},\quad \boxed{U=\tfrac12m\omega^2x^2},\quad \boxed{E=\tfrac12m\omega^2A^2=\text{constant}}
$$

### Damped Harmonic Oscillator

**Result:** with $F_{\mathrm{net}}=-kx-bv$:

$$
    \boxed{\ddot x+\gamma\dot x+\omega_0^2 x=0},\quad \boxed{\gamma=\frac{b}{m}},\quad \boxed{\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}}
$$

Underdamped $(\gamma \lt 2\omega_0)$ solution:

$$
    \boxed{x(t)=Ae^{-\gamma t/2}\sin(\omega t+\varphi)},\quad \boxed{\omega=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\frac{\gamma^2}{4}}}
$$

The amplitude decay rate is $\lambda=\gamma/2$ with amplitude time constant $\tau_A=2/\gamma$. Cycle-averaged energy decays twice as fast:

$$
    \boxed{\langle E(t)\rangle=E_0e^{-\gamma t}},\quad \boxed{E_0=\tfrac12m\omega_0^2A^2}\implies \frac{\mathrm d\langle E\rangle}{\mathrm d t}=-\gamma\langle E\rangle
$$

so the energy time constant is $\tau_E=1/\gamma$.

Damping regimes with $\zeta=\gamma/2\omega_0=b/2\sqrt{mk}$:

$$
    \boxed{\zeta \lt 1\implies \text{underdamped}},\quad \boxed{\zeta=1\implies \text{critically damped}},\quad \boxed{\zeta \gt 1\implies \text{overdamped}}
$$

**Correction note:** the previous revision wrote $E(t)=E_0e^{-\gamma t/2}$; the correct cycle-averaged decay is $e^{-\gamma t}$ since energy is quadratic in amplitude.

## Energy of a Harmonic Oscillator

**Result:** with $U=\tfrac12kx^2$ and $K=\tfrac12mv^2$, SHM conserves:

$$
    \boxed{E=K+U=\tfrac12kA^2=\tfrac12m\omega^2A^2}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

$v^2=\omega^2(A^2-x^2)$ with $\omega^2=k/m$, so $K=\tfrac12m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)=\tfrac12k(A^2-x^2)$; adding $U=\tfrac12kx^2$ cancels $x$. Cycle averages give $\langle K\rangle=\langle U\rangle=E/2$.

</div>

## Spring-Mass System

**Result:** the spring-mass system is the canonical SHO with $F_s=-kx$:

$$
    \boxed{\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}},\quad \boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{x=A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)},\quad \boxed{v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}},\quad \boxed{a=-\omega^2x}
$$

$$
    \boxed{K=\tfrac12m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)},\quad \boxed{U=\tfrac12kx^2},\quad \boxed{E=\tfrac12kA^2}
$$

The work by the spring from $x_0$ to $x$ is:

$$
    \boxed{W=-\tfrac12k(x^2-x_0^2)}
$$

Series and parallel stiffnesses add as:

$$
    \boxed{\frac{1}{k_s}=\sum\frac{1}{k}},\quad \boxed{k_p=\sum k}
$$

**Correction note:** the previous revision wrote $K=\tfrac12\omega(A^2-x^2)$, missing $m\omega$; the boxed form above restores dimensions of energy.

## Pendulum

For small amplitudes $(\theta_0\ll 1\ \mathrm{rad})$, pendulum motion linearizes to SHM.

### Simple Pendulum

**Result:** the tangential equation $L\ddot\theta+g\sin\theta=0$ linearizes to:

$$
    \boxed{\ddot\theta+\omega^2\theta=0},\quad \boxed{\omega=\sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}}
$$

with $(\theta_0\ll 1\ \mathrm{rad})$, solution $\theta(t)=\theta_0\sin(\omega t+\varphi)$ and period:

$$
    \boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Arc displacement $s=L\theta$ with restoring $-mg\sin\theta\approx -mg\theta$ gives $mL\ddot\theta+mg\theta=0$.

</div>

### Compound Pendulum

**Result:** for moment $I_0$ about the pivot and center-of-mass distance $r_{\mathrm{cm}}$, torque $-mgr_{\mathrm{cm}}\sin\theta\approx -mgr_{\mathrm{cm}}\theta$ gives:

$$
    \boxed{\ddot\theta+\omega^2\theta=0},\quad \boxed{\omega=\sqrt{\frac{mgr_{\mathrm{cm}}}{I_0}}=\sqrt{\frac{g}{l_{\mathrm{eq}}}}},\quad \boxed{l_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{I_0}{mr_{\mathrm{cm}}}}
$$

with period:

$$
    \boxed{T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_0}{mgr_{\mathrm{cm}}}}}
$$

The simple pendulum is recovered with $I_0=mL^2$, $r_{\mathrm{cm}}=L$.

## Coupled Oscillations and Coupled Oscillator

**Definition:** coupled oscillations are SHO degrees of freedom linked by springs or constraints; the system possesses normal modes in which all coordinates oscillate at one frequency.

Detailed mode analysis and beats belong to Waves (superposition), not here; the canonical two-mass-three-spring chain is the minimal example carried forward.
