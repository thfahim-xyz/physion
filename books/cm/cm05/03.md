## Laws of Gravitation

### Newton's Law of Gravitation

Newton's law of gravitation states that, every point mass attracts every single other point mass by a force acting along the line intersecting both points. The force is proportional to the two masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F = - \frac{GMm}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf r}}
$$

where

- $\mathbf F$ is the is the force between the objects
- $G$ is the Newtonian constant of gravitation
- $M$ is the mass of the first object
- $m$ is the mass of the second object
- $r$ is the distance between the centers of the masses
- $\mathbf r$ is the unit vector from the first object to the second object

### Gauss's Law for Gravity

Gravitational flux through a closed surface is proportional to the mass enclosed by the surface.

$$
    \boxed{\Phi_g = -4 \pi GM}
$$

$$
    \implies \oint_{\partial V} \mathbf g \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf A = -4 \pi GM \qquad \text{(Integral Form)}
$$

$$
    \implies \nabla \cdot \mathbf g = -4 \pi G \rho \qquad \text{(Differential Form)}
$$

### Poisson's Equation for Gravity

$$
    \boxed{\nabla^2 \phi_g = 4 \pi G \rho}
$$

## Gravitation of Spherical Bodies

### Shell Theorem

Shell theorem states that, a spherically symmetric body affects external objects gravitationally as though all of its mass were concentrated at a point at its center. If the body is a spherically symmetric shell (i.e. a hollow sphere), no net gravitational force is exerted by the shell on any object inside, regardless of the object's location within the shell.

### Gravitation of a Sphere

On the surface,

$$
    \boxed{g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}}
$$

At a height $h$ from the surface,

$$
    \boxed{g_h = \dfrac{g}{\left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R}\right)^2}}
$$

At a depth $d$ from the surface,

$$
    \boxed{g_d = \left(1 - \dfrac{d}{R}\right) g}
$$
