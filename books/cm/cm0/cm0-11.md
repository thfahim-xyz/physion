# {{ page.title }}

A collision is any event in which two or more bodies exert forces on each other in a relatively short time.

Total momentum is conserved in a collision where external forces are not applied.
$$
\begin{gather*}
    \boxed{\sum \Delta \mathbf p = \mathbf 0} \implies \sum \mathbf p_i = \sum \mathbf p_f \\
    \boxed{\sum \mathbf J = \mathbf 0}
\end{gather*}
$$

Collisions are of two types:
- **Elastic collision:** If all of the total kinetic energy is conserved, the collision is said to be perfectly elastic.

Such a system is an idealization and cannot occur in reality, due to the second law of thermodynamics.

Total kinetic energy is conserved in an elastic collision.
$$
    \boxed{\sum \Delta K = 0} \implies \sum K_i = \sum K_f
