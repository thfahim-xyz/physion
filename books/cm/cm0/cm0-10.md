## Kinematics in Cartesian Coordinate System

### Position

$$
    \mathbf r = \mathbf r (x,y,z) = x\hat{\mathbf x} + y\hat{\mathbf y} + z\hat{\mathbf z}
$$

### Velocity

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf r} & = \dot x \hat{\mathbf x} + \dot y \hat{\mathbf y} + \dot z \hat{\mathbf z} \\
    & = v_x \hat{\mathbf x} + v_y \hat{\mathbf y} + v_z \hat{\mathbf z}
\end{align*}
$$

### Acceleration

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf a = \ddot{\mathbf r} &= \ddot x \hat{\mathbf x} + \ddot y \hat{\mathbf y} + \ddot z \hat{\mathbf z} \\
    &= a_x \hat{\mathbf x} + a_y \hat{\mathbf y} + a_z \hat{\mathbf z}
\end{align*}
$$

## Kinematics in Polar Coordinate System

### Position

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf r = \mathbf r (r, \theta) = r \hat{\mathbf r}}
$$

### Velocity

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf r} &= \dot r \hat{\mathbf r} + r \dot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} \\
    &= \mathbf v_r + \mathbf v_t
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathbf v_r$ is the radial velocity and $\mathbf v_t$ is the tangential velocity. They are defined as

$$
\begin{gather*}
    \mathbf v_r =  \dot r \hat{\mathbf r} \\
    \mathbf v_t =  r \dot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf r
\end{gather*}
$$

Special Cases:

$$
\begin{gather*}
r = \text{constant} \implies \mathbf v = \mathbf v_t \implies \text{Circular Motion} \\
\theta = \text{constant} \implies \mathbf v = \mathbf v_r \implies \text{1D Motion}
\end{gather*}
$$

### Acceleration

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf a = \ddot{\mathbf r} &= \left(\ddot r - r \dot \theta^2\right) \hat{\mathbf r} + \left(r \ddot \theta + 2 \dot r \dot \theta\right) \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} \\
    &= \ddot{r} \hat{\mathbf r} - r \dot \theta^2 \hat{\mathbf r} + r \ddot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + 2 \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} \\
    &= \mathbf a_r + \mathbf a_c + \mathbf a_t + \mathbf a_{cor}
\end{aligned}    
$$

where $\mathbf a_r$ is radial acceleration, $\mathbf a_c$ is the centripetal acceleration, $\mathbf a_t$ is the tangential acceleration $\mathbf a_{cor}$ is the Coriolis acceleration. They are defined as

$$
\begin{gather*}
    \mathbf a_r = \ddot{r} \hat{\mathbf r} \\
    \mathbf a_c = - r \dot \theta^2 \hat{\mathbf r} = - \omega^2 \mathbf r = \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf v_t \\
    \mathbf a_t = r \ddot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \boldsymbol \alpha \times \mathbf r \\
    \mathbf a_{cor} = 2 \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = 2 \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf v_r \\
\end{gather*}
$$

Special Cases:

$$
\begin{gather*}
    r = \text{constant} \implies \mathbf a = \mathbf a_c + \mathbf a_t \implies \text{Circular Motion with Acceleration} \\
    \theta = \text{constant} \implies \mathbf a = \mathbf a_r \implies \text{1D Motion with Acceleration}
\end{gather*}
$$
