# Principles of Dynamics

## Newton's Laws of Motion

### Newton's First Law

Newton's first law (AKA law of inertia) states that:

**A body remains at rest, or continues to move with constant velocity in a straight line, unless acted upon by a nonzero net external force.**

Mathematically,

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}} = 0 \implies \mathbf a_{\mathrm{net}} = 0}
$$

This law establishes the **natural state of motion**. Contrary to everyday experience, a force is not required to maintain motion. Instead, a force is only required to change the state of motion (i.e., to produce an acceleration). It also introduces **inertia**, which is the tendency of matter to resist changes in its state of motion.

Newton's first law also defines the concept of an **inertial reference frame**. An inertial frame is one in which a body experiencing zero net external force moves with constant velocity. Newton's laws are strictly valid only in inertial frames.

### Newton's Second Law

Newton's second law states that:

**The net force acting on an object is proportional to the net acceleration of that object.**

Mathematically,

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}} = m \mathbf a_{\mathrm{net}}}
$$

where

$$
    \mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}} = \sum \mathbf F
$$

### Newton's Third Law

Newton's third law states that:

**If body A exerts a force on body B, then body B simultaneously exerts a force on body A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.**

Mathematically,

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F_{AB} = - \mathbf F_{BA}}
$$

The two forces are known as an **action–reaction pair**. It reflects the mutual nature of interactions: forces always arise from interactions between two bodies. No isolated force exists independently; every force is one member of an action–reaction pair.

Because the forces act on different objects, **they do not cancel each other**. Each force contributes to the motion of the body on which it acts according to Newton's second law.

## Work–Energy Principle

The total work done by the net force acting on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.

$$
    \boxed{W = \Delta K} \implies K = K_0 + W
$$

## Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but can only change forms. In other words, we cannot gain energy in one form without losing energy in another form.

Total mechanical energy of an isolated system is conserved.

$$
    \boxed{E = K + U = \text{constant}} \qquad \text{(For isolated systems)}
$$

This law emerges from the time-translational symmetry of the system.
