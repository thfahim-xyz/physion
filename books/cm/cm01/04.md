# Motion in Two and Three Dimensions

## Projectile Motion

$$
    T = \frac{2 v_0 \sin \theta}{g}
$$

$$
    H = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}
$$

$$
    R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin (2 \theta)}{g}
$$

$$
    y = x \tan \theta - \frac{g x^2}{2 v_0^2 \cos^2 \theta}
$$

## Circular Motion

$$
    a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r
$$

## Kinematics in Common Coordinate Systems

The position of a particle is described by the position vector $ \mathbf r $. The velocity and acceleration of the particle is defined, in terms of the position vector, as $ \mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf r} $ and $ a = \ddot{\mathbf r} $.

### Cartesian Coordinates

Position:

$$
    \mathbf r (x, y, z) = x \hat{\mathbf x} + y \hat{\mathbf y} + z \hat{\mathbf z}
$$

Velocity:

$$
    \mathbf v = \dot x \hat{\mathbf x} + \dot y \hat{\mathbf y} + \dot z \hat{\mathbf z}
$$

Acceleration:

$$
    \mathbf a = \ddot x \hat{\mathbf x} + \ddot y \hat{\mathbf y} + \ddot z \hat{\mathbf z}
$$

### Cylindrical Coordinates

Position:

$$
    \mathbf r (r, \theta, z) = r \hat{\mathbf r} + z \hat{\mathbf z}
$$

Velocity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf v &= \dot r \hat{\mathbf r} + r \dot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + \dot z \hat{\mathbf z} \\
                &= \mathbf v_r + \mathbf v_t + \mathbf v_z
\end{aligned}
$$

where

- Radial velocity $ \mathbf v_r =  \dot r \hat{\mathbf r} $

- Tangential velocity $ \mathbf v_t =  r \dot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf r $

Acceleration:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \mathbf a &= \left( \ddot r - r \dot{\theta}^2 \right) \hat{\mathbf r} + \left( 2 \dot r \dot \theta + r \ddot \theta \right) \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + \ddot z \hat{\mathbf z} \\
                &= \ddot{r} \hat{\mathbf r} - r \dot \theta^2 \hat{\mathbf r} + r \ddot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + 2 \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + \ddot z \hat{\mathbf z} \\
                &= \mathbf a_r + \mathbf a_c + \mathbf a_t + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{cor}} + \mathbf a_z
\end{aligned}
$$

where

- Radial acceleration $ \mathbf a_r = \ddot{r} \hat{\mathbf r} $

- Centripetal acceleration $ \mathbf a_c = - r \dot \theta^2 \hat{\mathbf r} = - \omega^2 \mathbf r = \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf v_t $

- Tangential acceleration $ \mathbf a_t = r \ddot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = \boldsymbol \alpha \times \mathbf r $

- Coriolis acceleration $ \mathbf a_{cor} = 2 \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} = 2 \boldsymbol \omega \times \mathbf v_r $

### Spherical Coordinates

Position:

$$
    \mathbf r (r, \theta, \varphi) = r \hat{\mathbf r}
$$

Velocity:

$$
    \mathbf v = \dot r \hat{\mathbf r} + r \dot \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} + r \dot \varphi \sin \theta \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi}
$$

Acceleration:

$$
    \mathbf a = \left( \ddot r - r \dot{\theta}^2 - r \dot{\varphi}^2 \sin^2 \theta \right) \hat{\mathbf r} 
                + \left( r \ddot \theta + 2 \dot r \dot \theta - r \dot{\varphi}^2 \sin \theta \cos \theta \right) \hat{\boldsymbol \theta} 
                + \left( r \ddot \varphi \sin \theta + 2 \dot r \dot \varphi \sin \theta + 2r \dot \theta \dot \varphi \cos \theta \right) \hat{\boldsymbol \varphi}
$$
