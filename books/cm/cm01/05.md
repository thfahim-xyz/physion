# Experimental Uncertainty

## Experimental Error

### Absolute Error

Absolute error (Commonly denoted by $ \Delta x, e, \varepsilon, \delta x $) is

$$
    \boxed{\Delta x = |x - \overline x|}
$$

where
- $x$ is the actual value
- $x_i$ is the $i$-th experimental value
- $n$ is the number of experimental values
- $\overline x$ is the mean experimental value, given by

$$
    \overline x = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum x_i
$$

### Relative Error

$$
    \boxed{\varepsilon = \dfrac{\Delta x}{x}}
$$

where
- $\varepsilon$ is the relative error
- $\Delta x$ is the absolute error
- $x$ is the actual value

### Error Propagation

$ \Delta $ denotes error.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    & x + y = z \implies \Delta x + \Delta y = \Delta z \\[0.4em]
    & x - y = z \implies \Delta x + \Delta y = \Delta z \\[0.4em]
    & xy = z \implies \dfrac{\Delta x}{x} + \dfrac{\Delta y}{y} = \dfrac{\Delta z}{z} \\[0.4em]
    & \dfrac{x}{y} = z \implies \dfrac{\Delta x}{x} + \dfrac{\Delta y}{y} = \dfrac{\Delta z}{z} \\[0.4em]
    & x^n = z \implies n \dfrac{\Delta x}{x} = \dfrac{\Delta z}{z} \\[0.4em]
    & A = \dfrac{x^p y^q}{z^r} \implies \dfrac{\Delta A}{A} = p\dfrac{\Delta x}{x} + q\dfrac{\Delta y}{y} + r\dfrac{\Delta z}{z}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Experimental Quantity

An experimental quantity $x$ is written as

$$
    \boxed{x = \overline x + e} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \overline x + \sigma
$$

where
- $\overline x$ is the mean experimental value
- $n$ is the number of experimental values
- $e$ is the mean deviation, given by
    
    $$e = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum \Delta x_i$$

- $\sigma$ is the standard deviation, given by
    
    $$\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{n} \Sigma  \Delta x_i^2}$$

- $\Delta x_i$ is the deviation, given by
    
    $$\Delta x_i = |x_i - \overline x|$$
