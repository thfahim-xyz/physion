# Dynamic Quantities

## Translational Dynamic Quantities

### Mass

Mass is an intrinsic, positive scalar property of a body that quantifies its resistance to acceleration under the action of forces.

**Properties:**

- **Inertia:** Mass measures resistance to acceleration.

- **Intrinsic:** Mass belongs to the object itself.

- **Additivity:** Total mass of a system equals the sum of its parts.

- **Positivity:** Mass is always positive.

- **Gravitational Equivalence:** In Newtonian gravitation, inertial mass is the same as gravitational mass.

### Momentum

Momentum is a physical quantity that describes the motion of an object and its resistance to being stopped.

Linear momentum is the product of an object's mass and its velocity.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf p = m \mathbf v}
$$

### Force

A force is an action that can cause an object to change its velocity or shape, or to resist other forces, or to cause changes of pressure in a fluid.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf F = \dot{\mathbf p}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Examples</div>

- Gravitational Force: Gravitational force is the force exerted by gravitational fields.

    $$F_g = mg$$

- Electric Force: The electric force is the force exerted by electric fields.

    $$F_e = qE$$

- Magnetic Force: The magnetic force is the force exerted by magnetic fields.

    $$F_m = qvB \sin \theta$$

- Normal Force: The normal force is the component of a contact force that is perpendicular to the surface that an object contacts.

    $$F_n$$

- Spring Force: Spring force is the restoring force produced when a spring or spring-like object is stretched or compressed.

    $$F_s = -k x$$

- Linear Drag Force: Linear drag force is the force that resists motion through a fluid and is proportional to velocity.

    $$F_d = -bv$$

- Quadratic Drag Force: Quadratic drag force is the force that resists motion through a fluid and is proportional to the square of speed.

    $$F_D = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 C_D A$$

- Dry Friction: Dry friction is the friction force between two solid surfaces in contact, without lubrication. Given by

    $$
        f =
        \begin{cases}
            F \, , & F \leq \mu_s F_n \\
            \mu_k F_n \, , & F > \mu_s F_n
        \end{cases}
    $$

</div>

#### Conservative Force

A force $\mathbf F$ is conservative if there exists a scalar potential energy function $\phi$ such that

$$
    \mathbf F = - \nabla \phi
$$

The properties of a conservative force are:

- **Mechanical Energy Conservation:** A conservative force conserves mechanical energy.

    $$
        E = K + U = \text{constant}
    $$

- **Path Independence:** The work done by a conservative force is path independent.

    $$
        \int_{C_1} \mathbf F \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r = \int_{C_2} \mathbf F \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r
    $$

- **Zero Work in a Loop:** If the conservative force does work in a closed loop, the total work done is zero.

    $$
        W = \oint \mathbf F \cdot \mathrm d \mathbf r
    $$

- **Irrotational:** The curl of the conservative force field is zero.

    $$
        \nabla \times \mathbf F = \mathbf 0
    $$

#### Centripetal Force

Centripetal force is the net force directed toward the center of a curved path that causes an object to follow that path.

$$
    \mathbf F_c = -m \omega^2 r \hat{\mathbf r}
$$

Centripetal force is the radial inward component of the net force acting on an object, which causes it to follow a curved path. It is not a separate force, but the name given to the net inward force required for curved motion.

#### Central Force

A central force is a force that is directed towards or away from a point.

$$
    \mathbf F \equiv \mathbf F (\mathbf r) = F(r) \hat{\mathbf r}
$$

An object moving under the influence of any central force always conserves angular momentum. Central force is conservative if and only if it is spherically symmetric (or rotationally invariant).

### Impulse

Impulse is the change in momentum of an object.

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm J = \Delta \mathbf p} = \int F \, \mathrm d t
$$

## Rotational Dynamic Quantities

### Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia is a measure of an object’s resistance to changes in its rotational motion about a given axis.

For discrete masses,

$$
    \boxed{I = \sum mr^2}
$$

For continuous mass distributions,

$$
    \boxed{I = \int r^2 \, \mathrm d m}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Examples</div>

- Thin Rod: 

    $$
        I = \frac{1}{12}ML^2 \qquad \text{(About the perpendicular axis through the center)}
    $$

- Thin Plate: 

    $$
        I = \frac{1}{12}M(a^2 + b^2) \qquad \text{(About the perpendicular axis through the center)}
    $$

- Solid Sphere: 

    $$
        I = \frac{2}{3}MR^2 \qquad \text{(About any diameter)}
    $$

- Spherical Shell:

    $$
        I = \frac{2}{5}MR^2 \qquad \text{(About any diameter)}
    $$

- Solid Cylinder: 

    $$
        I = \frac{1}{2}MR^2 \qquad \text{(About the central symmetry axis)}
    $$

- Thin Ring:

    $$
        I = MR^2 \qquad \text{(About the central symmetry axis)}
    $$

</div>

### Angular Momentum

Angular momentum is the quantity that measures rotational motion of an object about a point or axis.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf L = \mathbf r \times \mathbf p}
$$

It is the rotational analogue of linear momentum.

### Torque

Torque is the rotational effect produced by a force applied at a distance from a pivot. It is the rotational analogue of force.

$$
    \boxed{\boldsymbol \tau = \mathbf r \times \mathbf F}
$$

Torque is also the rate of change of angular momentum with respect to time.

$$
    \boldsymbol \tau = \dot{\mathbf L}
$$

### Angular Impulse

$$
    \boxed{\Delta L = \int \tau \, \mathrm d t}
$$
