# Motion in One Dimension

## Horizontal Motion

Assume a particle is in motion on the $x$-axis with constant acceleration $ a $. The particle started moving from the initial position $ x_0 $ at an initial velocity $ v_0 $.

The position of the particle at time $ t $ is given by

$$
    x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2 = x_0 + \frac{1}{2} (v_0 + v)t
$$

The velocity of the particle at time $ t $ is given by

$$
    v = v_0 + at
$$

The velocity at position $ x $ is given by

$$
    v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)
$$

## Vertical Motion

Assume a particle is in motion on the $y$-axis with constant acceleration $a$. The particle was thrown upward at a velocity $ v_0 $ from vertical position (height) $ y_0 $. The constant acceleration is usually assumed to be the acceleration due to gravity (i.e. $ a = -g $)

The vertical position of the particle at time $ t $ is given by

$$
    y = y_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2 = y_0 + \frac{1}{2} (v_0 + v)t
$$

The velocity of the particle at time $ t $ is given by

$$
    v = v_0 + a t
$$

The velocity at vertical position $ y $ is given by

$$
    v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(y - y_0)
$$
