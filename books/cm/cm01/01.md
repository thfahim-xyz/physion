# Translational Kinematic Quantities

## Position

Position is the location of a particle relative to a chosen reference frame.

The position of a particle is specified by its coordinates in a chosen coordinate system. Equivalently, it may be represented by a **position vector** $\mathbf r \equiv \mathbf r(t)$, defined as the vector from the origin of the coordinate system to the particle.

In common coordinate systems, $\mathbf r$ takes the following forms:

$$
\begin{align*}
    \mathbf r & = \mathbf r (x,y,z) = x \hat{\mathbf x} + y \hat{\mathbf y} + z \hat{\mathbf z} \qquad & \text{(Cartesian coordinates)}\\
     & = \mathbf r (r, \theta, z) = r \hat{\mathbf r}(\theta) + z \hat{\mathbf z} \qquad & \text{(Cylindrical coordinates)} \\
     & = \mathbf r (r, \theta, \varphi) = r \hat{\mathbf r}(\theta, \varphi) \qquad & \text{(Spherical coordinates)}
\end{align*}
$$

The coordinates used to specify a particle's position can be linear or angular.

**Linear Coordinates:** Linear coordinates are coordinates that describe location by measuring distances along lines or from a reference point. In Cartesian, cylindrical and spherical coordinates, $x$, $y$, $z$ and $r$ are linear coordinates.

**Angular Coordinates:** Angular coordinates are coordinates that describe location by measuring direction through angles relative to chosen reference lines, planes, or axes. In cylindrical and spherical coordinates, $\theta$ and $\phi$ are angular coordinates.

## Displacement

Displacement is the change in position of an object.

$$
    \boxed{\Delta \mathbf r = \int \mathrm d \mathbf r = \mathbf r(t) - \mathbf r(t_0)}
$$

where $\mathrm d \mathbf r$ is the infinitesimal displacement.

### Distance

Distance is the total amount of path traveled by an object in space.

Distance is described by arc length $s$, given by

$$
    \boxed{s = \int \mathrm d s}
$$

where $\mathrm d s$ is the infinitesimal distance (arc length), which is defined as

$$
    \mathrm d s = |\mathrm d \mathbf r|
$$

## Velocity

Velocity is the rate of change of position with respect to time.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf r}}
$$

The magnitude of velocity is called **speed** $(v = \Vert \mathbf v \Vert)$.

## Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf a = \dot{\mathbf v} = \ddot{\mathbf r}}
$$
