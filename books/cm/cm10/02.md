# Fluid Kinematics

## Flow

Flow is bulk motion of a continuum: at each $(\mathbf r,t)$ a velocity field $\mathbf v(\mathbf r,t)$ assigns the local fluid velocity. Steady flow has $\partial\mathbf v/\partial t=0$; uniform flow has $\nabla\mathbf v=0$. Streamlines are tangent to $\mathbf v$ at an instant; pathlines and streaklines coincide with them only in steady flow.

## Fluid

**Definition:** a fluid is a continuum that flows under any applied shear stress, however small. Liquids resist volume change (nearly incompressible) while gases expand to fill their container (compressible).

Kinematics describes $\mathbf v(\mathbf r,t)$ and its integrals without invoking forces; stresses and equations of motion belong to Fluid Dynamics.

### Perfect Fluid

**Definition:** a perfect (ideal) fluid is inviscid and non-conducting:

$$
    \boxed{\mu=0,\quad k=0\implies \text{perfect fluid}}
$$

so its stress is purely $-\,p\mathbf I$. Its motion obeys the Euler equations; that dynamics belongs to Fluid Dynamics and is only named here.

## Flux

**Definition:** the flux of a flux-density field $\mathbf j$ through oriented surface $S$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\Phi=\int_S \mathbf j\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S}
$$

For uniform $\mathbf j$ through plane area $A$ with normal angle $\theta$, $\Phi=jA\cos\theta$.

## Flux Density

**Definition:** flux density $\mathbf j$ is the vector rate per unit area whose surface integral is flux. For mass transport with density $\rho$ and velocity $\mathbf v$:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf j_m=\rho\mathbf v}
$$

so $[\mathbf j_m]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-2}\mathsf T^{-1}$ with SI unit $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$.

## Fluid Velocity

**Definition:** the Eulerian velocity field $\mathbf v(\mathbf r,t)$ is the velocity of the fluid parcel instantaneously at $(\mathbf r,t)$:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf v=\left.\frac{\mathrm d\mathbf r}{\mathrm d t}\right|_{\mathrm{parcel}}}
$$

Lagrangian trajectories integrate $\dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf v(\mathbf r,t)$; the material derivative $\mathrm D/\mathrm D t=\partial/\partial t+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla$ follows parcels and is used in Fluid Dynamics.

## Velocity Potential

**Definition:** an irrotational flow $(\nabla\times\mathbf v=\mathbf 0)$ admits a scalar velocity potential $\phi$ with:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf v=\nabla\phi}
$$

For incompressible flow $(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0)$, $\phi$ satisfies Laplace's equation $\nabla^2\phi=0$.

**Interpretation:** potential flow reduces the vector field to one scalar; lift and added-mass paradoxes reveal its inviscid limits, resolved only in Fluid Dynamics.

## Vorticity

**Definition:** vorticity is the local rotation rate of the fluid:

$$
    \boxed{\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf v}
$$

so $[\boldsymbol\omega]=\mathsf T^{-1}$. Irrotational flow has $\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf 0$; rotational flow carries shear or wakes.

## Circulation

**Definition:** circulation about closed curve $C$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\Gamma=\oint_C \mathbf v\cdot\mathrm d\boldsymbol\ell=\int_S \boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S}
$$

by Stokes' theorem, with $S$ any surface spanning $C$. Nonzero $\Gamma$ signals enclosed vorticity; the Kutta-Joukowski lift relation belongs to Fluid Dynamics.

## Volume Flow Rate

**Definition:** the volume flux of velocity through $S$ is:

$$
    \boxed{Q_V=\int_S \mathbf v\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S}
$$

For uniform speed $v$ normal to area $A$:

$$
    \boxed{Q_V=Av}
$$

with $[Q_V]=\mathsf L^3\mathsf T^{-1}$ and SI unit $\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-1}}$.

## Mass Flow Rate

**Definition:** the mass flux through $S$ is:

$$
    \boxed{q_m=\int_S \rho\mathbf v\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S}
$$

For uniform $\rho,v$ normal to $A$:

$$
    \boxed{q_m=\rho Av=\rho Q_V}
$$

with SI unit $\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$. Conservation of $q_m$ gives the continuity equation in Fluid Dynamics.

## Mass Flux Density

**Definition:** the mass flux density (mass current per unit area) is:

$$
    \boxed{\mathbf j_m=\rho\mathbf v}
$$

already introduced as the flux density above; its surface integral is $q_m$. For charge or energy transport the same structure holds with the corresponding density.

**Remark:** kinematics stops at bookkeeping of $\mathbf v$, $\phi$, $\boldsymbol\omega$, $\Gamma$, $Q_V$, $q_m$; forces, viscosity, and Bernoulli consequences belong to Fluid Dynamics.
