# Principles of Quantum Mechanics

## Schrödinger Equation

This is the equation of motion in the Schrödinger picture.

### Time–dependent Schrödinger Equation

$$
    i \hbar \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \ket{\Psi(t)} = \hat{H}(t) \ket{\Psi(t)}
$$

where $\hat{H}(t)$ is the time–dependent Hamiltonian operator

$$
    \hat{H}(t) = i \hbar \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t}
$$

### Time–independent Schrödinger Equation

$$
    \hat{H} \ket{\psi} = E \ket{\psi}
$$

where $\hat{H}$ is the time–independent Hamiltonian operator and $E$ is the energy of the system.

The solutions to this equation are stationary states.

### Schrödinger Equation for a Single Uncharged Spinless Non-relativistic Particle:

$$
    i \hbar \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t} \Psi(\mathbf r, t) =
    \left[ - \dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + V(\mathbf r, t) \right] \Psi(\mathbf r, t)
$$

## Heisenberg Equation of Motion

This is the equation of motion in the Heisenberg picture.

$$
    \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \hat{A}(t) = 
    \dfrac{i}{\hbar} \left[ \hat{H}(t), \hat{A}(t) \right] + \left( \dfrac{\partial \hat{A}}{\partial t} \right)
$$

If $\hat{A}(t)$ is time-independent (i.e. does not explicitly depend on time), then

$$
    \dfrac{\partial \hat{A}}{\partial t} = 0 \implies
    \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \hat{A}(t) = \dfrac{i}{\hbar} \left[ \hat{H}(t), \hat{A}(t) \right]
$$

## Ehrenfest Theorem

A particle in 3D space moving in a scaler potential has the following equations of motion:

$$
    m \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \braket{\mathbf r} = \braket{\mathbf p}, \quad
    \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d t} \braket{\mathbf p} = - \braket{\nabla V(\mathbf r)}
$$

## Canonical Commutation Relation

Canonical commutation relation is the commutation relation between conjugate observables.

For conjugate observables $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$, the relation is

$$
    \left[ \hat{A}, \hat{B} \right] = i \hbar \hat{I}
$$

where $\hat{I}$ is the identity operator.

For generalized coordinates $\hat{q}_i$ and conjugate momenta $\hat{p}_i$, the relation is

$$
    \left[ \hat{q}_m, \hat{p}_n \right] = i \hbar \delta_{mn}
$$

## Uncertainty Principle

### Robertson–Schrödinger Uncertainty Relation

For observables $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$,

$$
    \sigma_A \sigma_B \geq 
    \left| \dfrac{1}{2} \braket{[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]} \right|^2 + \left| \dfrac{1}{2} \braket{\{\hat{A}, \hat{B}\}} - \braket{\hat{A}} \braket{\hat{B}} \right|^2
$$

### Robertson Uncertainty Relation

This is a special case of Robertson–Schrödinger uncertainty relation when there is no statistical correlation.

$$
    \sigma_A \sigma_B \geq 
    \left| \dfrac{1}{2} \braket{[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]} \right|^2
$$

### Heisenberg Uncertainty Relation

For position and momentum,

$$
    \sigma_x \sigma_y \geq \dfrac{\hbar}{2}
$$

For angular momentum (spin, orbital or total),

$$
    \sigma_{L_x} \sigma_{L_y} \geq \dfrac{\hbar}{2} \left| \braket{\hat{L}_z} \right|, \quad
    \sigma_{L_y} \sigma_{L_z} \geq \dfrac{\hbar}{2} \left| \braket{\hat{L}_x} \right|, \quad
    \sigma_{L_z} \sigma_{L_x} \geq \dfrac{\hbar}{2} \left| \braket{\hat{L}_y} \right|
$$
