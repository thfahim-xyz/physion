# Elasticity

## Elastic Behavior

**Definition:** elasticity is the property of a solid by which deformation caused by applied stress disappears completely upon removal of the stress. The body returns to its natural (stress-free) state with no residual strain and no dissipated energy in the ideal limit.

Beyond a material-dependent yield stress $\sigma_Y$, deformation becomes partly irreversible; that regime belongs to Plasticity, not here.

## Linear Elasticity

**Result:** for small strain, stress is linear in strain. Uniaxially:

$$
    \boxed{\sigma=E\varepsilon}
$$

with $E$ Young's modulus. Generally:

$$
    \boxed{\sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\,\varepsilon_{kl}}
$$

where $C_{ijkl}$ is the fourth-rank stiffness tensor and summation over $k,l$ is implied. Stress and strain tensors, moduli, and Poisson's ratio are defined in Stress and Strain and are not redefined here.

For an isotropic solid the 81 components reduce to two independent constants, conventionally $E$ and $\nu$ (or equivalently any pair among $E,G,B,\nu$).

**Interpretation:** linearity holds for infinitesimal strain about the natural state; $C_{ijkl}$ encodes crystal symmetry, collapsing to isotropy for polycrystals and amorphous solids at macroscopic scales.

## Elastic Moduli Relations

**Result:** for an isotropic linear solid, Young's modulus $E$, shear modulus $G$, bulk modulus $B$, and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ satisfy:

$$
    \boxed{E=2G(1+\nu)=3B(1-2\nu)}
$$

$$
    \boxed{G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}},\quad \boxed{B=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}},\quad \boxed{\nu=\frac{3B-2G}{2(3B+G)}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Decompose a uniaxial stress into hydrostatic plus deviatoric parts; the volumetric response fixes $B$ and the shape response fixes $G$, while transverse contraction fixes $\nu$. Elimination among the three canonical loadings (uniaxial, shear, hydrostatic) yields the relations.

</div>

Stability of strain energy requires $G \gt 0$, $B \gt 0$, so $-1 \lt \nu \lt 1/2$; most solids have $0\le\nu\lesssim 1/2$, with $\nu\to 1/2$ the incompressible limit.

## Elastic Strain Energy

**Result:** the reversible work per unit volume stored during isothermal elastic deformation is:

$$
    \boxed{u=\frac12\sigma_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}=\frac12C_{ijkl}\,\varepsilon_{ij}\varepsilon_{kl}}
$$

Uniaxially:

$$
    \boxed{u=\frac12\sigma\varepsilon=\frac{\sigma^2}{2E}=\frac12E\varepsilon^2}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

With $\mathrm d u=\sigma_{ij}\,\mathrm d\varepsilon_{ij}$ for reversible loading from zero strain, linearity $\sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl}$ gives the factor $1/2$ upon integration.

</div>

The canonical bar of length $L$ and area $A$ extended by $x$ stores $U=\tfrac12 kx^2$ with $k=EA/L$, recovering Hooke's law $\mathbf F=-k\mathbf x$ for the lumped spring.

## Elastic Limit and Yield

**Definition:** the elastic limit is the largest stress (or strain) for which unloading returns the body to zero residual strain. The yield stress $\sigma_Y$ marks the onset of plasticity:

$$
    \boxed{|\sigma| \lt \sigma_Y\implies \text{elastic}},\quad \boxed{|\sigma| \gt \sigma_Y\implies \text{plastic flow or fracture}}
$$

**Interpretation:** below yield the loading curve retraces itself; beyond yield hysteresis, permanent set, or fracture appears, governed by Plasticity.

**Limitation:** $\sigma_Y$ depends on temperature, rate, and history; linear elasticity supplies no failure criterion beyond its domain.
