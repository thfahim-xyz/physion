# Plasticity

## Plastic Behavior

**Definition:** plasticity is the property by which deformation beyond yield persists after removal of stress. The total strain decomposes additively (small-strain form):

$$
    \boxed{\varepsilon=\varepsilon_e+\varepsilon_p}
$$

where $\varepsilon_e$ is the reversible elastic part and $\varepsilon_p$ the residual plastic part. Unlike elastic strain energy, plastic work is largely dissipated as heat.

This is the irreversible complement of Elasticity: $\lvert \sigma \rvert \lt \sigma_Y$ retraces, $\lvert \sigma \rvert \gt \sigma_Y$ leaves permanent set.

## Stress-Strain Curve

**Description:** the canonical uniaxial tension curve proceeds: linear elastic slope $E$, yield at $\sigma_Y$ (often defined by $0.2\%$ offset strain when no sharp point exists), strain hardening with diminishing slope, ultimate tensile strength, necking, and fracture.

Unloading from the plastic regime follows slope $E$ back to $\sigma=0$, leaving:

$$
    \boxed{\varepsilon_p=\varepsilon-\sigma/E}
$$

**Interpretation:** the offset construction makes $\sigma_Y$ operational; hardening raises subsequent yield while ductility measures plastic strain to fracture. Full tensor curve shapes belong to Stress and Strain, not here.

## Yield Criteria

**Result:** for principal stresses $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$, the two canonical isotropic criteria are Tresca (maximum shear) and von Mises (distortional energy):

$$
    \boxed{\max \lvert \sigma_i-\sigma_j \rvert=\sigma_Y\implies \text{Tresca yield}}
$$

$$
    \boxed{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}=\sigma_Y\implies \text{von Mises yield}}
$$

Both reduce to $\lvert \sigma \rvert =\sigma_Y$ uniaxially and to $\lvert \tau \rvert=\sigma_Y/2$ (Tresca) or $\lvert \tau \rvert =\sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}$ (von Mises) in pure shear.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Hydrostatic stress produces only volume change, so yield depends on deviatoric invariants. Tresca bounds the largest Mohr shear; von Mises equates the deviatoric energy $\tfrac{1}{4G}s_{ij}s_{ij}$ to its uniaxial value $ \sigma_Y^2/6G$.

</div>

**Remark:** Tresca is the conservative inner hexagon; von Mises is the smooth circumscribing ellipse, generally closer to ductile-metal data.

## Strain Hardening

**Definition:** strain hardening is the increase of subsequent yield stress with accumulated plastic strain. Perfect plasticity is the limiting idealization with no hardening:

$$
    \boxed{\sigma_Y=\text{constant}\implies \text{perfect plasticity}},\quad \boxed{\sigma_Y=\sigma_Y(\varepsilon_p)\implies \text{hardening}}
$$

Isotropic hardening expands the yield surface uniformly; kinematic hardening translates it, capturing the Bauschinger effect under reversed loading.

**Interpretation:** hardening reflects dislocation accumulation; the flow stress rises while elastic moduli remain essentially unchanged. Rate- and temperature-dependent flow belongs to viscoplasticity, not here.

## Plastic Work and Flow Rule

**Result:** the plastic work per unit volume is dissipated, not stored, with no factor of $1/2$ unlike elastic energy:

$$
    \boxed{w_p=\int \sigma_{ij}\,\mathrm d\varepsilon_{ij}^p}
$$

Uniaxially:

$$
    \boxed{w_p=\int \sigma\,\mathrm d\varepsilon_p}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

With $\mathrm d w=\sigma_{ij}\,\mathrm d\varepsilon_{ij}$ and $\varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ij}^e+\varepsilon_{ij}^p$, the elastic part integrates to $u=\tfrac12\sigma_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}^e$ while the plastic part accumulates irreversibly as heat and defect energy.

</div>

The associated flow rule states the plastic strain increment is normal to the yield surface $f=0$:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm d\varepsilon_{ij}^p=\mathrm d\lambda\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma_{ij}}}
$$

with plastic multiplier $\mathrm d\lambda\ge 0$.

**Limitation:** the additive split and normality assume small strain and stable hardening; large-deformation and softening regimes require the finite-strain theory.
