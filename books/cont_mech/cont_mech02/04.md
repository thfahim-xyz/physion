# Interfacial Phenomena

## Surface Tension

**Definition:** surface tension $\gamma$ is the tangential force per unit length acting in the interface, equivalently the free-energy cost per unit area:

$$
    \boxed{\gamma=\frac{F}{l}=\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial A}}
$$

with $[\gamma]=\mathsf M\mathsf T^{-2}$ and SI unit $\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}=\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}}$.

**Interpretation:** unbalanced molecular attraction at the interface pulls tangentially, minimizing area at fixed volume. Hence droplets, jets, and films. Pressure jumps across curved interfaces follow Young-Laplace below; bulk $P$, hydrostatics, and viscosity belong to Fluid Statics and Dynamics and are not re-derived here.

## Surface Energy

**Result:** the reversible work to create fresh area $\mathrm d A$ at fixed $T,V,n$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm dW=\gamma\,\mathrm d A},\quad \boxed{\gamma=\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial A}\right)_{T,V,n}}
$$

with Helmholtz free energy $\mathcal F$. Thus tension (force/length) and surface energy (energy/area) are the same quantity.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Stretch a film of width $l$ by $\mathrm d x$ against $2\gamma l$ (two sides); $\mathrm dW=2\gamma l\,\mathrm d x=\gamma\,\mathrm d A$. The canonical soap film minimizes $\mathcal F=\gamma A$ at fixed frame, giving minimal surfaces.

</div>

**Remark:** surfactants lower $\gamma$ by populating the interface; temperature dependence $\mathrm d\gamma/\mathrm d T \lt 0$ drives Marangoni flow, named only here.

## Contact Angle

**Result:** at a solid-liquid-vapor triple line, horizontal force balance gives Young's equation:

$$
    \boxed{\gamma_{SV}=\gamma_{SL}+\gamma_{LV}\cos\theta_c}
$$

where $\theta_c$ is measured inside the liquid.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Displace the contact line by $\mathrm d x$; solid-vapor area gained is $\mathrm d x$ while solid-liquid lost is $\mathrm d x$ and liquid-vapor extended by $\cos\theta_c\,\mathrm d x$. Stationary $\mathcal F$ gives the boxed balance.

</div>

Hence:

$$
    \boxed{\theta_c \lt \pi/2\implies \text{wetting}},\quad \boxed{\theta_c \gt \pi/2\implies \text{non-wetting}}
$$

The canonical droplet beads on hydrophobic ($\theta_c \gt \pi/2$) and spreads on hydrophilic ($\theta_c \lt \pi/2$) solids.

**Limitation:** ideal smooth homogeneous solid; roughness (Wenzel, Cassie-Baxter) and hysteresis pin real lines.

## Jurin's Law

**Result:** capillary rise (or depression) of height $h$ in a tube of radius $r$ is:

$$
    \boxed{h=\frac{2\gamma\cos\theta_c}{\rho g r}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

The meniscus of contact angle $\theta_c$ has radius of curvature $R=r/\cos\theta_c$. Young-Laplace gives capillary suction $\Delta p=2\gamma/R=2\gamma\cos\theta_c/r$ for a spherical meniscus. Hydrostatic balance $\rho g h=\Delta p$ yields Jurin's law.

</div>

Thus $h \gt 0$ (rise) for wetting ($\cos\theta_c \gt 0$) and $h \lt 0$ (depression) for non-wetting; narrower tubes rise higher as $1/r$.

**Limitation:** static equilibrium, uniform circular tube, negligible reservoir shift; dynamics follows Washburn flow, not here.
