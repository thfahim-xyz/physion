# Fluid Dynamics

## Mass Continuity

**Result:** conservation of mass for density $\rho(\mathbf r,t)$ and velocity $\mathbf v(\mathbf r,t)$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0}
$$

For incompressible flow $(\rho=\text{constant})$:

$$
    \boxed{\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0}
$$

and along a streamtube $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Net mass flux $-\oint \rho\mathbf v\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S$ through a fixed volume equals $\mathrm d/\mathrm d t\int\rho\,\mathrm d V$; divergence theorem gives the differential form. Kinematics definitions of $q_m,Q_V$ are assumed and not re-derived.

</div>

## Momentum Conservation in Fluid Dynamics

**Result:** Cauchy's momentum equation for stress tensor $\sigma$ and body force $\rho\mathbf g$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\rho\frac{\mathrm D\mathbf v}{\mathrm D t}=\nabla\cdot\sigma+\rho\mathbf g}
$$

with material derivative $\mathrm D/\mathrm D t=\partial/\partial t+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla$ following parcels (defined in Fluid Kinematics).

**Interpretation:** this is Newton's second law per unit volume; Euler and Navier-Stokes below insert the perfect-fluid and Newtonian viscous stresses respectively.

## Energy Conservation in Fluid Dynamics

**Result:** the first law per unit mass with internal energy $e$, heat flux $\mathbf q$, and viscous dissipation $\Phi_v$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\rho\frac{\mathrm D e}{\mathrm D t}=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf q+\sigma:\nabla\mathbf v}
$$

For adiabatic inviscid flow this reduces to Bernoulli's constant along streamlines; the full Bernoulli derivation is given at the end and only named here.

## Euler Equations for Fluid Dynamics

**Result:** for a perfect fluid $(\sigma=-p\mathbf I)$:

$$
    \boxed{\rho\frac{\mathrm D\mathbf v}{\mathrm D t}=-\nabla p+\rho\mathbf g}
$$

with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0$ if incompressible.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Insert $\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}$ into Cauchy's equation. No viscous term appears; vorticity is advected and Kelvin's circulation theorem holds.

</div>

**Limitation:** d'Alembert's paradox (zero drag in steady potential flow) shows viscosity is essential at walls despite small $\mu$; that resolution belongs to Navier-Stokes.

## Navier-Stokes Equation

**Result:** for an incompressible Newtonian fluid with dynamic viscosity $\mu$:

$$
    \boxed{\rho\frac{\mathrm D\mathbf v}{\mathrm D t}=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf v+\rho\mathbf g}
$$

with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0$.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Insert $\sigma=-p\mathbf I+2\mu\mathbf e+\lambda(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)\mathbf I$ with rate-of-strain $\mathbf e=\tfrac12(\nabla\mathbf v+\nabla\mathbf v^T)$ into Cauchy; incompressibility kills the $\lambda$ term and $\nabla\cdot 2\mu\mathbf e=\mu\nabla^2\mathbf v$.

</div>

## Viscosity

**Definition:** dynamic viscosity $\mu$ measures resistance to shear rate; kinematic viscosity is:

$$
    \boxed{\nu=\frac{\mu}{\rho}}
$$

so $[\mu]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-1}$ (SI $\mathrm{Pa\,s}$) and $[\nu]=\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-1}$ (SI $\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$).

## Newton's Law of Viscosity

**Law:** for a Newtonian fluid in simple shear with velocity $u(y)$:

$$
    \boxed{\tau=\mu\frac{\mathrm d u}{\mathrm d y}}
$$

where $\tau$ is the shear stress. Non-Newtonian fluids violate this linearity and are excluded here.

**Interpretation:** $\mu$ is the slope of stress versus strain rate; the no-slip condition transmits this stress to walls, generating drag.

## Terminal Velocity

**Result:** a body falling under weight $mg$ against drag $F_D(v)$ reaches $v_t$ where:

$$
    \boxed{mg=F_D(v_t)\implies a=0}
$$

The canonical Stokes sphere of radius $r$ and density $\rho_s$ in fluid $\rho_f$ gives:

$$
    \boxed{v_t=\frac{2r^2g(\rho_s-\rho_f)}{9\mu}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

$6\pi\mu r v_t=\tfrac43\pi r^3(\rho_s-\rho_f)g$ from Stokes' law below; rearrangement gives $v_t$.

</div>

## Reynolds Number

**Result:** the dimensionless ratio of inertial to viscous forces for scale $L$ and speed $V$ is:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm{Re}=\frac{\rho V L}{\mu}=\frac{V L}{\nu}}
$$

Laminar flow holds at $\mathrm{Re}\lesssim 10^3$ (pipe transition $\sim 2\times 10^3$); turbulence develops at large $\mathrm{Re}$.

**Interpretation:** $\mathrm{Re}$ alone governs dynamical similarity for incompressible Newtonian flow; drag coefficients below collapse onto $\mathrm{Re}$.

## Drag Equation

**Result:** at high $\mathrm{Re}$ the drag on frontal area $A$ is:

$$
    \boxed{F_D=\frac12\rho v^2 C_D A}
$$

with dimensionless drag coefficient $C_D(\mathrm{Re},\text{shape})$. At low $\mathrm{Re}$, $C_D\propto 1/\mathrm{Re}$ recovers linear Stokes drag.

## Stokes' Law

**Result:** for a sphere of radius $r$ at $\mathrm{Re}\ll 1$:

$$
    \boxed{F_D=6\pi\mu r v},\quad \boxed{C_D=\frac{24}{\mathrm{Re}}}
$$

with $\mathrm{Re}=\rho v(2r)/\mu$.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Solving Stokes flow $(\nabla p=\mu\nabla^2\mathbf v)$ past a no-slip sphere and integrating stress gives $6\pi\mu r v$; substitution into the drag equation yields $C_D=24/\mathrm{Re}$.

</div>

## Bernoulli's Equation

**Result:** along a streamline in steady, incompressible, inviscid flow:

$$
    \boxed{p+\frac12\rho v^2+\rho g z=\text{constant}}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box_title">Derivation</div>

Dot Euler with $\mathrm d\boldsymbol\ell$ along a streamline in steady flow: $\rho\mathbf v\cdot\nabla(\tfrac12v^2)+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p+\rho g\,\mathbf v\cdot\nabla z=0$, which integrates to the boxed constant. The $\tfrac12\rho v^2$ term is the dynamic pressure named in Fluid Statics.

</div>

## Bernoulli's Principle

**Principle:** where speed increases along a streamline (at fixed elevation), static pressure drops:

$$
    \boxed{v\uparrow\implies p\downarrow}
$$

The canonical Venturi meter infers $v$ from $p$ via continuity $A_1v_1=A_2v_2$ plus the equation above.

**Limitation:** viscosity, unsteadiness, compressibility, and rotational wakes invalidate the constant; the full Navier-Stokes or energy equation must then be used.
