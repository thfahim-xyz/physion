# Fluid Statics

## Pressure

**Definition:** pressure is the isotropic normal force per unit area exerted by a fluid at rest on its boundary or on an immersed surface:

$$
    \boxed{P=\frac{F_n}{A}}
$$

In tensor form, the static fluid stress is $\sigma_{ij}=-P\delta_{ij}$, so $P=-\tfrac13\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma)$ with no shear components. Pressure has $[P]=\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-2}$ with SI unit pascal, $1\ \mathrm{Pa}=1\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$.

**Interpretation:** at rest a fluid sustains no static shear; the single scalar $P$ fixes the entire stress state. Viscosity and flow stresses belong to Fluid Dynamics, not here.

### Static and Dynamic Pressure

**Definition:** the static pressure $p$ is the thermodynamic pressure in the fluid; the dynamic pressure associated with bulk motion of density $\rho$ at speed $v$ is:

$$
    \boxed{q=\frac12\rho v^2}
$$

In statics $v=0$, so only $p$ acts. The combination $p+q$ (plus hydrostatic terms) is conserved along streamlines in steady inviscid flow; that theorem belongs to Bernoulli's Equation in Fluid Dynamics and is only named here.

## Hydrostatic Equilibrium

**Result:** in a fluid at rest under gravity, pressure varies with depth as:

$$
    \boxed{\frac{\mathrm d p}{\mathrm d z}=-\rho g}
$$

with $z$ upward. For uniform $\rho$ between depths $z_1,z_2$:

$$
    \boxed{\Delta p=\rho g h},\quad \boxed{p=p_0+\rho g h}
$$

where $h=z_1-z_2$ is the depth below the free surface at $p_0$.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

Vertical force balance on a slab of area $A$ and thickness $\mathrm d z$ gives $p(z)A-p(z+\mathrm d z)A-\rho gA\,\mathrm d z=0$, whence $\mathrm d p=-\rho g\,\mathrm d z$. Integration at constant $\rho$ gives the boxed forms.

</div>

The canonical barometer reads $p_0=\rho g h$ for a mercury column of height $h$ supported by atmosphere.

## Pascal's Law

**Law:** a pressure change applied to an enclosed incompressible fluid at rest is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the container walls:

$$
    \boxed{\Delta p_1=\Delta p_2}
$$

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

In hydrostatic equilibrium with negligible gravitational head over the device, $p$ is uniform; an added piston pressure raises the entire field by the same $\Delta p$.

</div>

The canonical hydraulic lift with pistons $A_1,A_2$ gives $F_1/A_1=F_2/A_2$, so:

$$
    \boxed{F_2=F_1\frac{A_2}{A_1}}
$$

trading displacement for force at constant work.

## Archimedes' Principle

**Principle:** a body immersed in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid:

$$
    \boxed{F_B=\rho_f V_{\mathrm{disp}}g}
$$

where $\rho_f$ is the fluid density and $V_{\mathrm{disp}}$ the displaced volume.

<div class="box" markdown="1">
<div class="box__title">Derivation</div>

The net pressure force is $-\oint p\,\mathbf{\hat n}\,\mathrm d S=-\int \nabla p\,\mathrm d V$ by divergence theorem. With $\nabla p=-\rho_f g\,\mathbf{\hat z}$, this equals $\rho_f V_{\mathrm{disp}}g$ upward.

</div>

Hence floatation and sinking follow:

$$
    \boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{body}} \lt \rho_f\implies \text{float}},\quad \boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{body}} \gt \rho_f\implies \text{sink}}
$$

with partial submergence $V_{\mathrm{disp}}/V=\rho_{\mathrm{body}}/\rho_f$ at a free surface.

## Relative Density

**Definition:** relative density (specific gravity) is the dimensionless ratio to water at $4^{\circ}\mathrm C$:

$$
    \boxed{\mathrm{RD}=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{water}}}}
$$

The canonical hydrometer floats higher in denser fluid with submergence inversely proportional to $\rho_f$ by Archimedes' principle.

**Remark:** $\mathrm{RD}$ replaces absolute weighing by a single float reading; temperature corrections enter through $\rho_{\mathrm{water}}(T)$.
